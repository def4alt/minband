## Underwater and maritime: acoustic modems, UUV/USV swarms, Ukrainian USVs and Starlink

### Takeaway
Underwater links are genuinely tens-of-bps (NATO JANUS at 80 bps, propagation at 1,500 m/s), so the whole acoustic ecosystem already lives on tiny state messages rather than imagery; surface drones in the Black Sea run on Starlink and mesh, where the failure mode is not low bandwidth but total, geographic blackout (geofence or jammer), and the state they report to shore is own-position plus bearings/positions of contacts.

### Cited Findings
- JANUS was recognised as a NATO standard in April 2017 under STANAG 4748; its baseline modulation is frequency-hopped binary FSK using 13 pairs of orthogonal tones; the data rate is "low (80 bps) compared to internet standards, but ... adequate for most marine applications" (vendor characterisation) — [Popoto Modem news, Sept 2019](https://www.popotomodem.com/blog/09-09-2019/); [BusinessWire, Sept 2019](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20190904005062/en/)
- JANUS "Initial JANUS band" is 9–14 kHz with a centre frequency of 11,520 Hz; typical JANUS systems operate around 10 kHz — [SEANet paper, arXiv 2403.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01009)
- The JANUS specification is NATO ANEP-87; in the baseline bit allocation, bits 31–56 (26 bits) are the application data block, whose meaning depends on the ClassUserID and ApplicationType fields earlier in the message — [Popoto JANUS messages docs](https://docs.popotomodem.com/user-manual/pmm4511/api-reference/janus-messages)
- A commercial software-defined modem lists payload modes of FH-FSK at 80 bps (JANUS-compatible), BPSK 640–2,560 bps and QPSK 1,280–10,240 bps — [Popoto PMM6081 spec](https://www.popotomodem.com/static/51873df27af6aa927b74a7802c6970a6/PMM6081-Technical-Specifications.pdf)
- A non-standard "fast JANUS" experiment moved to a 115 kHz centre frequency with 23 ksps symbol rate for up to 34.5 kbps — [Lehigh/NSF PAR 10430268](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10430268)
- Sea trials (Padua): JANUS "ensures superior Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR), while BPSK achieves greater throughput over shorter distances" — [Univ. of Padua repository](https://research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3542241)
- EvoLogics S2C modems: "working ranges up to 8,000 m ... data rates up to 33 kbps (20 kbps option)" (vendor claim); a survey lists the S2C R 7/17 at up to 8 km with max 6.9 kb/s and the S2C R 18/34 at 13.9 kb/s at up to 3.5 km — [Naval Technology vendor page](https://www.naval-technology.com/contractors/maritime-c4/evologics/); [SIGNET Padua survey](https://signet.dei.unipd.it/underwater/media/download/436)
- Teledyne Benthos modems "traditionally ran a 5 kHz bandwidth at bit rates up to 2400 bits per second"; the BlueStreamX2 firmware widens WideBand C to 10 kHz, "doubling the data rate to 4800 bits per second" at the same range — [Ocean News](https://oceannews.com/news/science-technology/teledyne-marine-unveils-innovative-bluestreamx2-enhancement-for-benthos-acoustic-modems/)
- Underwater propagation uses the "low speed of sound (≈1500 m/s)"; multipath spread "can cover a few tens, or even hundreds of symbol intervals", forcing guard times — [Tetcos NetSim UWAN docs](https://www.tetcos.com/documentation/v15.0/UWAN/Model_Features.html); [MIT (Stojanovic) OPNET paper, 1999](https://www.mit.edu/people/millitsa/resources/pdfs/opnet99.pdf)
- Modem power: about 3 W receiving, 10–50 W transmitting (2006 figure) — [IEEE OES newsletter, 2006](https://stuff.mit.edu/people/millitsa/resources/pdfs/newsletter-oes.pdf)
- Early Magura prototypes "depended heavily on Starlink for command and control", a vulnerability because the service is restricted in Russian-occupied zones; the original design used a single communication channel via Starlink — [Kyiv Post analysis (2024)](https://www.kyivpost.com/analysis/29068)
- Current Magura V5 communicates via two Starlink antennas and a Kymeta satellite antenna, plus a cellular router for terrestrial mobile internet near shore — [Wikipedia: MAGURA V5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAGURA_V5)
- Sea Baby specifications list "mesh radio with an aerial repeater or satellite communication" — [Wikipedia: Sea Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Baby)
- The Magura MV11 carrier "depends on satellite and mesh communications in a theater saturated with electronic warfare, and its answer is redundancy rather than immunity" — [drone-warfare.com Magura research](https://drone-warfare.com/research/magura-v5-v7/)
- September 2022 Sevastopol attack: six explosive drone subs guided by Starlink; per Isaacson, Musk told engineers to turn off coverage within 100 km of the Crimean coast and "the drones lost connectivity and washed ashore harmlessly"; Musk says the regions "were not activated" and nothing was deactivated; eyewitnesses said the connection was abruptly halted 70 km from the target — [Euromaidan Press, Jan 2024](https://euromaidanpress.com/2024/01/03/up-elon-musk-indeed-thwarted-ukrainian-attack-on-crimea-by-blocking-starlink); [Ukrainska Pravda, Sept 2023](https://pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2023/09/8/7418936/); [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/musk-says-he-stopped-ukraine-attack-on-russian-navy-by-withholding-starlink-internet/)
- Russia copied the USV model on smuggled Starlink; Ukraine's Navy destroyed three Russian sea drones with Starlink terminals on 23 June 2026, after SpaceX cut unauthorised Russian access in February 2026 — [Euromaidan Press, June 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/06/24/russias-sea-drones-run-on-smuggled-starlink-ukraine-sank-three-before-they-reached-shore/)
- Saildrone (USCG evaluation, Nov 2020): camera array captures imagery every 5 s with onboard vessel-detection AI; on detection the drone sends "the image, line of bearing to the detected vessel, and its own location"; the AI "is set to send only the clearest images approximately every 15-20 minutes so the satellite link isn't overloaded and the command center isn't flooded with alerts"; Saildrone leases Iridium for USV–ground data — [USCG LCMDA QuickLook, Nov 2020](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/LCMDA_QuickLook_NOV2020.pdf)
- US Navy (NAVCENT) long-endurance surveillance USV carries an AIS transceiver, four cameras and a radar reflector — [Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/work-boat-world/maritime-security-world/unmanned-systems/vessel-review-long-endurance-usv-to-enhance-us-navys-surveillance-capabilities/)
- A research USV (NTNU AutoNaut) uses Iridium SBD to report "onboard system state and position" and receive simple operator commands — [NTNU AutoNaut comms page](https://autonaut2.itk.ntnu.no/doku.php?id=l1_comm)

### Inferences
- At 80 bps a 64-bit-class JANUS packet needs ~0.8 s of payload airtime before preamble and coding; with 1,500 m/s propagation, a 5 km link adds ~3.3 s one-way. Any ack-based protocol is multi-second per exchange, which is exactly the regime of "send a delta only when the receiver's belief is wrong" rather than streaming.
- The Saildrone design is already a crude twin: periodic bearing+position reports with imagery deliberately rate-limited to one chip per 15–20 min. That is the clearest real-world precedent for "image chips on demand" as a stretch feature.
- For Black Sea USVs the dominant failure is a total blackout (geofence, jammer, destroyed antenna), not low throughput. The useful contribution of a twin there is graceful state hand-back after a multi-second-to-multi-minute gap and tolerating a drop to a mesh/cellular fallback with far less capacity than Starlink.

### Gaps
- No source gave JANUS end-to-end packet duration or the full 64-bit layout (ANEP-87 and JanusWiki not reachable).
- No public figures found for what a Magura/Sea Baby sends per second to shore, nor for the capacity of the Sea Baby aerial-repeater mesh.
- Ocean Aero and US Navy USV-swarm comms were not covered by any result; Task Force 59 sensor-reporting rates not found.

## HF / BLOS and SATCOM-denied: NATO HF rates, Iridium, Starlink jamming/geofencing, DDIL doctrine and exercise bandwidth assumptions

### Takeaway
Real BLOS fallbacks are kbps or worse: narrowband HF carries 3.2–9.6 kbps per 3 kHz channel, Iridium SBD is 340-byte messages with 10–15 s round trips, Iridium Certus 100 measures ~1 s latency with 7–8 % UDP loss, and Starlink is now being jammed over ~20 km² patches; the US Army's own officials admitted Project Convergence ran on a "pristine" network, and the only quantified tactical-edge framework found (MITRE) assumes up to 2 Mbps per SOCOM user, far above what these fallbacks deliver.

### Cited Findings
- STANAG 4539 provides 3,200–9,600 bps coded and 12,800 bps uncoded in a 3 kHz channel (3200 bps QPSK up to 9600 bps 64-QAM with 3/4 FEC), each speed except 12800 having 6 interleaver modes — [sigidwiki STANAG 4539](https://sigidwiki.com/wiki/STANAG_4539)
- Wideband HF per Thales HF XL: 200 kHz divided into 16 channels of 3 kHz that hop, vs the single 24 kHz block of MIL-STD-188-110C (2017 reporting) — [Asian Military Review, 2017](https://www.asianmilitaryreview.com/2017/05/imdex-show-news-thales-showcase-new-naval-wideband-hf/)
- STANAG 5069 is a family of wideband HF waveforms for single non-hopping channels of 3–48 kHz, technically identical to MIL-STD-188-110 Appendix D, and used as the wideband traffic modem in 4G ALE (WALE) — [sigidwiki STANAG 5069](https://sigidwiki.com/wiki/STANAG_5069)
- STANAG 5066 Edition 4 (promulgated): frame sequence number grows to 16 bits because that is "needed for ARQ to work at wideband speeds"; rate selection moves to the sender — [Isode STANAG 5066 Ed4 whitepaper](https://www.isode.com/whitepaper/stanag-5066-ed4/)
- Isode (May 2026) documents operating TAK over HF, noting current systems "will generally use a more compact Protobuf encoding" of CoT — [Isode, Operating TAK over HF Radio (PDF, May 2026)](https://www.isode.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Operating-TAK-over-HF-Radio.pdf)
- EDA LORACO project (2021–2025) aims "to increase the HF bandwidth of 3 kHz with solutions provided by different STANAGs in order to transmit higher data rates" — [RMA Radiolab LORACO](https://radiolab.rma.ac.be/?p=1722)
- Iridium SBD via RockBLOCK 9603: send up to 340 bytes, receive up to 270 bytes; "one credit per 50 bytes", credits £0.04–£0.11 each, £10/month line rental; marketed as a BVLOS failover link — [Unmanned Systems Technology: RockBLOCK 9603](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/company/ground-control/rockblock-9603/)
- The Iridium network supports SBD messages up to 1,960 bytes but the lightweight SBD-only modem supports 340 bytes (older WMO guide) — [WMO WIS wiki file](https://old.wmo.int/wiswiki/tiki-download_file.php%3FfileId=1781)
- PX4 documentation: Iridium/RockBLOCK round trip "roughly 10 to 15 seconds with good signal quality"; a user measured ~15 s per transmission — [PX4 satcom docs](https://docs.px4.io/v1.14/en/advanced_features/satcom_roadblock.html); [SparkFun forum](https://community.sparkfun.com/t/failed-to-see-the-messages-sent-received-by-rockblock-9602-in-the-core-rock-7-websites-and-measured-transmission-time/60257)
- Iridium Certus 100 (SKYTRAC DLS-100): 22 kbps uplink / 88 kbps downlink (vendor); an independent ArduPilot MAVLink test over Certus 100 measured 800 ms latency (UDP) and 1.2 s (TCP), 7–8 % packet loss on UDP and 0 % on TCP, recommended lowering telemetry stream rate from 4 Hz to 2 Hz, and saw ≥50 % loss/outages through VPN tunnels; antenna must be above tree/roof line — [SKYTRAC DLS-100 sheet (2024)](https://sts.skytrac.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/SKYTRAC-DLS-100-US-EN-r05-May-1-2024-1.pdf); [ArduPilot forum test](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/ardupilot-and-the-iridium-certus-satellite-service/142601)
- Starlink jamming (Reuters, 8 July 2026): the Russian "Volna Kupol Garant" jammer "throws out a signal strong enough to destabilise a Starlink connection across roughly 20 square kilometres"; Ukraine has identified ~10 such systems and is targeting them; Ukraine's "mid-strike" drones are often flown via Starlink — [Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/07/08/russia-begins-jamming-starlink-to-counter-ukrainian-drones-reuters-reports); [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/79894); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/russia-elon-musks-starlink-jam-ukraine-drones/)
- SpaceX blocked unauthorised Russian terminals in late January/February 2026 at Ukraine's request; Brig. Gen. Biletski: "After the blocking of Starlink for the Russians, the level of their efficiency compared to ours has sharply decreased"; a Ukrainian general claimed Russia's drone campaign was cut by up to 40 % (self-reported) — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/musk-cutting-starlink-russia-drones-081935078.html)
- A 15 Sept 2025 report (Russian state media via GlobalSecurity, treat with caution) described a Starlink blackout along the entire Ukrainian front line — [GlobalSecurity/Sputnik, Sept 2025](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/09/ukraine-250915-sputnik01.htm)
- DDIL = denied, degraded, intermittent, limited (also "disconnected, disrupted, intermittent, limited/low-bandwidth"); characterised as "low bandwidth and high latency ... recurring disconnects from a broader computing network" — [ExecutiveBiz](https://www.executivebiz.com/articles/ddil-dod-cyber-cloud-cjadc2-low-bandwidth); [Legion Intel](https://www.legionintel.com/blog/what-is-ddil)
- MITRE Tactical Edge Characterization Framework: SOCOM-tailored values include ">99% connectivity (even for dismounted users) and up to two megabits of bandwidth per user" — [MITRE MTR080310 via DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1107094.pdf)
- Project Convergence data-sharing success "ran on a network that was essentially pristine and was not representative of what would maybe be in the field, an austere environment" (Joseph Welch, Army Futures Command, Oct 2024); NetModX tests the network/compute layer under terrain and spectrum challenges — [Defense News, Oct 2024](https://www.defensenews.com/land/2024/10/16/how-an-experiment-in-new-jersey-could-shape-the-armys-future-network/)
- NetModX runs eight weeks at JB-MDL/Aberdeen with ~80 technologies (2023) — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/268961)
- TSM (TrellisWare) ITN waveform is line-of-sight barrage relay where every radio repeats all traffic; scales to 200+ nodes per network (2018) — [C4ISRNET, 2018](https://www.c4isrnet.com/c2-comms/2018/03/01/the-army-radio-that-could-work-in-cities-caves-and-tunnels/)
- CoT over Meshtastic LoRa: raw CoT XML is ~400–2,300 bytes; compressed wire payloads have a median of 87 bytes and max 184 bytes against a 237-byte LoRa frame limit; the Apple implementation budgets 225 bytes after the envelope and drops the message if it still does not fit; V2 path uses zstd with a 4 KB dictionary for aircraft tracks — [TAKPacket SDK (klibs.io)](https://klibs.io/project/meshtastic/TAKPacket-SDK); [Meshtastic TAK protocol docs](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/apple/developer/tak-protocol/); [TAK protocol description](https://takproto.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tak_protocols/)

### Inferences
- A twin feed at 60–800 B/s sits between Iridium SBD (one 340-byte message per ~15 s ≈ 23 B/s) and Certus 100 (22 kbps ≈ 2.75 kB/s). Over SBD, MinBand's lower rates would need a byte budget of ~20 B/s and 2 s keyframes would have to stretch to ~15 s to fit one message per round trip. Over Certus or narrowband HF the full rate fits with margin.
- The measured Certus profile (≈1 s latency, 7–8 % UDP loss) and MinBand's 10–20 % loss design point overlap; the ArduPilot author's workaround (halve MAVLink rate) is the manual version of threshold widening under a byte budget.
- The Welch quote is the strongest available justification for a DDIL demo: the Army's flagship experiment admitted it had not tested on a degraded network.

### Gaps
- No kbps assumptions for NetModX/Project Convergence/EDGE exercises were found in public reporting; no 2024–2026 wideband HF trial with throughput figures was found.
- Iridium direct tariffs found were 2000s-era; only reseller pricing is current.
- The "front-line Starlink blackout" of Sept 2025 was found only in a Russian-state-media-sourced item; no Ukrainian or Western confirmation surfaced in this session.

## Loitering munitions and strike handoff (find-fix-track-target), Ukraine and Israel 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Ukraine's Delta turned a voice-relay chain (~20 min detection-to-strike) into a published detection record of coordinates + time + imagery + source + reporter handle that reaches strike crews in 3–5 min, with AI pre-flagging ~70 % of visible vehicles and a human validating each cue; Western and Israeli loitering munitions keep a man-in-the-loop over a datalink with control transferable between operators, and the recognised failure mode is a stale coordinate.

### Cited Findings
- Delta "combines drone video, radio intercepts, and satellite imagery, then assigns detected targets to reconnaissance and strike drones"; commanders say the detection-to-strike gap fell from about 20 minutes to 3–5 minutes; the platform tracks targets so officers see "when enemy equipment was first detected, how many times it was struck, and who carried out the strikes" — [RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/ukraine-has-battlefield-edge-us-is-now-trying-1787815768.html); [DroneXL, Aug 2026](https://dronexl.co/2026/08/26/us-army-ukraine-delta-drone-software-driscoll/)
- Pre-Delta: crews could see a target but relaying coordinates to the guns "meant a chain of radio calls, and the target was often gone by then" (Honchar, Aerorozvidka, per NYT) — [Charter'97 summary of NYT, Aug 2026](https://charter97.org/en/news/2026/8/26/696161)
- Modern War Institute: a published Delta detection "appears with coordinates, time, imagery, source, and the reporting user's handle"; "the AI flags a probable vehicle, and a human reviews the cue, classifies the object, and publishes it to a DELTA layer"; after a human selects a target or aim point, computer vision can maintain the track during the final approach; "a stale coordinate, weak identification, or poor handoff can send a technically successful drone to the wrong place" — [MWI, Borrow What Works](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/borrow-what-works-the-case-for-army-adoption-of-ukraines-combat-tested-horizontal-kill-web-architecture/)
- Ukraine's MoD says its AI detection identifies 70 % of enemy vehicles and equipment visible in processed video — [The Defense Post, Aug 2026](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/08/12/ukraine-ai-drone-target-detection/)
- Hedgehog 2025 (Estonia): ~10 Ukrainian drone operators as OPFOR against 16,000 NATO troops mock-destroyed 17 armoured vehicles in half a day with ~30 further strikes — [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/feature/the-next-evolution-ukraines-drone-defense)
- UVision Hero-120: "With man-in-the-loop at all times, the Hero120 is controlled via datalink-terminal and hand-held Fire-Control Unit"; operator can abort, return to loiter, select another target, re-approach or terminate; imagery and mission data can go to the tactical network and control can be transferred to another compatible operator or echelon — [Rheinmetall Hero-120 brochure](https://www.rheinmetall.com/Rheinmetall%20Group/brochure-download/Uncrewed-Systems/D1169e1025-HERO-120-portable-anti-tank-loitering-munition-system.pdf); [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/military-products/army/unmanned-systems/unmanned-aerial-vehicles/hero-120-loitering-munition)
- Hero ranges (2016-era vendor figures): Hero-30 5–40 km/30 min; Hero-70 45 km/45 min; Hero-120 40–60 km/60 min; Switchblade 600: 40 km+ standoff, ~40 min endurance (vendor) — [Air Recognition, 2016](https://www.airrecognition.com/index.php/news/airshow/air-show-2015/paris-air-show-2015/paris-air-show-2016/2028-uvision-unveils-comprehensive-range-of-new-smart-loitering-systems-the-hero-family.html); [AeroVironment](https://www.avinc.com/?p=861)
- IDF ordered Rafael Fire Weaver (Feb 2020) for armoured brigades; Rafael claims sensor-to-shooter loops "in seconds"; at US Army AEWE 21 soldiers "achieved effects in seconds that might traditionally have taken minutes"; AI target–weapon pairing with a human in the loop for lethal force (vendor/experiment claims, 2020–21) — [Janes, Feb 2020](https://www.janes.com/defence-news/news-detail/idf-orders-fire-weaver-network-fire-control-system); [EDR Magazine](https://www.edrmagazine.eu/rafael-u-s-army-conducts-operational-assessment-of-fire-weaver-sensor-to-shooter-system)
- Lieber Institute: in Ukraine "jamming often severs operator links" and operators "report engagement tempos they cannot meaningfully review"; argues per-engagement human-in-the-loop approval is effectively obsolete and proposes human responsibility at program selection and map designation — [Lieber Institute, Beyond Human-in-the-Loop](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/beyond-human-loop-battlefield-maps-drone-autonomy/)
- A Ukrainian national security official described FPV drones that autonomously recognise a tank/IFV/dugout and strike without operator control (May 2025); a NYT-reported case had a Russian drone autonomously pick a propane tank (secondhand summary) — [Apostrophe, May 2025](https://apostrophe.ua/en/news/society/2025-05-05/ukraina-formiruet-sobstvennyiy-front-ai-v-snbo-rasskazali-o-trendah/343070); [AI Understanding summary of NYT](https://aiunderstanding.org/news/new-york-times-reports-russian-drone-used-ai-to-select-a-target-in-ukraine)

### Inferences
- The Delta record (coords, time, class, source, image) is a superset of a MinBand entity (position, velocity, class). What Delta lacks per the MWI critique is freshness: a static published coordinate goes stale. A dead-reckoned entity with a predicted position and last-update age is a direct answer to "stale coordinate".
- The "3–5 minutes" handoff latency is dominated by human validation and tasking, not link bandwidth, so the twin's value in this chain is keeping the picture current while the human decides, and surviving the jammed interval in the terminal phase (where Lieber notes links get severed).

### Gaps
- No published byte-level sensor-to-shooter message format or measured link latency for Delta/Kropyva was found.
- No lost-link behaviour or datalink range figures were found for Hero or Switchblade.
- No 2024–2026 Israeli combat reporting on digital handoff timelines surfaced; Fire Weaver material is 2020–21.

## Interceptor drones and C-UAS: how a radar/acoustic track reaches an interceptor

### Takeaway
Ukraine's cueing chain is a national acoustic network of ~9,500–14,000 Android-phone sensors on cellular backhaul feeding the Virazh C2 system (≈20 % of targets detected only acoustically), with radar-integrated apps that let an interceptor operator select a track and have software fly the drone to it; interceptors went from ~25 % to 40–70 % of Shahed kills by early 2026 at $1,500–5,000 each, with operators now remote-piloting from hundreds of km away rather than on a local radio link.

### Cited Findings
- Sky Fortress sensor: a ~30×30×5 cm box containing a commercially available Android smartphone using local mobile networks, switched on continuously to record ambient sound; detections feed Ukraine's national air-defence C2 network "Virazh" — [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/weapons/ukraine-conflict-ukrainian-air-defence-employs-passive-sensors-for-detection-and-tracking)
- Network size: 9,500 microphones on six-foot poles (Foreign Policy, June 2025, each under $500); more than 10,000 sensors (Al Jazeera citing Reuters, May 2026); approximately 14,000 units (Defense Post); unit cost $400–1,000; "approximately 20 percent of targets entering Ukrainian airspace are detected solely through their acoustic signature" — [NV](https://english.nv.ua/russian-war/ukrainian-engineers-develop-sky-fortress-drone-detection-system-to-counter-shaheds-50520480.html); [Al Jazeera, May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/5/4/what-is-the-ukrainian-anti-drone-system-sky-map-being-used-in-the-gulf); [The Defense Post](https://gecici.thedefensepost.com/?p=108947)
- January 2026: a record 1,704 Shaheds shot down, ~70 % credited to interceptor drones (The Economist via DroneXL); separately, interceptors "now account for 40 percent of all Shahed-style kills, up from around 25 percent last winter" (Cherevashenko); production 950 interceptors/day (Dec 2025); Russia has massed 800+ Shaheds in a night — [DroneXL, March 2026](https://dronexl.co/2026/03/01/ukraine-is-scaling-up-interceptor-drones-as-russias-shahed-threat-outpaces-every-defense); [DroneXL, Pentagon](https://dronexl.co/2026/03/05/pentagon-ukrainian-interceptor-drones/)
- Costs: Shahed ~$10,000 (Zelensky) or $20,000–50,000 (other estimates); interceptors $3,000–5,000, some under $1,500; ZIRKA autonomous interceptor claimed at $2,000 (July 2026, manufacturer claim) — [Euromaidan Press, July 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/07/01/russias-shaheds-cost-10000-each-ukraine-just-unveiled-drone-that-kills-them-for-2000/)
- First-generation interceptors "relied on radio frequencies, which kept operators close to their targets and exposed them to Russian strikes"; Fedorov: Ukraine is the first to systematically deploy remote-piloted interceptors "from hundreds or thousands of kilometers away" — [Hvylya](https://en.hvylya.net/news/2330-ukraine-claims-world-first-in-remote-piloted-air-defense-drones)
- Brave1-backed system "integrates with the radar network, enabling it to track the movement of aerial targets in real time. The operator selects a target in a program and issues the command to engage, and the program guides the drone to the target on its own"; Sky Hunter "analyses data from Ukrainian radar systems, tracks air targets in real time and visualises their coordinates on a control panel" — [Brave1](https://brave1.gov.ua/en/news/brave1-maxon-systems-eng); [Ukrainska Pravda, Feb 2025](https://pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/02/24/7499878)
- Defense News (March 2026): Ukraine's most common intercepts "still start with cueing: radar" — [Defense News, March 2026](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/03/05/novel-interceptor-drones-bend-air-defense-economics-in-ukraines-favor/)
- Sky Sentinel gun turrets, fed radar data, "detect, lock on, track flight paths, calculate the shot, and fire"; 12+ installed in Kyiv with 8 more for the region (Oct 2026) — [United24, Oct 2026](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/meet-kyivs-new-jet-drone-killer-12-sky-sentinel-turrets-are-already-on-the-hunt-23126)
- Valkyrie Dynamics miniature radar homing head for interceptors: cm-level range accuracy, tracks up to 25 targets, shows distance, azimuth, elevation, speed on the operator's screen — [Militarnyi](https://militarnyi.com/en/news/radar-homing-head-interceptor-drone-ukraine/)
- Zelensky: interceptor production has overtaken the number of pilots available (operator shortage) — [Denver Gazette/Reuters, Sept 2025](https://denvergazette.com/2025/09/04/ukraine-commander-seeks-improved-use-of-interceptor-drones-7d1c52b6-9c57-532e-9859-a60f69ea97df)
- Sternenko Foundation funded HORNET VISION Ctrl remote control for Sting interceptors enabling operation "from significant distances" (March 2026) — [Wikipedia: Sternenko Community Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternenko_Community_Foundation)

### Inferences
- The acoustic network is a 10,000-node edge-sensor-to-twin system on commodity phones and cellular backhaul: each node contributes a time-stamped bearing/detection and the C2 fuses tracks. Its per-node payload is tiny; its hard problem is fusing and extrapolating tracks between sparse detections, which is a dead-reckoning problem.
- Remote piloting over internet backhaul means the interceptor video link is now the bottleneck in a chain that already carries tracks in bits. A compact, predictable Shahed track (≈ 180 km/h straight-line, so dead reckoning works well) is what an operator needs to be vectored; the video matters only for the last seconds.

### Gaps
- No source gave the track update rate, message format or radio used between Virazh/Sky Hunter and an interceptor operator, nor latency tolerances.
- Western C-UAS track-distribution rates (e.g., over SAPIENT/ASTERIX) were not researched in this session.

## Ground robots and unattended ground sensors on marginal links

### Takeaway
Ukrainian UGVs are ~99 % Starlink-dependent (industry estimate), get as little as 10 Mbps per terminal, drop in tunnels/tree lines and under FPV strikes on the antenna, and one casevac run lost link every 5 m so that 1.7 km took 2.5 h; the fixes are redundant short-range radios, mesh/relay robots and 60 m Wi-Fi fallback. The teleop video requirement is the part a state twin cannot replace, but the C2-side picture (robot position, status, route) is exactly what crews lose. Unattended ground sensors report detections to TAK over RF/SATCOM but no public byte formats were found.

### Cited Findings
- Starlink is used by roughly 99 % of deployed ground robots (industry estimate); the blog frames the problem as "50,000 ground robots need a better link than Starlink" — [Aerobavovna blog](https://blog.aerobavovna.com/ugv-connectivity-problem/)
- UGV terminals "have to make do with as little as 10 megabits per second, which results in poor quality of the video feed used to control the UGVs"; clouds, rain and tree canopy further degrade the signal (Oct 2025) — [Space.com, Oct 2025](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/satellites/spacex-starlink-internet-isnt-fast-enough-for-ukraines-combat-robots)
- ROBONEERS: sections of a route "such as a tunnel or a tree line, where LTE and Starlink simply cannot get a signal" risk leaving the vehicle without control; a hostile FPV can strike the antenna and disable the Starlink unit while the robot keeps working — [Militär Aktuell](https://militaeraktuell.at/en/ukrainian-company-equips-ugvs-to-counter-starlink-outages/)
- Kharkiv casevac run: "the Starlink connection dropped every five meters, stretching a 1.7 km trip to two and a half hours" — [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/mechanical-medics-transform-ukraines-frontline/)
- Lynx PRO/Lynx+ (Aug 2026) gained a combined LTE + Wi-Fi antenna; Wi-Fi works up to ~60 m so another UGV or drone can regain control — [United24](https://united24media.com/defense-tech/starlink-knocked-out-ukraines-lynx-robots-can-now-reconnect-over-wi-fi-21436)
- Units run robots on a decentralised mesh and sometimes use a separate robot as a forward relay; tethered drones carry repeaters; ground-to-ground radio reaches over 40 km with elevated relays — [HGBR](https://hgbr.org/ukraines-war-robots-strain-starlink-sparking-innovation/)
- Latvia's Natrix UGV (tested in Ukraine) carries two other radios, a medium longer-range one and a close-range one, so one can take over if the other fails; woodland is the new comms challenge (May 2026) — [Breaking Defense, May 2026](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/05/in-latvia-military-robots-roll-across-a-new-communication-challenge-woodlands/)
- After Russian UGVs lost Starlink (March 2026), operators are "unable to operate their ground robotic complexes remotely and must work them with handheld consoles from alongside" — [Euromaidan Press, March 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/03/12/russias-ground-drones-need-infantry-escorts/)
- UGS transmit "activity reports or imagery over radio-frequency or satellite (SATCOM) links to a remote processing, exploitation, and dissemination station"; a US Army TRSS-based setup has sensors report a detection to a TAK server which forwards it to clients — [Wikipedia: Unattended ground sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unattended_ground_sensor); [DTIC AD1171274](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1171274.pdf)
- Iridium markets partner UGS that send "data messages, photos, and videos" over its network (no sizes given) — [Iridium blog](https://iridium.com/blog/unattended-sensors)

### Inferences
- A UGV's own state (position, heading, speed, battery, mode) plus what its camera detector sees is well under 100 B/s; the 10 Mbps complaint is entirely about teleop video. A twin does not fix teleop, but it would keep the operator's and the C2's picture alive through the 5 m dropouts and make the hand-over to a relay robot or Wi-Fi fallback stateful instead of blind.
- The Russian handheld-console regression shows the floor: with no link at all, no codec helps; the design question is what a vehicle should do autonomously when the twin stops receiving deltas.

### Gaps
- No public byte counts, report rates or message schemas for any fielded UGS were found (would need vendor ICDs).
- No reporting on fibre-optic-controlled UGVs surfaced in this session.

## Satellite ISR: on-board processing, downlink budgets, tasking latencies

### Takeaway
Commercial on-orbit AI already does what MinBand does at the extreme: Ubotica claims a 200-byte ship-detection message replaces a 3 GB image and reaches the ground in under 10 min versus hours; the physics forcing this is 5–10 min passes, 4–6 contacts a day at mid-latitudes (<2 % of the day in contact) and hour-scale downlink latency.

### Cited Findings
- Ubotica CogniSAT-6 (July 2024, vendor claims): identified 142 ships outside Khor Fakkan within minutes of capture; "vessels are detected, sized and given a heading onboard, and what leaves the spacecraft is a 200-byte message instead of a 3 GB file"; answer reaches the ground "in under ten minutes, against the hours a conventional downlink takes"; metadata (location, size, orientation) is fused with AIS to flag anomalies — [Ubotica press release](https://ubotica.com/press-release-cognisat-6-breakthrough/); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366599605/Ubotica-claims-further-breakthroughs-in-live-earth-satellite-intelligence)
- Ubotica–NOVI Space (May 2026): downlink only extracted insights; in a demonstration the satellite "transmitted a localized, low-bandwidth alert packet directly to tactical maritime units instead of imagery" — [SatNews, May 2026](https://satnews.com/2026/05/18/ubotica-and-novi-space-form-strategic-partnership-for-real-time-onboard-space-ai/)
- Ubotica CogniSAT-CRC cloud filtering claims an 80 % downlink cost reduction while trebling useful data (2023 claim) — [SatNews, 2023](https://satnews.com/2023/03/07/ubotica-slashes-satellite-data-overload-with-on-board-ai-solution/)
- Satellogic $30 M contract (April 2025) "generates analytics directly onboard each satellite, enabling insights to be downlinked within minutes" (no payload detail) — [Satellogic](https://satellogic.com/news/press-releases/satellogic-awarded-30-million-contract-for-its-ai-first-constellation-services/)
- Planet Pelican-2 launched January 2025 carrying NVIDIA Jetson for on-orbit compute "to vastly reduce the time between data capture and value"; no public on-orbit detection results found — [SatNews, Jan 2025](https://satnews.com/2025/01/15/planet-launches-high-resolution-pelican-2-satellite-plus-36-superdoves); [Planet investor release, 2024](https://investors.planet.com/news/news-details/2024/Planet-Labs-PBC-Announces-Real-Time-Insights-Technology-Using-NVIDIA-Jetson-Platform/default.aspx)
- Research downlink modes range from "metadata only (box, class and score of each detection)" to image crops around vessels, trading vessels kept against volume downlinked; building-damage work proposes transmitting bounding boxes plus damage levels instead of imagery — [arXiv 2504.03705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03705); [arXiv 2605.29575](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29575); [EarthSight, arXiv 2511.10834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10834)
- LEO pass: ~8–10 min horizon-to-horizon directly overhead at 500 km, usually 5–7 min above a 10° mask; 4–6 passes/day per mid-latitude station; polar stations see almost every orbit; a LEO satellite is in range of a given station "no more than 10 percent of a day, and typically for less than 2 percent" — [SpaceNexus ground stations and latency](https://spacenexus.us/learn/communications/space-communications-101/ground-stations-and-latency); [US patent 10484095](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10484095)
- Centralised ground segments "incur large hour-level data download latency"; the L2D2 distributed design reduces downlink latency from 90 min to 21 min (SIGCOMM 2021) — [SIGCOMM 2021 L2D2](https://conferences.sigcomm.org/sigcomm/2021/files/papers/3452296.3472932.pdf)
- Tasking orders can be relayed to satellites through a GEO communications constellation; collected imagery waits for the next ground-station pass — [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=129458)
- BlackSky >$100 M seven-year contract (Jan 2025) cites sub-hourly revisit and delivery for 35 cm Gen-3 imagery (vendor language) — [BlackSky release, Jan 2025](https://s21.q4cdn.com/515173672/files/doc_news/2025/01/BlackSky-Wins-New-More-Than-100-Million-Contract-from-Strategic-International-Defense-Sector-Customer-for-Gen3-RealTime-SpaceBased-Monitoring-Capabilities.pdf)

### Inferences
- Satellite on-board AI is the strongest "adjacent precedent" for the pitch: a 200-byte detection versus a 3 GB image is the same bytes-per-entity argument at 10^7:1, and the same open question (when does the analyst need the chip?) applies.
- The satellite case differs from MinBand in one key respect: contacts are scheduled and predictable, and ship state between passes is mostly handled by AIS fusion rather than dead reckoning. A twin that dead-reckons between sparse contacts is more novel for drones than for satellites.

### Gaps
- No DARPA/NRO programme details on on-board processing surfaced (Pit Boss/Blackjack not in results).
- No measured tasking-to-delivery latency from Planet or BlackSky found; only marketing language.

## Disaster response and SAR: cellular-down, LoRa/satellite texting, drone search with human detection

### Takeaway
After Hurricane Helene 74 % of cell towers were out in affected North Carolina counties and even responders' own channels failed; the field tools that worked were satellite backhaul, direct-to-cell for alerts, and ATAK over Meshtastic LoRa (position, markers, chat in ≤237-byte frames). A peer-reviewed forest-SAR drone that runs detection on board and "sends rescue teams only the classification results" so it works over intermittent satellite links found 38 of 42 hidden people.

### Cited Findings
- Helene (late Sept 2024): "more than 74% of cell towers were out of service in disaster-impacted areas of North Carolina", later 17 %; fibre cuts and tower damage, "even the channels emergency responders rely on to talk to each other were affected" — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/hurricane-helene-causes-cellular-outages-across-several-us-states); [NC DPS blog, Nov 2024](https://www.ncdps.gov/blog/2024/11/06/helene-response-communications)
- FCC allowed SpaceX/T-Mobile to enable early Starlink direct-to-cell in hard-hit NC areas; "the focus is on enabling emergency alerts to smartphones" and expectations had to be managed — [RCR Wireless](https://www.rcrwireless.com/?p=409018); [Hawaii Tribune-Herald/AP](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/?p=331945)
- Help.NGO used a mix of GEO and LEO links; kit had to be "intuitive enough for non-technical responders" and battery-powered for teams on foot — [Intelsat Help.NGO case study (2025)](https://www.intelsat.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/HelpNGO-case-study.pdf)
- JKU Linz forest SAR drone (Science Robotics, 2021): thermal + airborne optical sectioning with onboard processing found 38 of 42 hidden persons in 17 field experiments; "the drone sends rescue teams only the classification results that indicate detections, so it can work with intermittent, low-bandwidth links such as satellite"; adaptive re-sampling raised confidence ~15 % and found 8 of 8 — [Inside Unmanned Systems](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/drone-can-see-through-dense-forests-to-help-rescue-people/); [JKU](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/an-autonomous-drone-for-search-and-rescue-in-forests-using-airbor/)
- LJMU coastal thermal-drone trial lists remaining barriers: range, flight time, hazard avoidance, and "data transfer limits" — [LJMU Research Online](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/11583/1/SAR_Published.pdf)
- Fire and Rescue NSW AI thermal drone found two lost hikers ~500 m off the track and used its speaker to guide rescuers — [Let's Data Science news](https://letsdatascience.com/news/fire-and-rescue-nsw-uses-ai-drone-to-locate-lost-hikers-e39cb79b)
- ATAK over Meshtastic for SAR: Akita MeshTAK plugin adds search sectors, geofences, stale-marker callouts to the ATAK map; recommended architecture is responders with phone + LoRa node for PLI/GeoChat, low-power GNSS trackers reporting periodic positions, elevated relay nodes — [hamradio.my Akita MeshTAK](https://hamradio.my/akita-meshtak-an-atak-plugin-for-off-grid-mesh-communication/); [SDR Store SAR hardware guide](https://www.sdrstore.eu/meshtastic-tak-hardware-guide-search-rescue-field-teams/)
- Bernalillo County (NM) Sheriff's SAR uses ATAK on Galaxy S21s and says it "dramatically increased the likelihood of completing a rescue safely" (vendor case study) — [Samsung Insights](https://insights.samsung.com/?p=20991)
- Avalanche study: UAV with Meshtastic T-beam LoRa receiver at 868 MHz located a buried LoRa transmitter — [PMC12664345](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12664345/)
- LoRa surveillance robot link: 2.0 km in obstructed environments, 15 km open, 5 km urban — [Academia.edu (2025)](https://www.academia.edu/143762935/LoRa_enabled_remote_controlled_surveillance_robot_for_monitoring_and_navigation_in_disaster_response_missions)

### Inferences
- The JKU design is the closest published analogue to MinBand's edge model: detector on the aircraft, classification results only across the link, explicitly to survive intermittent satellite links. Its adaptive re-look to raise confidence maps onto "image chip on demand".
- A SAR twin's entities are people and teams, which barely move (walking speed) so dead reckoning is near-trivial; the value is in the 2 s keyframe repair after mesh dropouts and in fitting a whole team's picture into 237-byte LoRa frames.

### Gaps
- No after-action report on drone-detected survivors being relayed over LoRa/satellite during a real cellular outage was found.
- No ATAK/drone usage report specific to Helene surfaced.

## Counter-arguments: where the twin idea fails, and what practitioners say they lack

### Takeaway
The twin fails where (a) the receiver needs raw pixels for human validation or control (Delta requires a human to classify each AI cue, AI finds only 70 % of vehicles, UGV operators need video to drive), (b) the link is not merely thin but gone (Starlink geofence/jammer, Russian UGVs reduced to walking consoles), or (c) the receiver's workload is already saturated (Saildrone throttles alerts to avoid flooding the command centre; Zelensky says interceptors outnumber pilots). What practitioners report lacking is a degraded-network testbed, more operators, and freshness/provenance on published detections.

### Cited Findings
- Delta keeps a human in the loop for every cue: "the AI flags a probable vehicle, and a human reviews the cue, classifies the object, and publishes it"; the published record includes imagery and source; a stale coordinate or weak identification sends a drone to the wrong place — [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/borrow-what-works-the-case-for-army-adoption-of-ukraines-combat-tested-horizontal-kill-web-architecture/)
- Ukraine's AI detection identifies 70 % of visible vehicles, i.e. roughly three in ten are missed — [The Defense Post, Aug 2026](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/08/12/ukraine-ai-drone-target-detection/)
- Lieber Institute: operators "report engagement tempos they cannot meaningfully review", and jamming "often severs operator links"; the US policy requirement to legally review new means of warfare remains; human control "cannot fully compensate for systems that cannot reliably apply distinction and proportionality" — [Lieber, Beyond Human-in-the-Loop](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/beyond-human-loop-battlefield-maps-drone-autonomy/); [Lieber, Compliance by Design](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/compliance-by-design-i-loac-us-autonomous-combat-platforms/)
- UGV teleop fails on bandwidth, not state: 10 Mbps gives "poor quality of the video feed used to control the UGVs" — [Space.com, Oct 2025](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/satellites/spacex-starlink-internet-isnt-fast-enough-for-ukraines-combat-robots)
- Total-blackout cases: Starlink link "abruptly halted 70 km away from the target" (2022 USV raid); Russian UGV crews reduced to handheld consoles alongside the robot after losing Starlink (2026) — [Euromaidan Press, Jan 2024](https://euromaidanpress.com/2024/01/03/up-elon-musk-indeed-thwarted-ukrainian-attack-on-crimea-by-blocking-starlink); [Euromaidan Press, March 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/03/12/russias-ground-drones-need-infantry-escorts/)
- Receiver saturation: Saildrone throttles images to every 15–20 min "so ... the command center isn't flooded with alerts" — [USCG LCMDA QuickLook, 2020](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/LCMDA_QuickLook_NOV2020.pdf); interceptor production has overtaken available pilots — [Denver Gazette/Reuters, Sept 2025](https://denvergazette.com/2025/09/04/ukraine-commander-seeks-improved-use-of-interceptor-drones-7d1c52b6-9c57-532e-9859-a60f69ea97df)
- What is missing on the US side: Project Convergence "ran on a network that was essentially pristine" (Welch) — [Defense News, Oct 2024](https://www.defensenews.com/land/2024/10/16/how-an-experiment-in-new-jersey-could-shape-the-armys-future-network/); Army Secretary says the US Army cannot match Delta — [DroneXL, Aug 2026](https://dronexl.co/2026/08/26/us-army-ukraine-delta-drone-software-driscoll/)
- Meshtastic's TAK compressor "returns nothing if it still does not fit" the 225-byte budget, i.e. existing low-bandwidth CoT paths drop rather than degrade — [Meshtastic TAK protocol docs](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/apple/developer/tak-protocol/)

### Inferences
- Domains where state is poorly predictable (dismounted infantry in cover, small boats manoeuvring, FPV terminal phase) will generate frequent threshold violations and lose the compression advantage; the twin's wins are largest for vehicles, ships, Shaheds and people on foot in open terrain.
- Legal/forensic needs are an argument for the "chip on demand" stretch goal rather than against the twin: the twin carries the cue, the chip carries the evidence, and the existing Delta record already pairs the two.
- The dominant unmet needs voiced in these sources are (1) freshness and provenance of a detection (MWI), (2) a realistic degraded-network testbed (Welch), (3) graceful degradation instead of drop (Meshtastic), and (4) operator time. A demo that shows age-of-information per entity and smooth degradation under a byte budget addresses the first three directly.

### Gaps
- No source was found stating a doctrinal requirement for raw full-motion video or imagery retention for legal review/BDA; the BDA-needs-imagery point is inference only.
- No practitioner quotes were found specifically on whether a dead-reckoned (predicted) position would be accepted as a basis for engagement versus a measured one.
- Fibre-optic FPV drones (no RF link at all) were not covered by any result in this session, so their relevance as a "nothing to compress" counter-case is unsourced here.
