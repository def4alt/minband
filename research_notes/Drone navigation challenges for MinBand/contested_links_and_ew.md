## Key question 1: Link types and real data rates / packet structures that drones actually fly with

### Takeaway
Control links run at tens of bits to a few kbit/s of usable telemetry (ExpressLRS telemetry at default settings is on the order of 80–100 bps), serial telemetry radios at 64 kbit/s air rate, mesh IP radios at tens of Mbit/s best-case (but narrowband modes of 12 Mbit/s or less), HF at 0.075–9.6 kbit/s per 3 kHz (up to 240 kbit/s wideband), Iridium SBD at 340 bytes per message with tens of seconds latency, and LoRa/Meshtastic LongFast at ~1 kbit/s raw with 354 ms airtime for a 16-byte packet. Digital FPV video needs 12–50 Mbit/s and so is in a different universe from any of the narrowband channels.

### Cited Findings

ExpressLRS (ELRS) control link
- ELRS telemetry ratio options, in increasing order of telemetry share: Off, 1:128, 1:64, 1:32, 1:16, 1:8, 1:4, 1:2; "1:64" means one packet in 64 is used for telemetry — [ExpressLRS Lua how-to](https://expresslrs.org/quick-start/transmitters/lua-howto)
- ELRS Telemetry Bandwidth table: at 500 Hz packet rate, ratio 1:128 gives ~3.9 telemetry packets/s and 78 bps; 1:64 gives 7.8 packets/s; 1:2 gives 250 packets/s; at 500 Hz and 1:16 the table lists 625 bps (no burst) and 1172 bps (burst) — [ExpressLRS Telemetry Bandwidth](https://www.expresslrs.org/info/telem-bandwidth/)
- "Burst mode attempts to maintain a minimum rate of LINK packets and use the rest of the available telemetry slots for DATA"; LINK packets = link statistics, DATA packets = flight-controller telemetry — [ExpressLRS Telemetry Bandwidth](https://www.expresslrs.org/info/telem-bandwidth/)
- ELRS 3.x "Std" telemetry mode "uses the Packet Rate to maintain approximately 4x telemetry packets per second for classic rates, or 3x packets per second for fullres rates"; "Race" mode turns telemetry off when armed — [ExpressLRS Telemetry Bandwidth](https://www.expresslrs.org/info/telem-bandwidth/)

SiK / RFD900x serial telemetry
- RFD900x factory defaults: "57600 baud, data rate N, 8, 1 and 64k" (i.e. 57.6 kbaud serial, 64 kbit/s air) — [jDrones wiki (RFD900x)](https://wiki.jdrones.com/com/jdrf900x)

### Inferences
- ELRS telemetry budget (derived from the cited table): 78 bps / 3.9 packets/s ≈ 20 bits of telemetry payload per telemetry slot. The default "Std" mode (~4 telemetry packets/s) therefore gives roughly 80 bps ≈ 10 bytes/s of uplink-direction telemetry; the maximum 1:2 ratio at 500 Hz would be about 250 × 20 bits ≈ 5 kbps (≈ 625 B/s) without burst. MinBand's quoted 60–800 B/s per feed would not fit ELRS telemetry at default settings and only fits at the most aggressive ratio.
- LoRa LongFast time-on-air (my calculation with the Semtech formula: SF11, 250 kHz, CR 4/5, 16-symbol preamble, explicit header, no LDRO; this reproduces the cited 354 ms for 16 bytes): 60-byte payload ≈ 680 ms; 100 bytes ≈ 1.0 s; 237 bytes ≈ 2.0 s. So a sustained 60 B/s stream occupies roughly 70 % of a LongFast channel by itself, and 800 B/s is impossible on LongFast; faster presets or SiK/RFD-class radios are needed for the upper end of MinBand's range.
- Iridium SBD is a natural "keyframe" transport: a 340-byte MO message would carry 20–40 entities at 8–16 bytes each, with 6–22 s latency and no continuous stream.
- Mesh radio headline rates are 10 m, wide-channel, single-hop figures; the narrowband modes actually used for range (3–5 MHz channels, 12–20 Mbps) and multi-hop relaying are where PLI-style traffic lives, and the Silvus 559-node demo shows that even PLI can consume a third of airtime at scale.

## Key question 2: How EW degrades these links in practice (partial loss vs denial, duration, geography)

### Takeaway
Front-line reporting gives EW-caused FPV loss rates from ~30 % of sorties (first-hand unit data) up to 75 % (a French general's estimate), with overall FPV mission success at 20–40 %; the dominant pattern is total link denial near the target rather than graceful degradation, and the main answers have been relay drones at altitude, frequency diversification and, above all, fibre-optic control.

### Cited Findings
- A former Ukrainian drone-unit member reports "enemy electronic warfare downed a full 31 percent of our sorties"; counting missions aborted before launch, success "drops to between 20 and 30 percent"; interference from other (friendly or hostile) drones "resulted in the failure of at least three percent of our missions" — [War on the Rocks, "I Fought in Ukraine and Here's Why FPV Drones Kind of Suck"](https://warontherocks.com/i-fought-in-ukraine-and-heres-why-fpv-drones-kind-of-suck/)
- Robert Brovdi ("Madiar"), commander of the Madiar strike-drone unit: FPV effectiveness "between 20% and 40%", counting only strikes that reach the target and are recorded; "a significant percentage is suppressed by various electronic warfare systems" (page metadata suggests late 2024) — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/44059)
- Hit rates for radio-controlled FPVs, estimated by Ukrainian operators at 40–60 % in favourable 2023 conditions, "reportedly dropped to 20 to 30 percent or lower in heavily jammed areas by mid-2024" — [Ukraine's Arms Monitor (Substack)](https://ukrainesarmsmonitor.substack.com/p/drone-warfare-in-ukraine-the-interplay)
- French Army Chief of Staff Gen. Schill "suggested that 75% of FPVs were being brought down by electronic warfare" (reported mid-2024) — [Forbes / David Hambling, Jan 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidhambling/2025/01/14/why-fpv-drones-are-still-ukraines-biggest-tank-killers/)
- A counter-drone site attributes "roughly 50%" of Ukrainian FPV losses to Russian jamming as a 2023 baseline (secondary source, unclear provenance) — [drone-warfare.com](https://drone-warfare.com/counter-uas/electronic-warfare/)
- Ukrainian EW officer Ihor Shutyi: vehicle-mounted jammers were effective against FPVs in early 2024, but once fibre-linked models appeared "there's nothing left to disrupt" — [Euromaidan Press, 26 Jun 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/06/26/ukraine-jamming-fails-fiber-drones-russia/)
- Relay drones: "Russians launch large drones with repeaters at 2–3 km altitude, hovering for hours, relaying signals to FPV drones. Hard to detect at altitude; neutralizing requires significant EW resources" (Aug 2025) — [Ukraine's Arms Monitor (Substack)](https://ukrainesarmsmonitor.substack.com/p/drone-warfare-in-ukraine-military)
- Draganfly's CEO claimed Russia may hide "lunchbox-size" ground repeaters in Ukrainian cities forming a mesh for encrypted drone relays (unconfirmed, single-source) — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/russia-may-using-hidden-lunchbox-size-drone-devices-counter-ukraine-defenses-expert)
- Early Ukrainian responses included "frequency diversification (use of 900 MHz, 433 MHz, and other bands outside initial jammer coverage)" (secondary analysis site) — [ukraine-war-analytics.com, EW 2026](https://ukraine-war-analytics.com/analysis/electronic-warfare-ukraine-2026.html)
- Some FPVs use "multiple receivers with antennas in different polarizations" and some can "dynamically change their operating frequency, use signal amplifiers" to evade EW — [drone-warfare.com](https://drone-warfare.com/counter-uas/electronic-warfare/), [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/44059)
- Typical FPV hit rates: "most FPV systems achieve a hit rate of 30–50%, but for inexperienced pilots this can be as low as 10%" — [Ukraine's Arms Monitor (Substack)](https://ukrainesarmsmonitor.substack.com/p/drone-warfare-in-ukraine-the-interplay)
- Russia, which "lost Starlink access in February 2026, had previously held an edge with fiber-optic systems first deployed in the Kursk region" — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/military/russia-launches-new-fiber-optic-kvs-drone)

### Inferences
- The figures span 31 %–75 % because they measure different things (sorties lost to EW vs. all failures vs. one officer's estimate), but every source agrees EW is the single largest cause of FPV mission failure in 2024–2025 and that the loss is binary (drone lost) rather than a degraded-but-usable link.
- Relay drones at 2–3 km altitude "hovering for hours" are themselves continuous emitters, which is why they are described as hard to neutralise only because of their altitude, not because they are quiet; a store-and-forward low-rate link could in principle ride such a relay with far less airtime.
- The WOTR "3 % of missions failed from interference by other drones" is a direct data point that spectrum congestion from friendly links, not only enemy jamming, is a real loss mechanism.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying typical jamming blackout durations (seconds vs minutes), the spatial extent of "jamming domes" around vehicles/trenches, or the "last-kilometre" geometry; searches on these were queued when the search budget expired.
- A claim that Russian "Boomerang" drones moved their video channel to 6–7.2 GHz, above the ceiling of most fielded detectors, appeared in the search tool's summary of results for the relay-drone query, but I could not confirm which page it came from; treat as unattributed.
- Per-link-class breakdown (what jammers do to analog video vs digital video vs ELRS 900 MHz vs 2.4 GHz vs GNSS) was not obtained; the Pravda "A shield against FPV drones" (8 Aug 2025) article likely contains this but was blocked — [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/articles/2025/08/08/7525279/)
- No 2026-dated EW loss percentage was found; the most recent hard numbers are 2024–2025.

## Key question 3: Fibre-optic FPV drones: scale, spools, weight, limits, and what still needs RF

### Takeaway
Fibre-optic FPVs went from a Russian Kursk-area novelty (2024) to mass production (Ukraine alone reports 352,000 delivered since July 2025), yet still constitute at most ~15 % of drones along the line of contact (Jan 2026); workhorse spools are 10 km, with 15–20 km less common and 30–100 km claimed; the trade-offs are weight (canisters up to ~5 kg), reduced manoeuvrability, snagging and cable-cutting, and they only remove RF from the strike drone itself, not from the reconnaissance, relay and C2 links around it.

### Cited Findings
- Serhii Melnyk (Ukraine's 69th centre): fibre-optic drones "make up no more than 15% of all drones along the entire line of contact", varying "sharply depending on the intensity and character of fighting and on how well individual units are supplied"; C-in-C Syrskyi said in January 2026 Ukraine was only "catching up"; ex-defence minister Shmyhal: 352,000 fibre-optic FPV drones delivered to the armed forces since July 2025 — [Ukrainska Pravda, 25 Jan 2026](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/articles/2026/01/25/8017810/)
- A Ukrainian commander asked what share of Ukrainian drones used fibre in mid-2025: "less than 5%" — [Euromaidan Press, 26 Jun 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/06/26/ukraine-jamming-fails-fiber-drones-russia/)
- "Through 2025 the 10-kilometer spool was the Ukrainian workhorse, with 15-kilometer spools less common and 20-kilometer spools still in testing"; typical spools "hold 5 to 20 kilometers" — [The War Zone, "Inside Ukraine's Fiber-Optic Drone War"](https://www.twz.com/news-features/inside-ukraines-fiber-optic-drone-war)
- Russian fibre-optic FPVs hit areas of Kramatorsk "more than 19 kilometres behind the front lines in October 2025" — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russian-fiber-optic-fpv-drones-appear-in-heart-of-ukraines-donbas-stronghold/); Russian systems "credited with more than 30 kilometers" — [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/news-features/inside-ukraines-fiber-optic-drone-war)

- Fibre optic links "are designed for hovering platforms like surveillance quadcopters, not for acrobatic FPV flying" — [Aomway 2026 guide](https://www.aomway.com/post/1264.html)

### Inferences
- The 10-km workhorse spool, ≤15 % share, and 352k deliveries together mean the majority of strike drones in 2026 are still RF-controlled and therefore still exposed to jamming and emission detection; fibre is a complement, not a replacement.
- Fibre removes RF only on the strike drone's own control/video path. The sources describe reconnaissance drones, repeaters at altitude, Starlink-equipped USVs and operator C2 as separate RF links, so target hand-off from a spotter to a fibre FPV crew and reporting of results to C2 still happen over RF; this is where a very-low-rate, low-duty-cycle entity link could matter.
- Weight numbers from vendors (80–160 g/km fibre plus a ~165 g spool body) imply a 10 km spool of roughly 1–2 kg including mechanism, consistent with the Moscow Times "up to 5 kg" for longer Russian canisters; the 0.5 g/m figure is an outlier.

### Gaps
- No source gives the share of fibre-optic drones in strikes (as opposed to inventory) for 2026.
- No source explicitly describes how a fibre FPV crew receives target coordinates from a reconnaissance drone (radio type, message format); the Defense News trainer interview (7 Nov 2025) probably covers this but was blocked — [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2025/11/07/of-fiber-optics-and-fpvs-6-questions-with-a-ukrainian-drone-trainer/)
- Fibre video/control bandwidth (it is typically full HD video over the fibre; figures not collected).

## Key question 4: Emission detection of ground stations/operators and EMCON practice

### Takeaway
Both sides passively detect and direction-find drone control and video emissions, operators have been targeted by artillery after their signals were geolocated, and even non-drone radios (line-of-sight Wi-Fi backhaul) become markers; detection ranges published for handheld detectors are a few kilometres, but no public source quantifies detectability as a function of transmit duty cycle or packet count.

### Cited Findings
- CRFS (RF-monitoring vendor): "drones emit RF signals, they too can be tracked and geolocated. There have been several incidences of drone operators in Ukraine being targeted by Russian artillery after their drone signals have been geolocated"; geolocation by TDOA/3D-TDOA and AoA — [CRFS, EMCON for ground forces](https://www.crfs.com/blog/emissions-control-emcon-for-ground-forces), [CRFS, RFeye for EMCON](https://www.crfs.com/blog/rfeye-for-emcon)
- Ukraine's Kvertus "Aero Azimuth" balloon-borne system "uses passive signals intelligence (SIGINT) equipment to detect and then locate the radio-frequency signals emitted by enemy (Russian) drone operators" (article ~2024; deployment status unclear) — [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/air/balloon-based-sensor-that-pinpoints-location-of-drone-operators-emerges-in-ukraine)
- Ukrainian commercial detectors (Dec 2023): a handheld unit "can detect a drone up to 2.4 miles away, send out a vibrating alert, and tell users what direction it's coming from"; another product is "a multi-antenna set-up tied to a computer workstation that identifies the direction and movements of multiple enemy drones" — [Defense One, Dec 2023](https://www.defenseone.com/threats/2023/12/ukraines-soldiers-use-cheap-tech-hide-russias-deadly-drones/392957/)
- A Ukrainian brigade soldier: Russian Wi-Fi-based alternatives to Starlink "require clear line-of-sight placement, making every antenna a marker for a drone operator position or EW station" — [Euromaidan Press, 21 Apr 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/04/21/ukraines-drone-operators-are-about-to-stop-carrying-five-different-controllers-to-front/)
- Hobbyist-level method is commoditised: a paid guide teaches using a TinySA Ultra spectrum analyser to "identify the radio frequencies used by the drone's controller or video transmitter, locate the strongest signal direction, and narrow down the operator's location" — [Cy-Hawk Labs](https://cyhawklabs.gumroad.com/l/upgfz)
- 2026 policy review: EMCON is "a passive, preventive defense mechanism that reduces electromagnetic emissions from friendly forces, thereby minimizing detectability"; "overly strict control may isolate units, while lax control increases exposure"; EMCON "provides limited effectiveness against drones that use optical or infrared sensors" and must be supplemented with camouflage, thermal masking and terrain concealment — [ResearchGate: Implementation of EMCON in Countering Drone Attacks (2026)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397551507_Implementation_of_Emission_Control_EMCON_in_Countering_Drone_Attacks_A_Conceptual_and_Policy_Review)
- LPD definitions: "the most popular way to achieve LPD is to spread signal energy over a wide bandwidth, such as with DSSS"; a signal that is LPD "is, by definition, also LPI and LPE" — [Emitter Detection and Geolocation for Electronic Warfare (book listing)](https://dokumen.pub/emitter-detection-and-geolocation-for-electronic-warfare-1630815640-9781630815646.html)
- Distributed sensor networks locate LPD emitters using "cyclo-stationary feature detection, or energy detection at sensor nodes ... or angles of arrival" — [US patent 10598762](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10598762)
- A US Navy SBIR topic sought an LPI/LPD UAV data link for "a reliable 30 km RF link between an unmanned aerial vehicle and a ground station" (design goal, not measured data) — [Navy SBIR 05.1 topic N05-198](https://www.navysbir.com/05_1/198.htm)
- Fibre-optic drones are explicitly chosen partly because "a radio FPV announces itself the moment it powers up and can be located by direction finding" — [Rifle Configurator](https://www.rifleconfigurator.com/articles/fiber-optic-drones)

### Inferences
- Everything found treats detection as a function of emitting at all; continuous video/control links (and 2–3 km-altitude repeaters) are the easiest emitters to find. A link whose ground side never transmits (simplex) and whose air side emits only short, infrequent bursts removes the ground-station emission entirely and shrinks the airborne one, which is consistent with the LPI/LPD doctrine summarised above, but no source quantifies the benefit.
- The commoditisation of DF (TinySA-class analysers, 2.4-mile handheld detectors) means emission control matters even against lightly equipped adversaries, not just national SIGINT.

### Gaps
- No quantitative relation between transmit duty cycle / packet count and probability of detection or geolocation accuracy was found; TDOA/AoA accuracy vs. burst length would be in EW textbooks or NATO STO papers that were not reachable.
- No detection-range figures for specific control links (ELRS 900 MHz / 2.4 GHz, 5.8 GHz video) from detector vendors were retrieved (planned search on "Bulat"/"Sky Sentinel"-type detectors did not run).
- No named, dated case of a specific operator team being struck after DF was found beyond the CRFS general statement; the searches for such cases did not run before the budget expired.
- The 35 GHz radar example in results (13.9 km mainlobe, 923 m sidelobe detection) is not applicable to a GCS link and is omitted from findings.

## Key question 5: Bit-level formats already used for low-rate tactical messages

### Takeaway
Fielded military formats are bit-packed: Link 16 uses 70-bit data words with 3/6/12 words per time slot; VMF/MIL-STD-2045-47001 is bit-oriented with field-presence indicators; ADS-B is a 112-bit (14-byte) frame with 56 payload bits; Remote ID uses 25-byte messages; Iridium SBD is 340 bytes. On the TAK side, CoT XML is "a few hundred" to 400–2300 bytes per event, TAK protobuf is smaller, and Meshtastic's zstd-dictionary TAKPacketV2 reaches a median 87 B / max 184 B wire payload per CoT event, with a bare PLI holding five scalar fields. DIS mandates a 5-s heartbeat and position/orientation thresholds but the numeric defaults were not retrievable.

### Cited Findings

Link 16 / J-series
- "Each Link 16 data word contains 70 bits of data"; a message in one time slot spans "3, 6, or 12 Link 16 data words, depending on whether the Standard (STD), Packed-2 double packed (P2DP), or Packed-4 (P4) data packing structure is used" — [US patent 11109373 (multiple message single-timeslot relay)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11109373)
- Another description lists packing modes STD-1, STD-2 and PACK-4 (single/double pulse) with up to 12 words per slot, PACK-4 requiring an Enhanced Throughput terminal — [Brainscape data-link flashcards](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/system-3-data-link-3422265/packs/5255283)
- Each J-series message has a label J0–J31 and a sublabel; "256 possible fixed-format messages: 32 categories (e.g. J12), 8 subcategories (e.g. J12.6)" — [Wikipedia, TADIL-J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TADIL-J); transmission types: Fixed Word Format, Free Text, Variable Message Format, Round-Trip Timing — [US patent 11109373](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11109373)
- VMF "originated in Link 16 and is one of the J-series message formats, along with Link 22 and the integrated broadcast system common message format" — [Canada DAOD 6002-5](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/policies-standards/defence-administrative-orders-directives/6000-series/6002/6002-5-joint-tactical-data-links.html); a DSTO Wireshark dissector for Link 16 is described in [DTIC ADA603968](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA603968.pdf)

VMF (MIL-STD-2045-47001 / MIL-STD-6017)
- "The VMF application header is defined by MIL-STD-2045-47001, and the message body consists of K Series messages"; the main VMF standard is distribution class C, but "the standard for the header is openly available" — [Wikipedia, Variable Message Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_Message_Format)
- VMF is controlled by three documents: MIL-STD-6017 (rules and K-series catalogue), MIL-STD-2045-47001 (mandatory header), MIL-STD-188-220 (combat-net-radio protocols); coding is "bit-oriented ... messages are passed via binary code" to reduce data volume; the syllabus names Data Field Identifier (DFI) and Data Use Identifier (DUI) — [Tonex VMF course listing](https://www.tonex.com/training-courses/variable-message-format-vmf-training-bootcamp/); revisions cited as 47001C — [Knogin](https://knogin.com/developers/vmf-mil-std-47001-message-catalog) and 47001D — [KoreaScience paper](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO200935535848349.pdf)

Cursor-on-Target / TAK
- "The typical size of the CoT XML messages is a few hundred bytes" — [Isode, Operating TAK over HF Radio (2026)](https://www.isode.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Operating-TAK-over-HF-Radio.pdf); the Meshtastic SDK quotes 400–2300 bytes of CoT XML per event — [TAKPacket SDK](https://klibs.io/project/meshtastic/TAKPacket-SDK)
- TAK Protocol Version 1 payload is "a Google Protocol Buffer based payload ... one (and only one) atakmap::commoncommo::v1::TakMessage"; stream framing is "191 <varint payload length> <payload>"; "current systems will generally use a more compact Protobuf encoding" — [takproto docs](https://takproto.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tak_protocols/), [takprotobuf (PyPI)](https://pypi.org/project/takprotobuf/)
- Meshtastic ATAK plugin: V1 path on port 72 carries a bare TAKPacket protobuf for PLI and GeoChat only (firmware <2.8.0); V2 path on port 78 uses TAKPacketV2 compressed with a zstd dictionary (firmware ≥2.8.0) and carries PLI, GeoChat, shapes, markers, routes, casevac, emergency, task — [Meshtastic TAK integration](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/android/user/tak/)
- TAKPacketV2 wire payload "median 87B, max 184B" within "≤237 B MTU"; dictionaries: 4 KB aircraft (ID 0x01), 512 KB non-aircraft (ID 0x00); if compression does not shrink the protobuf the raw protobuf is sent with a flag byte; if still too large the compressor retries with <remarks> stripped; wire payload = one flags byte + compressed protobuf — [TAKPacket SDK](https://klibs.io/project/meshtastic/TAKPacket-SDK), [Meshtastic TAK protocol (Apple dev doc)](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/apple/developer/tak-protocol/)
- Meshtastic protobuf schema (v2.7.26): ATAK.PLI = {latitudeI, longitudeI, altitude, speed, course}; ATAK.TAKPacket = {isCompressed, contact, group, status, oneof pli/chat/detail}; TAKPacketV2 adds cotTypeId, how, uid, callsign, team, role, battery, geoSrc/altSrc, staleSeconds, remarks, sensorFov, etc.; Mesh.MeshPacket carries from, to, channel, id, hopLimit, hopStart, nextHop, relayNode and flags including pkiEncrypted and xeddsaSigned; Mesh.Data carries portnum, payload, dest, source, requestId, replyId and an xeddsaSignature — [jsr.io @meshtastic/protobufs 2.7.26](https://jsr.io/@meshtastic/protobufs@2.7.26/doc)
- Older community bridge (atak-forwarder) reported "PLI: ~190 bytes" per message with its own compression — [atak-forwarder README](https://github.com/paulmandal/atak-forwarder/blob/main/README.md)
- Meshtastic TAK_TRACKER role "turns off many of the routine broadcasts to favor ATAK CoT packet stream and automatic TAK PLI broadcasts", using the position-module interval — [Meshtastic protobufs (DeviceConfig Role)](https://jsr.io/@meshtastic/protobufs@2.5.7/doc/~/Config.Config_DeviceConfig_Role)

Remote ID / ADS-B
- ASTM F3411 Broadcast Remote ID messages are 25 bytes each; a Bluetooth 5 extended-advertising "Message Pack" allows a 255-byte payload, commonly "up to five standard ASTM Broadcast RID messages (each of which are 25 bytes) plus a single authentication message (5 pages of 25 bytes each)"; Bluetooth 4 "can only transmit 25 bytes of payload where the others all can support 252 byte payloads"; the Authentication Message is paged, up to 16 pages — [IETF draft-ietf-drip-auth-03](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-drip-auth-03), [draft-ietf-drip-auth-12](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-drip-auth-12)
- ADS-B DF17 extended squitter: 5-bit Downlink Format + 3-bit Capability + 24-bit ICAO address + 56-bit ME field (first 5 bits = Type Code) + 24-bit parity = 112 bits (14 bytes) — [gr-adsb overview](https://www.mintlify.com/mhostetter/gr-adsb/concepts/adsb-overview), [gr-adsb message types](https://www.mintlify.com/mhostetter/gr-adsb/concepts/message-types)

DIS (IEEE 1278.1)
- DIS "specifies a duration which is necessary to refresh the entities states with duration equal to HEART BEAT TIMER fixed to 5 seconds"; when extrapolated state drifts past a position threshold (TH_pos) or orientation threshold (TH_or) a new state is transmitted (numeric defaults not given) — [arXiv 1008.3758, survey of QoS/dead reckoning in DIS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.3758)
- NPS DIS data dictionary lists "Entity State PDU Size: 1280" (unit unstated; if bits, 160 bytes) and Dead Reckoning Parameters = algorithm (8-bit enum), entity acceleration, angular velocity — [NPS DIS data dictionary](https://faculty.nps.edu/brutzman/vrtp/mil/navy/nps/disEnumerations/JdbeHtmlFiles/pdu/29.htm)
- Open-DIS implements the DIS dead-reckoning algorithms as a library — [open-dis DIS_DeadReckoning javadoc](https://open-dis.sourceforge.net/javadoc/open-dis/docs/edu/nps/moves/deadreckoning/DIS_DeadReckoning.html)

### Inferences
- A byte-count ladder for one entity position report, from the cited numbers: ADS-B 14 B (7 B payload) < Remote ID 25 B < Link 16 one STD slot ≈ 3 × 70 bits ≈ 26 B (data only) < Meshtastic TAKPacketV2 median 87 B < Meshtastic PLI-over-LoRa ~190 B (old bridge) < CoT XML 400–2300 B. MinBand's proposed 8–16 B per entity sits at the ADS-B/Link-16 end, which is defensible only if it carries far fewer fields (no callsign/uid strings, no timestamps as strings) than CoT.
- The Meshtastic experience (dictionary compression, stripping remarks, 225-byte budget) is evidence that teams already fight for every byte when putting TAK over LoRa, so a native bit-packed delta codec is not an exotic idea.

### Gaps
- Exact bit layouts of a Link 16 J2.x/J3.x track message, the VMF header fields (version, FPI bits, etc.), and MIL-STD-6017 K-series sizes are in distribution-restricted standards; not available here. My recollection that a Link 16 word is 75 bits on air (70 data + 5 parity) is unverified.
- Entity State PDU length (commonly stated as 144 bytes in the 1995 standard) conflicts with the NPS "1280" entry; could not resolve because the NPS page was blocked.
- Default DIS thresholds (my recollection: 1 m position, 3 deg orientation, 5 s heartbeat, 12 s timeout) could not be sourced; only the 5-s heartbeat is confirmed.
- STANAG 4586 message sizes: not found (no search ran).
- Exact serialized byte size of a Meshtastic bare PLI (five varint/fixed fields, likely under 25 B) and of a TAK Protocol v1 PLI could not be computed because the .proto wire types and the TAK proto were not retrievable.
- Remote ID Location/Vector field-by-field layout: not retrieved.

## Key question 6: Authenticated encryption overheads on constrained tactical links and what militaries require

### Takeaway
Deployed constrained-link schemes spend 4–16 bytes per frame on authentication: IEEE 802.15.4 AES-CCM with MIC-32/64/128 (4/8/16 B tags), MAVLink 2 signing at a fixed 13 B (6 B SHA-256-truncated signature + 6 B timestamp + 1 B link ID, no encryption), and NIST's Ascon with a 16-byte tag that SP 800-232 now allows to be truncated to 32–128 bits (sub-64-bit tags only after careful risk analysis). US military classified links require NSA Type 1 (e.g. Type 1 Link 16 and HF radios, a Type 1 conversion of Puma's DDL), with CSfC layered COTS as the alternative at a size/weight/performance cost.

### Cited Findings
- MAVLink 2 signing: "The 13 bytes of the signature are a link ID, a timestamp, and a signature"; link ID 8 bits, timestamp 48 bits "in 10 microsecond units since 1st January 2015 GMT and must monotonically increase for every message on a particular link", signature 48 bits "based on the complete packet, timestamp, and secret key"; signed packets set incompatibility flag 0x01; "when no key is present, signing is completely bypassed with no CPU or bandwidth overhead" — [MAVLink message signing](https://mavlink.io/en/guide/message_signing.html); PX4 notes the signature is SHA-256-based and "does not encrypt the message payload" — [PX4 MAVLink message signing](https://docs.px4.io/main/zh/mavlink/message_signing.html)
- IEEE 802.15.4 security levels: MIC-32 = 4-byte tag, MIC-64 = 8 bytes, MIC-128 = 16 bytes; nonce is 13 bytes = 8-byte source address + 4-byte frame counter + 1-byte security level — [IEEE P802.15-24-0617 comments to NIST SP 800-232](https://mentor.ieee.org/802.15/dcn/24/15-24-0617-04-04ae-comments-to-nist-sp-800-232-ipd.docx)
- Ascon: original specification defined only 128-bit tags; the standardisation proposal asked for shorter ones — [NIST LWC 2023 workshop paper on Ascon standardisation](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/Events/2023/lightweight-cryptography-workshop-2023/documents/accepted-papers/03-proposals-for-standardization-of-ascon-family.pdf); NIST SP 800-232 allows tag truncation to 32–128 bits but tags under 64 bits "should only be chosen after a careful risk analysis"; the COSE-Ascon draft calls the 32-bit variant discouraged, "for extremely constrained use cases" — [draft-ochkas-cose-ascon-03](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ochkas-cose-ascon-03.xml); a draft 802.15.4 annex lists Ascon with 16-byte key, 16-byte nonce and 4/8/16-byte MIC — [IEEE P802.15-24-0484 list of changes](https://bibliotheek.ehb.be:3499/802.15/dcn/24/15-24-0484-03-04ae-list-of-changes-to-ieee-std-802-15-4.docx)
- AES-CCM needs only the AES encrypt primitive (small footprint) but makes two passes over the data — [RFC 6655 (AES-CCM for TLS)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/inline-errata/rfc6655.html)
- Doodle Labs: "using 256-bit AES caps throughput at 12 Mbps, while 128-bit AES gives full throughput" (hardware crypto limit on a fielded mesh radio) — [miniOEM 2450 datasheet](https://mouser.com/datasheet/2/895/miniOEM_2450-3446604.pdf)
- Meshtastic's current protobuf schema contains per-packet `pkiEncrypted`, `xeddsaSigned`, `publicKey` and `xeddsaSignature` fields, i.e. optional public-key encryption and XEdDSA signing exist alongside channel encryption — [jsr.io @meshtastic/protobufs 2.7.26](https://jsr.io/@meshtastic/protobufs@2.7.26/doc)
- NSA Type 1 = "a classified or controlled cryptographic item endorsed by the NSA for securing classified and sensitive US Government information, when appropriately keyed" — [ExecutiveBiz](https://executivebiz.com/?p=226905); Viasat/AeroVironment effort to convert the Puma AE's Digital Data Link radios "into a Type 1 crypto communication system for video and data transmission" (company announcement) — [ExecutiveBiz](https://executivebiz.com/?p=226905); Viasat BATS-D handheld Link 16 radio achieved NSA Type 1 — [Viasat press release](https://investors.viasat.com/node/21976); Raytheon LLC 7M encryptor holds the "only Type 1 certification for ... Secret level in the Link 22 environment" — [Military & Aerospace Electronics](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/rf-analog/article/16725957/raytheon-to-provide-nsa-type-1-encryption-for-navy-link-22-radio-communications-networks); L3Harris AN/PRC-160 "world's first and only Type 1 HF manpack" meeting crypto-modernisation, up to Top Secret — [L3Harris AN/PRC-160 datasheet](https://www.l3harris.com/sites/default/files/2020-07/cs_tc_datasheet_an-prc-160v-wideband-hf-vhf-tactical-radio.pdf)
- Type 1 vs CSfC (vendor-sponsored view): Type 1 adds "MIL-STD ruggedization and TEMPEST protections" and "Render Useless Zeroize for unmanned or leave-behind applications"; "the layering approach associated with CSfC can often degrade performance and add size, weight, power, and cost" — [Breaking Defense sponsored Q&A with GDMS, Nov 2021](https://breakingdefense.com/2021/11/your-tolerance-for-risk-in-critical-comms-will-drive-your-security-solution/); PacStar's CSfC-registered tactical gateway is pitched as "eliminating Type 1 hardware encryption devices that require special handling" (vendor claim) — [Help Net Security, Sep 2019](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2019/09/20/pacstar-tactical-wireless-gateway/)

### Inferences
- A "~12 B overhead" authenticated-encryption design (e.g. 8-byte tag + 4-byte counter/nonce hint) is in line with fielded practice: 802.15.4 MIC-64 plus its 4-byte frame counter is exactly 12 bytes on the wire, and MAVLink's 13-byte trailer is the same order. An 8-byte tag matches the 802.15.4/NIST comfort zone; a 4-byte tag is allowed but explicitly "discouraged".
- Militaries will not accept hackathon crypto for classified traffic (Type 1 or CSfC required); the honest claim is "commercial-grade AEAD with minimal overhead, key management out of scope".

### Gaps
- Meshtastic's channel cipher (my recollection: AES-128/256 in CTR mode with no per-packet MAC for channel messages, and PKI only for direct messages since 2.5) could not be verified: meshtastic.org was blocked and the search budget expired before the query ran.
- No official DoD/NSA document was found stating crypto requirements per UAS Group (1–5) or whether CSfC is accepted for small-UAS data links.
- Ascon-based deployed tactical links: none found (Ascon in 802.15.4 is still a draft annex).

## Key question 7: Counter-arguments: why operators insist on video, and low-rate metadata links that were tried and rejected

### Takeaway
No source was found of a low-rate metadata-only link being tried and rejected by operators; the evidence that exists says detections must be validated before they count as intelligence and that video remains the verification layer, while industry pushes on-board metadata precisely to cut bandwidth and operator overload.

### Cited Findings
- "A detected object is not automatically intelligence"; observations must go through "processing, analysis, comparison, and validation before they are useful" — [Defense and Munitions interview with DroneAsAService.com](https://www.defenseandmunitions.com/news/defense-and-munitions-online-exclusive-drone-as-a-service-dot-com-daas-drone-sensor-data-operational-intelligence/)
- Embedded AI on military drones can generate "metadata such as object trajectories, speeds, and behavior patterns" on board and send it selectively, "which reduces bandwidth needs and operator overload" — [IDGA](https://www.idga.org/government-defense-it-communications/articles/embedded-ai-in-military-drones-is-redefining-autonomy-and-operations)
- Madiar's effectiveness metric counts a strike only when "a drone successfully reaches its target, delivers the strike, and the impact is duly recorded", i.e. video confirmation is part of the accounting — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/44059)
- Ukrainian USV operators "wait until conditions are right and then attack the target from multiple angles using remote control and video feeds" — [Hudson Institute](https://hudson.org/technology/sea-drones-russia-ukraine-war-inspire-new-tactics-bryan-clark)
- Forbes (4 Nov 2025) reported a new Ukrainian set-up that "turns drone operators into squadron commanders" (headline only; body not read) — [Forbes / David Hambling](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidhambling/2025/11/04/new-setup-turns-ukrainian-drone-operators-into-squadron-commanders/)

### Inferences
- The strongest counter-argument is accountability rather than bandwidth: strike crews are judged on recorded impacts, so a link that carries only "entity at X moving at V, class=tank" cannot replace the video that proves the hit. A low-rate entity link is therefore most credible for the reconnaissance/hand-off and C2-picture roles (where PLI-over-Meshtastic and Link 16 already live), not for terminal guidance or BDA.
- The repeated Meshtastic/TAK work on squeezing CoT into 87–190 bytes suggests the metadata-only use case is being adopted rather than rejected, at least for position reporting.

### Gaps
- Searches on operator trust in autonomous terminal guidance and on failed metadata-only links did not run before the search budget expired; no primary operator testimony either way was collected.
- The War on the Rocks first-person piece likely contains operator views on video dependence but only its EW statistics were visible in snippets.
