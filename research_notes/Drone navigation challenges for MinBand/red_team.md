# Red team: the case against "send a predicted-state digital twin instead of video"

Researcher note on method and limits (read first): the egress proxy blocked WebFetch for every substantive domain tried (darpa.mil, breakingdefense.com, csis.org, pubmed, jhuapl.edu, dtic.mil, arxiv.org, meshtastic.org, grantedai.com, bwcoconsulting.com), and the shared WebSearch budget ran out before the last five planned queries. Every finding below therefore rests on search-engine result snippets and summaries, not full-text reads. Quotes are as rendered in those snippets. Where a snippet did not unambiguously tie a number to one URL, I say so. Facts are dated where the source gave a date; anything from before 2023 is marked as older material. Current as of 9 Oct 2026.

Framing for the report writer: the strongest objections are not "AI detectors are bad" (everyone knows that) but (1) the people who would consume the feed are, by doctrine, policy and incentive, required to see pixels before acting; (2) every prior "picture made of tracks" system that reached the field (Patriot, Aegis, BFT, Link 16, JSTARS) produced a well-documented class of accident or distrust that a predicted-state feed reproduces in its purest form; (3) a smooth dead-reckoned display hides exactly the information a commander needs (staleness, uncertainty, whether anyone has looked); (4) DARPA's own current programme on this exact problem explicitly asks for reduced video with traceability and object-agnostic selection, not for replacing video with class labels; and (5) at MinBand's own byte budget, the honest competitor is not "64 kbit/s H.265" but "a 100–200-byte thumbnail every few seconds", which the project must beat on mission utility, not on bytes.

## Key question 1: What do after-action reports and journalism from Ukraine (2023–2026) say about AI target recognition reliability on drones, decoys, and the share of false identifications?

### Takeaway
Journalism and think-tank work from 2024–2026 consistently reports that first-generation AI targeting on Ukrainian drones underperformed, that operators distrusted it and used it only when jamming left no alternative, and that inflatable/thermal decoys confuse both algorithms and humans; however, no source gives a measured false-identification share for AI recognition against decoys, and the only hard numbers are hit rates (10–50 % manual; vendor claims of 80 % with AI; one brigade's 50 % in combat) and one acoustic-sensor false-positive figure (1.6 %), none of which measure class confusion.

### Cited Findings
- Ukrainian and Russian forces both use inflatable tanks and false artillery, which "confound recognition algorithms and human operators alike"; AI recognition models need continuous real-world data updates as adversaries refine evasion tactics — [CSIS, "Ukraine's Future Vision and Current Capabilities for Waging AI-Enabled Autonomous Warfare"](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ukraines-future-vision-and-current-capabilities-waging-ai-enabled-autonomous-warfare)
- CSIS cites a false-positive rate of 1.6 % for Zvook (an acoustic drone-detection network); the snippet does not say decoys were tested, so this is not evidence about visual decoy robustness — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ukraines-future-vision-and-current-capabilities-waging-ai-enabled-autonomous-warfare)
- CSIS front-line interviews: most small, low-cost FPV missions succeed only about 10–15 % of the time; highly skilled operators reach 30–50 %; CSIS *projects* (not measures) that autonomous navigation could raise success from ~10–20 % to ~70–80 % — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ukraines-future-vision-and-current-capabilities-waging-ai-enabled-autonomous-warfare)
- Feb 2024: machine-vision algorithms "have proven even easier to deceive than human operators" (no test data given); CNAS says Lancet-3 autonomous-targeting claims are unverified — [Breaking Defense, "The revolution that wasn't: how AI drones have fizzled in Ukraine (so far)"](https://breakingdefense.com/2024/02/the-revolution-that-wasnt-how-ai-drones-have-fizzled-in-ukraine-so-far/)
- Target-detection software is "unreliable and still needs operator input" — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/chicken-wire-ai-and-mobile-phones-on-sticks-how-the-drone-war-in-ukraine-is-driving-a-fierce-battle-of-innovation-235503)
- Mar 2025: footage of a Ukrainian drone investigating apparent Russian T-72s that turned out to be inflatables; convincing from a distance, exposed on closer inspection by rounded corners and lack of detail; decoys "must be convincing enough to fool drone and satellite detection algorithms" — [Forbes (Mittal), 3 Mar 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/vikrammittal/2025/03/03/russia-and-ukraine-are-deploying-increasingly-advanced-decoy-tanks/)
- Russian footage of drones "hitting" M270 launchers often showed inflatable decoys; a decoy succeeds even if only recognised as fake after munitions are spent — [Forces News](https://www.forcesnews.com/ukraine/bouncy-castles-battlefield-ukraine-deploying-inflatable-afvs)
- Brave1 AI lead Max Makarchuk: FPV hit rates 30–50 %, as low as 10 % for novices; he *expected* AI could raise accuracy to ~80 %. Early AI-guided drones "performed poorly, disappointing Ukrainian army units that had paid for them. Few wanted to keep paying." Units often prefer fibre-optic drones. ISW (June 2025): promises of an immediate AI drone revolution are premature; problems include poor camera quality, difficulty hitting moving targets, inconsistent software — [ASPI Strategist](https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/why-ukraines-ai-drones-arent-a-breakthrough-yet/)
- Jan 2026: first-generation result was "a lot of unimpressed FPV operators who would only use automated guidance when jamming made it impossible to do the job manually"; automated systems lacked the human skill of finding the specific weak spot; a second generation appeared in 2025; >100 Ukrainian companies now work on guidance — [Forbes (Hambling), 2 Jan 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidhambling/2026/01/02/ukraines-killer-ai-drones-are-back-with-a-vengeance/)
- Vendor claim (The Fourth Law, Azhnyuk): hit rate raised from 20 % to 80 % in one brigade; "2–4 times" effectiveness for +10 % cost — [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/ukraine-is-autonomizing-more-of-its-drones-ai-is-only-part-of-the-solution/)
- TFL-1 module: 100 % over 10 trial launches, but 412th "Nemesis" brigade reported ~50 % success in its first 20 combat uses — [NextGenDefense](https://nextgendefense.com/ai-upgrade-ukrainian-drones/)
- Thermal camouflage (May 2025): cloaks reduce heat signature but "the silhouette of the cold spots is often still visible"; most Russian units lack them — [Euromaidan Press, 7 May 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/05/07/frontline-report-russians-become-walking-trash-bags-to-evade-ukrainian-thermal-drones); a 63rd Brigade video of cloaks failing could not be independently verified — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/41476); Russian firm HiderX claims 600 IR-blurring suits per month (manufacturer claim, Aug 2026 article) — [EurAsian Times](https://www.eurasiantimes.com/russia-bets-on-invisibility-suits-to-counter/)
- Aerial small-object detection is hard for nano models: an improved YOLOv8n variant on VisDrone reports 37.6 % mAP at 3 M parameters / 40 FPS on an edge device, and other papers report only relative gains (e.g. +6.3 % mAP50 for MBD-YOLO-n over YOLOv8n; +17.2 % relative mAP for a C3Ghost/attention variant). Exact claim-to-paper mapping could not be confirmed from snippets — [Scientific Reports (Jan 2025) via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ce32d485bea540f5b5dabc502a2464cc); [Applied Sciences (Oct 2025) via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/6484bf7c72bc49cfb6c201a30c11dde1); [CEUR-WS Vol-4224 paper 2](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4224/Paper2.pdf)

### Inferences
- The reader's detector (YOLOv8n, COCO classes, phone/drone camera) is in the regime that every source above calls unreliable: nadir/oblique small objects, out-of-domain classes (COCO "truck" vs. a BMP), and decoys engineered to pass the "from a distance" test that a low-resolution detector performs. A state feed carries the detector's class label *without the pixels that would let a human catch the rounded corners*.
- Nothing in the record quantifies "share of false identifications" for visual AI on the Ukrainian front; evaluators will ask for it, and the project cannot cite a benchmark that answers it. The only honest answer is a measured confusion matrix on the team's own data.
- The pattern in Ukraine is that operators adopt automation where the alternative is nothing (jamming), not where it replaces a working video link. MinBand's natural pitch is therefore "blackout survival", not "video replacement".

### Gaps
- No published measurement of how often AI recognition misclassifies inflatable or thermal decoys (searched; none found).
- No per-class confusion data for YOLO-family models on military vehicle sets in the search results; VisDrone numbers are civilian traffic.
- Could not fetch the CSIS or Breaking Defense full texts; dates and quotes are from snippets.

## Key question 2: What legal/ROE/positive-identification requirements force a human to see imagery before engagement, and when is a track alone sufficient?

### Takeaway
LOAC itself only requires a reasonable belief "based on all information at their disposal at the time", but policy layers (ROE PID requirements, the CDE process built on imagery analysis, DoD 3000.09's "appropriate levels of human judgment", the ICRC's demand for predictability, Ukraine's man-in-the-loop policy and its video-proof kill-bonus system) in practice make imagery the currency of engagement decisions; tracks alone have been judged sufficient only in time-critical air/missile defence, where the two canonical track-only engagements (Vincennes 1988, Patriot 2003) are the textbook fratricide/misidentification cases.

### Cited Findings
- LOAC baseline: distinction is met when there is "a reasonable belief the object being attacked is a military target based on all information at their disposal at the time"; "for policy reasons nations may specify heightened identification requirements through Rules of Engagement (ROE) necessitating Positive Identification (PID) prior to attack"; PID may rest on "visual recognition, electronic signatures, non-cooperative target recognition techniques, identification friend or foe systems, or other identification techniques" — [JAPCC, Employment Considerations for 5th Generation Systems](https://japcc.org/?p=11036)
- Coalition OIF ROE card (2003, older): "Positive identification (PID) is required prior to engagement. PID is a reasonable certainty that the proposed target is a legitimate military target"; without PID, refer to higher commander — [HRW 2003 report ch. 11](https://www.hrw.org/reports/2003/usa1203/11.htm)
- US targeting practice: first step requires commanders to positively identify, "with reasonable certainty", that the object/person is a legitimate military target; for targeted killings this is "an intelligence-heavy task" — [McNeal, Lawfare guest post](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/guest-post-gregory-mcneal); a secondary claim that 70 % of collateral-damage incidents were due to failed PID appears in the same author's write-up and could not be verified against an official source — [gsmcneal.com](https://gsmcneal.com/the-u-s-practice-of-collateral-damage-estimation-and-mitigation/)
- CDE methodology: the CDE graphic "is developed using imagery analysis and data from CDE methodology", showing target outline/aimpoint and collateral effects radius — [Joint Staff CDE slides released under FOIA to ACLU](https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/document/foia/drone_dod_ACLU_DRONES_JOINT_STAFF_SLIDES_1-47_2.pdf); CJCSI 3160.01B (2015) is public via FOIA — [Joint Staff FOIA reading room](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/Joint_Staff/16-F-0427_Damage_Estimate_Methodology.pdf); the current 3160.01D (21 May 2021) is referenced in CJCSN 3162 and is not public — [CJCSN 3162](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Library/Notices/CJCSN%203162.pdf)
- DoD Directive 3000.09 (25 Jan 2023): autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems "will be designed to allow commanders and operators to exercise appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force"; critics note "appropriate level" is undefined and the senior review can be waived for "urgent military need" — [HRW, 14 Feb 2023](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/02/14/us-new-policy-autonomous-weapons-flawed); [Harvard IHRC](https://humanrightsclinic.law.harvard.edu/new-us-policy-on-autonomous-weapons-flawed/)
- ICRC position: unpredictable autonomous weapon systems "should be expressly ruled out"; ban systems designed/used so that effects "cannot be sufficiently understood, predicted and explained"; ban AWS that target persons; regulate the rest with limits on target types, duration, geographic scope and scale — [ICRC position on autonomous weapon systems](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-position-autonomous-weapon-systems); [ICRC recommendation](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/autonomous-weapons-icrc-recommends-new-rules)
- Ukraine: government, military and industry "emphasize the 'man-in-the-loop' (MITL) concept, ensuring that a human operator remains involved in decisions about target engagement" — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ukraines-future-vision-and-current-capabilities-waging-ai-enabled-autonomous-warfare)
- Ukraine's Army of Drones Bonus (Brave1): "Units upload video proof of confirmed kills to Delta"; data verified by Digital Ministry and State Special Communications Service teams; monthly score buys drones on Brave1 Market — [Euromaidan Press / Politico, 30 Apr 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/04/30/politico-ukrainian-drone-operators-get-game-styled-points-for-russian-kills-exchange-them-for-more-weapons/); [NV opinion](https://english.nv.ua/opinion/ukraine-gamifies-war-with-drone-kill-points-in-army-of-drones-bonus-program-50520526.html); 12 points per confirmed kill under current rules (Mar 2026) — [Euromaidan Press, 30 Mar 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/03/30/ukraine-may-award-drone-purchase-points-when-russian-soldiers-kill-themselves-under-drone-pressure/); launched Aug 2024 as a pilot, >420 units / ~90 % of UAV forces by mid-2025 — [DroneXL, 4 Nov 2025](https://dronexl.co/2025/11/04/ukraine-viral-call-of-duty-drone-system)
- Track-only engagement, case 1 (older, 1988): USS Vincennes crew believed Iran Air 655 was "descending and accelerating" in an attack profile; the Aegis system had recorded it climbing; Fogarty report: "continuous ascent in altitude from takeoff", squawking Mode III, "not on an attacking profile"; intercept at 8 nm and 13,500 ft; investigators invoked "scenario fulfillment" — [DTIC ADA259045](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA259045.pdf); [Foreign Policy, 17 Jan 2020](https://foreignpolicy.com/2020/01/17/accidental-shootdown-iran-united-states-ukraine/)
- Track-only engagement, case 2 (older, 2003): Defense Science Board summary found Patriot "had been given too much autonomy"; "combat identification systems that performed poorly and an inability to bring together data from the Patriot radar and other sensors"; "The operating protocol was largely automatic, and the operators were trained to trust the system's software" — [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=7463); [MIT Technology Review, June 2005](https://www.technologyreview.com/2005/06/01/230882/preventing-fratricide/); CNAS: automatic mode was "mostly an all-or-none operating mode" with few points for operator influence — [CNAS, Patriot Wars, Jan 2017](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/patriot-wars)

### Inferences
- A MinBand feed delivers exactly the information class (position, velocity, machine-assigned category) that was available to Vincennes and Patriot crews, and lacks the information class (pixels) that ROE PID practice and CDE depend on. Evaluators from NATO or US backgrounds will say "this is a cueing product, not a targeting product", and Ukrainian evaluators will add "and it cannot generate the video proof our bonus and accountability system runs on".
- "Operator confirm/reject" as a stretch feature inverts the problem: the operator is asked to confirm a label without seeing the evidence. Without an image chip, confirm/reject is a guess with a button.
- Where tracks alone *are* accepted (blue-force tracking, air defence), the data comes from cooperative or physically characterised sources (IFF, radar signatures), not from a COCO classifier; the project should not cite air-defence track-only engagement as precedent.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the DoD Law of War Manual text or NATO AJP-3.9 targeting doctrine; the JAPCC summary is the best available proxy.
- No public source on Ukrainian ROE specifics beyond the MITL statement and the video-proof bonus rule.
- Current CJCSI 3160.01D imagery requirements are classified; only the process description is public.

## Key question 3: Prior art that stalled: DIS dead reckoning outside simulation, Blue Force Tracker latency, Link 16 correlation, JSTARS GMTI vs FMV, Nett Warrior; what were the lessons?

### Takeaway
Each precursor shipped and is still used, but each generated a documented pathology that a predicted-state feed inherits: DIS thresholds are hard to tune and can "introduce more inconsistency than [they avoid]"; BFT's multi-minute latency for other units and a 10-second experimental delay measurably increased friendly engagements; Link 16 dual tracks "undermine the confidence of human operators"; JSTARS GMTI "can't identify a vehicle type or tell whether it is friendly, hostile, or a bystander" and is used to cue FMV, not replace it; Nett Warrior's radio layer, not its app, failed operational test.

### Cited Findings
**DIS dead reckoning (simulation origin)**
- DIS specifies a heartbeat timer "fixed at 5 seconds" and separate position (TH_pos) and orientation (TH_or) thresholds; exceeding them triggers extra updates — [arXiv 1008.3758, survey of DIS dead-reckoning QoS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.3758)
- Threshold-based DR, "unless carefully tuned to application and network characteristics, ... can introduce more inconsistency than it avoids"; the standard distance threshold may be the wrong metric; a path-curvature model is proposed instead — [Salford repository paper](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1499305)
- Fixed thresholds "cannot adequately handle the dynamic relationships between moving entities"; the right threshold depends on inter-entity distance — [Auto-adaptive multi-threshold DR (NTU/UTM record)](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/ntu-10356%2F2534); [academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/795560/An_auto_adaptive_dead_reckoning_algorithm_for_distributed_interactive_simulation)
- DR "ignores available contextual and a priori information" and "sacrifices remote predictive accuracy in lieu of low computational complexity" — [Maynooth neuro-reckoning paper](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/1446/1/TWUsing_Neural-Networks.pdf); perception-based threshold setting sends updates only when actual behaviour differs from prediction by a set amount — [Maynooth CGAMES paper](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/1282/1/TWCGAMES_final_AK.pdf)
- Without DR a remote entity looks static then jumps, and shrinking the update interval "quickly exhausts available network bandwidth" with many entities — [NUS CS4344 notes](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~cs4344/0607s1/deadreckoning.pdf)

**Blue Force Tracker (BFT/FBCB2/JBC-P)**
- Iraq-era (older, 2004): own position updated on the digital map within 10–15 s, but other friendly positions "took several minutes" — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, "Better Blue Force Tracking"](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0604blue/)
- Human-factors study (2012–13): "current BFT systems can have a five-minute latency for ground assets"; a two-second delay can mean 2–3 m of position error for moving soldiers — [ResearchGate, "Blue Force Tracking: Effects of Spatial Error on Soldier Performance"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258014391_Blue_Force_Tracking_Effects_of_Spatial_Error_on_Soldier_Performance)
- Adding a 10-second latency to BFT position updates made participants "significantly more likely to engage friendly targets"; BFT's effectiveness "can be dramatically reduced when it does not provide real-time data" — [PubMed 23516795, "Impact of blue force tracking on combat identification judgments"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23516795/)
- BFT "depends on a communication network to gather data" (listed as a significant weakness) — [Nagy, Repüléstudományi Közlemények 2013](https://www.repulestudomany.hu/folyoirat/2013_1/2013-1-13-Nagy_Janos.pdf)
- Soldier feedback (2011, anecdotal): JBC-P has "noticeably less latency than FBCB2" — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/70634/soldier_feedback_shaping_next_generation_blue_force_tracking_handhelds); Viasat BFT2 "refreshes position data faster" (vendor claim) — [Viasat](https://www.viasat.com/government/missions/blue-force-tracking/)

**Link 16 / tactical data links**
- A "dual track" is "the erroneous representation of a single target by two tracks"; it arises from over-tight correlation rules; its main cost is that dual tracks "tend to undermine the confidence of human operators in the air picture" — [JHU APL Technical Digest, Bath, V23 N2-3](https://secwww.jhuapl.edu/techdigest/Content/techdigest/pdf/V23-N2-3/23-02-Bath.pdf)
- Navy interoperability work: "new problems proliferated with interactions among multiple data links and varying interpretations of this correlation standard" — [CNA, Making Weapons Work as One](https://cna.org/about-us/research/case-studies/making-weapons-work-as-one)
- Link 16 J3.6 sensor-track messages carry target track data plus a Track Quality (TQ) field; TQ-to-covariance mapping is non-trivial enough to be patented — [US 8,052,054](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8052054)

**JSTARS GMTI vs FMV**
- GMTI "gives approximate vehicle counts, locations, speed, and heading, but can't identify a vehicle type or tell whether it is friendly, hostile, or a bystander. Commanders therefore often crosscheck JSTARS data against other sources" — [Wikipedia, E-8 Joint STARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_E-8_Joint_STARS)
- JSTARS's value is cueing: it "will tell you that there's unusual movement in specific places and hand off that information for closer inspection by Predator surveillance drones or manned aircraft"; ground clutter makes GMTI harder than air tracking (advocacy piece) — [Lexington Institute issue brief](https://www.lexingtoninstitute.org/?p=595)
- "False/branch track rate" is listed as a factor driving association error in GMTI tracking — [DTIC ADA432962](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA432962.pdf); ground movers can be masked by the clutter notch (Doppler blinding) — [SPIE 4728, EM applied to GMTI convoy tracking](https://neurophotonics.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/4728/0000/Expectation-maximization-applied-to-GMTI-convoy-tracking/10.1117/12.478525.full)

**Nett Warrior (older, 2011–2015)**
- DOT&E: Nett Warrior "operationally effective in mounted formations at the platoon and troop levels"; full-rate production deferred pending interoperability with the Manpack radio — [DOT&E FY2015 Nett Warrior](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2015/army/2015nettwarrior.pdf); earlier interoperability problems with JBC-P — [DOT&E FY2013](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2013/army/2013nettwarrior.pdf)
- Rifleman Radio "demonstrated numerous suitability issues that contributed to Soldiers concluding that this radio was not yet acceptable for combat in its current Nett Warrior configuration" — [Breaking Defense, Jan 2014](https://breakingdefense.com/2014/01/army-radios-get-low-marks-from-dote/)

### Inferences
- Lesson from DIS: the threshold is the product. A hackathon demo with one hand-tuned threshold will be asked "what happens when two entities approach each other / turn / stop behind cover?"; the literature says fixed thresholds fail precisely there, and MinBand's "widen thresholds under a byte budget" is the regime in which the Salford result (more inconsistency than it avoids) bites.
- Lesson from BFT: latency of other people's tracks is the killer, and 10 s is already enough to change engagement decisions in a lab. A predicted picture that hides a 10-second-old update behind a smoothly moving icon is worse than a visibly stale icon.
- Lesson from Link 16 and GMTI: fused track pictures lose operator confidence through duplicate/ghost tracks, and GMTI survived only as a cue for imagery. A MinBand feed from multiple phones viewing the same person will create exactly the dual-track problem unless it has cross-sensor correlation, which is not in the current design.
- Lesson from Nett Warrior: the app is rarely the failure; the radio and its interoperability are. "Radio-neutral" is a claim that will be tested by asking for a real radio and a real loss/latency profile.

### Gaps
- No GAO report on BFT latency or fratricide surfaced; the GAO/DOT&E angle is covered only by the Nett Warrior and Rifleman Radio documents.
- Could not read the full JHU APL or CNA texts; no numeric dual-track rates found.
- No source stating commanders' *preference* for FMV over GMTI in those words; the cueing relationship is documented, the preference is inferred.
- IEEE 1278.1 default threshold values were not retrievable (paywalled); only the 5 s heartbeat is sourced.

## Key question 4: Integrity: how can a predicted-state feed be spoofed, replayed or poisoned; how do existing tactical links authenticate tracks; what do evaluators expect from a hackathon-grade crypto story?

### Takeaway
Unauthenticated state feeds (ADS-B is the canonical example) are trivially injectable with an SDR, and the Ukrainian Delta system has already been targeted by credential-stealing phishing; production links authenticate at the transport layer (Link 16 MSEC/TSEC, TAK mutual TLS) and, where per-message signing exists (MAVLink 2), the replay defence depends on a persisted monotonic timestamp that fails in practice when batteries are pulled; evaluators will expect the hackathon to name its threat model, show replay/timestamp handling and key provisioning, and will laugh at home-rolled ciphers, static shared keys with no rotation, or "encrypted" claims without integrity.

### Cited Findings
- ADS-B "does not use any authentication methods in its messages, and so infusion can be performed by commercially available devices"; attacks include ghost-aircraft injection by a ground attacker and replay of recorded traffic; attacks are "easy and practically feasible to launch for a moderately sophisticated attacker with a SDR"; a PHY-layer DNN detector reached 99.34 % detection with 0.43 % false alarms (research result) — [arXiv 1904.09969](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09969); injected messages can target the UAV itself or the ground command centre ("Command Center Ghost Injection") — [arXiv 2504.07358 survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07358)
- Proof of concept: spoofed ADS-B generated with GNU Radio + USRP and "inserted on the display of a commercial ADS-B receiver" — [AFIT thesis (CORE record)](https://api.core.ac.uk/oai/oai:scholar.afit.edu:etd-2135); Costin & Francillon, Black Hat USA 2012 "Ghost in the Air(Traffic)" is the foundational demonstration (older) — [Simula T-SAR 2019 slides](https://web-backend.simula.no/sites/default/files/publications/files/t-sar_-_2019.pdf)
- A mixed-reality framework exists specifically for T&E of UAVs against false-data-injection attacks — [arXiv 2407.09342, MIXED-SENSE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09342)
- Delta (Ukraine) phishing, Dec 2022: users received emails from a compromised MoD account with fake warnings to "update root certificates in the Delta software", linking to a fraudulent domain (delta-storages.com) hosting a page mimicking Delta's login; payloads FateGrab and StealDeal (info-stealers); cluster UAC-0142 — [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2022/12/ukraines-delta-military-system-users.html); [Infosecurity Magazine](https://infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ukraines-delta-military-intel); Ukraine MoD (Nov 2022) denied any intrusion and called hack claims a Russian information operation — [LB.ua](https://en.lb.ua/news/2022/11/01/17684_ukrainian_defence_ministry_russia.html)
- MAVLink 2 signing: signature is the first 48 bits of SHA-256 over the packet plus secret key; 48-bit timestamp in 10 µs units since 1 Jan 2015 must "monotonically increase for every message on a particular link", per (SystemID, ComponentID, LinkID); provides "authentication and integrity only" (no confidentiality) — [MAVLink message signing guide](https://mavlink.io/en/guide/message_signing.html); "it is the responsibility of each MAVLink system to store and restore the timestamp into persistent storage (this is critical for the security of the signing system)" — [mavgen C signing docs](https://mavlink.io/en/mavgen_c/message_signing_c.html); PX4: since most vehicles are powered off by pulling the battery, "the on-disk timestamp will typically remain at the value from the last key provisioning on reboot", and anyone with SD-card access can read/modify the key file — [PX4 message signing](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/mavlink/message_signing.html)
- Link 16: both message and transmission are encrypted; MSEC via the JTIDS/MIDS crypto, TSEC via the same or a second variable controlling the waveform; MIDS carries MSEC/TSEC on an embedded board; keys via CCI/KMI — [Tonex Link 16 overview](https://www.tonex.com/?p=14752); waveform hops over 51 bins at ~77,000 hops/s — [sigidwiki](https://sigidwiki.com/wiki/Link_16); 2015 JCS mandate on Link 16 COMSEC keying for crypto-modernised terminals — [Military Aerospace](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/trusted-computing/article/14207046/secure-data-cryptography-link-16)
- TAK: TAK Server authenticates clients by X.509 mutual TLS on the TLS port (conventionally 8089, auth="x509" in CoreConfig.xml) — [pytak configuration docs](https://pytak.readthedocs.io/en/latest/configuration/); FreeTAKServer likewise uses mutual TLS with CRL checking — [FreeTAKServer SSL service docs](https://www.mintlify.com/FreeTAKTeam/FreeTakServer/api/cot/ssl-service); no evidence found of per-message CoT signatures; protection is the TLS session
- Positions from GNSS are themselves an attack surface: ADS-B coasting AD cites misleading data affecting TCAS (see Q5); ARKit-derived positions were not researched (budget exhausted)

### Inferences
- A MinBand delta is a small, highly structured, predictable message ("entity 7, +0.3 m, class person"). That is the easiest possible target for replay and for bit-flipping if integrity is a CRC rather than a MAC. Replaying yesterday's keyframes during a blackout would be indistinguishable from "loss repaired by resending current state" unless every message carries a signed monotonic counter *and* the receiver persists the high-water mark.
- "Compact authenticated encryption" (e.g. an AEAD with a short tag) is defensible, but evaluators will ask: tag length vs. forgery probability per message at 10 msg/s; nonce management across reboots; key provisioning; what happens when the edge device is captured (device-level keys, revocation). MAVLink's documented failure (timestamp resets on battery pull) is exactly the hackathon-grade mistake to pre-empt.
- Decoys create *ghost entities* at the semantic layer regardless of crypto: an inflatable tank produces an authentic, signed, correct-by-construction "tank" track. Integrity protects the channel, not the truth. The right framing is "authenticated provenance plus retrievable evidence", not "secure feed".
- Dropped-update ambiguity: under MinBand's rule, silence means "prediction is still right". An adversary who can jam selectively (or a radio that drops asymmetrically) converts "entity stopped/vanished" into "entity continues along its predicted path". Keyframes every 2 s bound this only if the receiver treats a missed keyframe as a staleness event and displays it.

### Gaps
- No source found on per-message CoT signing in official TAK Server; the mutual-TLS finding comes from third-party client/server docs.
- Could not confirm the Link 16 crypto device designations (KGV-8 vs KGV-135); one LLM-generated page conflicts and was discarded.
- No search completed on GNSS spoofing prevalence (2024–2026) or ARKit outdoor drift; both are obvious objections to "positions come from ARKit on a phone".
- No documented case of a *tactical track feed* (as opposed to ADS-B/AIS) being spoofed in Ukraine.

## Key question 5: Human factors: does a smooth, predicted picture create false confidence during blackouts; stale-track accidents; display conventions?

### Takeaway
The accident record (Patriot 2003: "unwarranted trust in automation", operators "trained to trust the system's software"; Vincennes 1988: crew believed a climbing airliner was descending; ADS-B: five documented cases of ATC seeing coasted stale data "with no warning to the crew") plus the experimental BFT result (10 s latency raised friendly engagements) says yes: a continuously moving icon with no visible age/uncertainty is the hazard, and the existing conventions all push the other way (CoT carries a mandatory `stale` time; ADS-B/TCAS regulators treat coasting as a hazard requiring crew warning).

### Cited Findings
- Army investigators found "automation bias" was a factor in the 2003 Patriot fratricides; the ARL team focused on "lack of vigilance" and "unwarranted trust in automation" — [Hoffman & Hawley, Myths of Automation Part 2](https://jeffreymbradshaw.net/publications/Hoffman-Hawley-54.%20Myths%20of%20Automation%20Part%202.pdf); Patriot operational tests "going back to the 1980s indicated that the weapon system's automated engagement logic was subject to track misclassification problems", and these were not fixed in software nor carried into training — [GovInfo LPS66633](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D-PURL-LPS66633/pdf/GOVPUB-D-PURL-LPS66633.pdf); "The operating protocol was largely automatic, and the operators were trained to trust the system's software" (DSB) — [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=7463); the theme is revisited as "algorithmic fratricide" in Dec 2025 — [Small Wars Journal, 25 Dec 2025](https://smallwarsjournal.com/2025/12/25/algorithmic-fratricide/)
- Vincennes: a crew member reported the aircraft "descending and accelerating"; the ship's own system recorded it ascending; "scenario fulfillment" cited — [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2020/01/17/accidental-shootdown-iran-united-states-ukraine/); [DTIC ADA259045](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA259045.pdf)
- 10-second BFT latency → significantly more friendly engagements — [PubMed 23516795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23516795/)
- FAA airworthiness directive (2017) prompted by "five instances of air traffic control observing coasting (extrapolated stale data) of automatic dependent surveillance-broadcast data (position/velocity data)"; "the extrapolation of the data occurs with no warning to the crew"; misleading altitude data "can adversely affect TCAS and possibly lead to mid-air collision due to an incorrect initial resolution advisory" — [Federal Register, 15 Nov 2017](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2017-11-15/pdf/2017-24066.pdf); [EASA mirror of AD 2017-22-14](https://ad.easa.europa.eu/blob/2017-22-14.pdf/AD_US-2017-22-14_1)
- DO-317B defines ADS-B In traffic symbology (per a Honeywell-style patent reference); the coasting/drop rules themselves were not retrievable — [US 10,991,256](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10991256)
- CoT: every event carries `time`, `start` and a required `stale` datetime marking when the event "should be treated as expired"; a third-party doc states a default of 60 s after generation (its own example uses 1 day, so treat the default as unverified) — [FreeTAKServer CoT concepts](https://www.mintlify.com/FreeTAKTeam/FreeTakServer/concepts/cot-messages); schema is maintained by the MITRE CoT program office (AFLCMC, Hanscom AFB) — [MITRE FY14 CoT user-group AAR](https://www.mitre.org/news-insights/publication/after-action-report-cursor-target-fy14-international-user-group-meeting); [DTIC AD1112602](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1112602.pdf)
- Dual tracks "undermine the confidence of human operators in the air picture" — [JHU APL, Bath](https://secwww.jhuapl.edu/techdigest/Content/techdigest/pdf/V23-N2-3/23-02-Bath.pdf)

### Inferences
- MinBand's core mechanism (receiver keeps predicting; silence is consent) is the same mechanism the FAA treated as a hazard: extrapolation "with no warning to the crew". The demo must visibly age tracks (time since last delta, time since last keyframe, predicted-error ellipse growing with the active threshold) or evaluators will cite the AD.
- The DIS keyframe-every-2 s habit does not transfer: in simulation a heartbeat is bookkeeping; on a battlefield it is the only evidence the sensor is alive, and its absence must change the display state, not just the error bound.
- "Works through blackouts" is a human-factors claim, not a networking claim; the honest statement is "degrades visibly through blackouts". Evaluators will ask what the operator sees at t+5 s, t+30 s, t+5 min of silence, and whether the twin ever deletes entities.
- CoT already gives MinBand a free convention: emit `stale` = now + f(threshold, class speed), and let TAK grey/drop the icon. Not doing so will be read as not knowing the ecosystem.

### Gaps
- No TCAS II / DO-185B coast-timing numbers retrieved.
- No friendly-fire case explicitly attributed to BFT stale data in the search results (the BFT evidence is experimental; the Patriot/Vincennes cases involve radar tracks, not BFT).
- The official MITRE CoT `stale` semantics could not be read (DTIC blocked); only the third-party description is cited.

## Key question 6: What evaluation questions and metrics do DARPA, NATO and Ukrainian evaluators actually use for ISR data reduction and edge AI; what does a convincing utility test look like?

### Takeaway
DARPA's current topic on exactly this problem (DPA26BZ05-DV019 "Semantically-Aware ISR", closed 23 Sep 2026) sets threshold = 90 % (10×) reduction versus full-frame mission video "with preserved mission utility", objective 95–99 %, and demands traceable, *object-agnostic* selection of regions/events with real-time embedded, onboard-only execution; tracking and ATR communities score on Pd/false-alarm density/confusion matrix and HOTA/MOTA/IDF1 with ID-switch counts; Ukraine's operational "metric" is video proof uploaded to Delta. A convincing utility test therefore compares operator task performance on MinBand vs. a thumbnail/low-rate-video baseline at equal bytes, not bytes alone.

### Cited Findings
- DV019 Phase II prototypes "shall demonstrate threshold = 90 percent (10X) reduction versus full-frame mission-video baselines with preserved mission utility, with objective = 95-99 percent reduction in mission-suitable scenes, traceable and object-agnostic selection of regions and events, real-time embedded execution" — [Funding Landscape listing of DPA26BZ05-DV019](https://fundinglandscape.com/opportunity/57d6e386-805c-4808-b122-d293cda973d7); traceability means "an operator must be able to audit why any given piece of video was selected"; "No cloud processing. No continuous high-bandwidth reachback. Whatever decides what to transmit has to run on the aircraft" — [BWCO Consulting summary](https://www.bwcoconsulting.com/fod/darpa-sbir-dv019-semantically-aware-isr); [grantedai summary (mentions 2 W power budget in its title)](https://grantedai.com/blog/darpa-dv019-semantically-aware-isr-1-5-million-direct-to-phase-ii-september-23-2026-deadline-99-percent-video-reduction-2-watt-power-budget-strategy)
- DV019 is Direct-to-Phase-II only; base up to $1,500,000 over 18 months, option $500,000/6 months; opened 26 Aug 2026, closed 23 Sep 2026 12:00 ET; proposers "shall provide documentation that Phase I-equivalent feasibility has been achieved" via five specified evidence elements — [DARPA FAQ for DPA26BZ05-DV019 (Sep 2026)](https://www.darpa.mil/sites/default/files/attachment/2026-09/faq-dpa26bz05-dv019.pdf); [Govly listing](https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16915400)
- ATR performance "is generally measured in terms of a probability of detection, a false alarm rate, and a 'confusion matrix'" — [DTIC ADA357723](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA357723.pdf); for search problems the FROC curve is used because false alarms are unconstrained; false-alarm density = false detections / area surveyed — [SPIE 5094 evaluation paper](https://medicalimaging.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/5094/0000/Evaluation-of-automated-target-detection-using-image-fusion/10.1117/12.486816.full); an older human-factors study found an aided-ATR has obvious tactical value only at correct-detection ≥ 0.7 with ≤ 0.167 false alarms per square degree — [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/works/24869871)
- MOT metrics: MOTA = 1 − Σ(FN + FP + IDSW)/ΣGT, detection-dominated and blind to *when* an ID switch happens; IDF1 = 2·IDTP/(2·IDTP + IDFP + IDFN) from a global track matching, association-sensitive; HOTA = geometric mean of DetA and AssA averaged over localisation thresholds — [SportsLabKit metrics guide](https://sportslabkit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/notebooks/02_user_guide/03_evaluation_metrics.html); [arXiv 2506.13457 MOT survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13457); [Roboflow trackers benchmarks](https://trackers.roboflow.com/2.1.0/learn/benchmarks)
- Link 16 J3.6 tracks carry an explicit Track Quality field — [US 8,052,054](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8052054); GMTI evaluation uses false/branch track rate — [DTIC ADA432962](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA432962.pdf)
- Ukraine: video proof of confirmed kills uploaded to Delta, verified by Digital Ministry / SSSCIP teams — [Euromaidan Press, 30 Apr 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/04/30/politico-ukrainian-drone-operators-get-game-styled-points-for-russian-kills-exchange-them-for-more-weapons/)
- Tracker survey for airborne UAV-swarm tracking (Sep 2026) exists as a recent benchmark reference — [arXiv 2609.15171, EECTracker](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15171)

### Inferences
- The hard questions, in the order evaluators ask them: (1) What is the baseline? DARPA's baseline is full-frame mission video; the reduction number is meaningless without stating resolution/frame rate/codec of the baseline. (2) What is "mission utility" and who measured it? DARPA wants a task-level measure; "the twin matched ground truth within X m" is not it. (3) Traceability: can the operator see *why* an entity exists? A class label and a confidence are not an audit trail; an image chip is. (4) Object-agnostic: DARPA explicitly does not want a fixed class list; a COCO "person/car" detector fails that criterion by construction. (5) False track rate and track continuity: how many ghost entities per hour per feed; how often does an entity ID swap or fragment; what are MOTA/HOTA/IDF1 of the *received* twin versus the *edge* tracker versus ground truth. (6) Time-to-detect and time-to-display: latency from first frame containing the object to its appearance in the twin, under 0 %, 10 %, 20 % loss and a 30 s blackout. (7) PID: what fraction of twin entities could an operator positively identify from what the twin gives them (answer today: none).
- A convincing utility test: same video, same byte budget (e.g. 400 B/s), three conditions (MinBand twin; a 100–200 B thumbnail every N s; a 2 kbit/s keyframe-only H.265 stream if the budget allows), operators perform a task (count, classify, decide engage/hold, detect the decoy), score task accuracy, time, and confidence calibration. Without the decoy condition, the test will be read as rigged.
- Reporting MOTA alone will be challenged (detection-dominated); report HOTA with DetA/AssA and raw ID-switch counts, plus false-track-per-hour.

## Key question 7: The strongest "why not just…" objections, with real numbers for the alternatives

### Takeaway
At MinBand's stated 60–800 B/s per feed (0.5–6.4 kbit/s), no video codec is a competitor, so "just use 64 kbit/s H.265" is really the objection "does a link that only carries 60–800 B/s exist, and if the link carries 8 kB/s why not send pictures?"; the live competitors at MinBand's rate are a compressed CoT PLI (~190 B) over LoRa-class links and AI-compressed thumbnails (~100–200 B), while stock Meshtastic (5–20 B/s) is *below* MinBand's own minimum rate, which undercuts "radio-neutral".

### Cited Findings
- Stock Meshtastic: packet payload 51–222 bytes depending on spreading factor; standard configuration throughput "about 5–20 bytes per second"; 1 % duty cycle ≈ 36 s of airtime per hour; EU 868 MHz 10 % duty cycle ≈ 6 s per minute; raw LoRa short bursts ≈ 0.1 kB/s; "even a single bit error can corrupt the image" so images need ack/retransmit, which the community considers out of scope — [Meshtastic Discourse thread](https://meshtastic.discourse.group/t/how-can-i-send-multimedia-files-through-meshtastic/2867)
- ATAK Forwarder plugin (third-party, Meshtastic 2.2.16 era): after libcotshrink compression a PLI ≈ 190 bytes, a simple shape ≈ 200 bytes, group chat ≈ 400 bytes; throughput ≈ 5 map markers or PLI per minute (cited from search snippet; page not opened per scope rules) — [paulmandal/atak-forwarder README](https://github.com/paulmandal/atak-forwarder)
- Official Meshtastic TAK path (firmware ≥ 2.8.0): ATAK_PLUGIN_V2 port carries TAKPacketV2 protobuf compressed with a TAKPacket-SDK zstd dictionary, with a `maxWireBytes` limit and fallback dictionaries; the page gives no resulting PLI byte count — [Meshtastic TAK protocol docs](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/apple/developer/tak-protocol/); V2 uses compact compressed encoding rather than raw CoT XML — [SDR Store guide](https://www.sdrstore.eu/meshtastic-tak-hardware-guide-search-rescue-field-teams/)
- AI-compressed images over MeshCore: ~100–200 bytes per image, reconstructed as "a similar image" (approximation, not original pixels) — [Seeed Studio MeshCore AI image transmission](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/meshcore_ai_image_transmission_l2/)
- Raw LoRa image transfer (ESP32, stop-and-wait): ~2 s per message, a 6,810-byte image in about a minute — [Latinoware paper (SBC)](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/latinoware/article/download/22964/22791)
- H.265 on tactical radios (2016, older): an H.265 service for DIL networks needed on average 39 % less bandwidth than the STANAG 4609-recommended H.264 service at similar perceptual quality, with up to +2.1 dB PSNR from unequal error protection — [Aston/UWS, "Reliable full motion video services in disadvantaged tactical radio networks"](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/reliable-full-motion-video-services-in-disadvantaged-tactical-rad/); H.265 configurations reached colour video "at bandwidths compatible with transmission using existing IP enabled tactical radio networks" (no figures in snippet) — [UWS portal](https://research-portal.uws.ac.uk/en/publications/enhancing-visual-communications-capabilities-in-tactical-networks/)
- 2019 UAV encoder test: at reduced bit rate, H.264 "is considered good enough for close targets but too fuzzy for long-range operation"; vendor says H.265 imagery was 50 % clearer (vendor test) — [Unmanned Systems Technology, Sep 2019](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2019/09/tactical-h-265-video-encoder-for-uavs-tested/); legacy codecs "struggle to maintain critical visual detail at the low single-digit Mbps" of UAV downlinks (vendor marketing) — [V-Nova FlyStream case](https://v-nova.com/case/flystream/)
- Acoustic alternative for the "person/vehicle present" question: Zvook, 1.6 % false positives (CSIS) — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ukraines-future-vision-and-current-capabilities-waging-ai-enabled-autonomous-warfare)

### Inferences (arithmetic is mine, from the cited numbers)
- 64 kbit/s = 8,000 B/s, i.e. 10–130× MinBand's 60–800 B/s. If the radio can carry 8 kB/s, an evaluator will ask why not send a 320×240 H.265 stream or one ~7 kB JPEG per second, each of which supports PID; MinBand must show the link really is in the sub-kbit/s class, or that the bytes saved buy something (more feeds, lower emission duty cycle, longer range).
- At 800 B/s a 6.8 kB image takes ~8.5 s; at 60 B/s it takes ~113 s. A 150 B AI thumbnail fits every 0.2–2.5 s at MinBand rates. So "image chips on demand" is not a stretch goal; at the project's own rates it is the only thing that makes confirm/reject meaningful, and a competitor could ship "thumbnail every 2 s plus a dead-reckoned icon" with less novelty and more utility.
- Stock Meshtastic (5–20 B/s) cannot carry one MinBand feed at its 60 B/s floor; the ATAK-forwarder figure (~190 B per PLI at ~5/min ≈ 16 B/s) is the realistic LoRa budget. "Radio-neutral" needs a per-radio table: LoRa (tens of B/s, duty-cycle limited), tactical UHF/VHF data (hundreds of B/s to a few kB/s), MANET (tens of kB/s and up, where video returns).
- The "why not CoT + compression" objection is strong: a compressed CoT PLI is ~190 B, a CoT event already has `stale`, `how` (source/quality) and a type taxonomy, and TAK renders it. MinBand's delta scheme must beat ~190 B × update rate under equal loss, and must explain why its twin is not simply CoT with dead reckoning and a byte budget.
- The "why not JPEG every N seconds" objection is strongest for stationary scenes, which is exactly where dead reckoning saves the most bytes; the honest comparison plots bytes vs. operator task accuracy across scene motion, not a single rate.
