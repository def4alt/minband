## KQ1. Swarm programs and products: what drones exchange with each other and with C2, over which links, and how they cope with link loss

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the fielded "swarms" are small (typically 3, up to 8, tested to 25 drones in Ukraine; 10–20 in Western demos) and the public record describes *what they do* when the link drops (local autonomy: continue to a designated target, re-identify it, re-assign roles) far better than *what bytes they exchange*. No vendor or program published inter-drone or drone-to-C2 bandwidth figures; the only hard numbers are from radio vendors (Silvus) and from Russian relay-chain practice (2–3 hops max). Replicator has delivered "hundreds" of drones and is transitioning to the services; its promised swarm software remains unverified in public.

### Inferences
- Every fielded or near-fielded swarm (Swarmer, Nemyx, HX-2, Hivemind) is designed around the assumption that the C2 link *will* drop: the operator transmits a designation (zone, pixel-lock, target) and the swarm then needs only intermittent sync. That is architecturally the same "ground truth is on the edge, C2 gets a sparse summary" shape that a delta-sync link layer serves.
- Russian relay chains (≤3 hops, explicit latency trade-off) are the best public evidence that practitioners cap hop count for control traffic; low-rate track summaries tolerate more hops than video/manual control.

### Gaps
- No program or vendor published inter-drone message content, rates or bytes; "drone-to-drone communication" (Nemyx), "peer-to-peer links" (Hivemind) are unquantified claims.
- Could not verify Replicator 2026 delivery totals beyond "hundreds" (Aug 2025).
- Swarmer's radio/mesh (vendor, frequencies, rates) was not found before the search budget ran out.

## KQ2. Mesh scaling: how throughput collapses with nodes and hops, and practical node counts

### Takeaway
Single-radio, half-duplex relays roughly halve usable throughput per hop (vendor-neutral rule; Cisco's worked example 14 → 9 → 4 Mbps over three hops), barrage-relay (TrellisWare-style) flooding caps broadcast throughput at W/3 of link rate regardless of size, and Silvus's 559-node flat MANET consumed <35 % of airtime for all-to-all position reports while leaving ~5.5 Mbps. Fielded practice in Ukraine (Russian side) is 2–3 relay hops.

### Cited Findings
- **Silvus 559-node demo (Oct 2022, older).** A flat network of 559 StreamCaster Mini 4210 radios on one narrowband channel, "every node sending position data to every other node"; PLI "consumed less than 35 % of total network airtime, leaving about 5.5 Mbps for voice, video, and other IP traffic"; "latency ... averaged less than 45 milliseconds under load"; proprietary MN-MIMO waveform. Hop count not stated — [Unmanned Systems Technology](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2022/10/large-scale-tactical-mesh-radio-network-demonstrated/); [Military Embedded Systems](https://militaryembedded.com/comms/radio/silvus-pushes-the-limits-of-manet-scalability-and-capacity-with-559-node-network-demonstration); [Silvus press release PDF](https://silvustechnologies.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/559-Node-Press-Release-FINAL.pdf)
- Silvus single-link peak: "up to 100 Mbps" (SL5200 OEM module; claim) — [Commercial UAV News](https://www.commercialuavnews.com/silvus-technologies-unveils-streamcaster-lite-5200-ultra-low-swap-oem-module-delivering-powerful-manet-radio-performance-for-leading-edge-unmanned-systems)
- **Per-hop halving.** Cisco mesh guidance: "throughput is approximately halved over every hop", recommends ≤3–4 backhaul hops; worked example ≈14 Mbps first hop, 9 Mbps second, 4 Mbps third — [Cisco Community thread](https://community.cisco.com/t5/wireless/client-hops-to-gateway-cut-by-50-per-hop-is-this-true/td-p/3867599); mechanism (a relay cannot receive and transmit simultaneously) — [Meshmerize](https://meshmerize.net/radio-hopping/); a patent states "throughput drops exponentially with the number of daisy chained hops" — [USPTO 10306481](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10306481)
- **Barrage relay (TrellisWare-associated patents).** Broadcast throughput "W/3 ... with W as the capacity of a single point-to-point link" via spatial pipelining; more generally W/M where "M must be at least 3" when network size is unknown and M=4 for "highly mobile" topologies; TTL example of 8 hops; "each node may relay a given message only once"; identical copies are combined at the PHY — [USPTO 10477543](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10477543); [USPTO 9054822](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9054822); [US8873391](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8873391)
- Doodle Labs Mesh Rider: "up to 100 Mbps" (claim), no per-hop table found — [Mouser/Doodle Labs](https://www.mouser.vn/new/doodle-labs/doodle-labs-rm-5800-smart-radios); Persistent MPU5 / Wave Relay multi-hop forwarding, no per-hop figures — [US Army on MPU5 (2019)](https://www.army.mil/article/222056/) (older)
- **Fielded hop counts.** Russian Shahed/Gerbera mesh chains use "no more than two or three retranslations" because each hop costs speed and adds latency — [Hvylya](https://amp.en.hvylya.net/news/2414-shahed-drones-with-mesh-modems-reach-kyiv-and-odesa-flash-maps-the-strike-zone); [dev.ua](https://dev.ua/en/news/rosiiany-zbilshyly-dalnist-keruvannia-dronamy-1788522323)

### Inferences
- Combining the rules: a per-feed budget of ~60–800 B/s (0.5–6.4 kbit/s) is 3–4 orders of magnitude below a single MANET link, so hop-halving is not the binding constraint for track summaries; airtime is. Silvus's figure (559 nodes of PLI at <35 % airtime) implies a flat mesh can carry hundreds of low-rate position feeds if each feed stays in the kbit/s class — but the PLI interval in that demo was not retrievable, so this cannot be converted into "bytes per node per second".
- For video-class feeds the same mesh supports only a handful of hops/nodes (halving rule), which is why practitioners cap at 2–3 hops.

### Gaps
- Silvus PLI update interval and channel width for the 559-node demo (search budget exhausted before follow-up).
- No published Doodle Labs / Persistent / TrellisWare throughput-vs-hops tables.
- No Ukrainian-side statement of practical mesh node counts per link was found.

## KQ3. Low-cost sensor networks as track producers (acoustic, ground, C-UAS radar) and how interceptors get tracks

### Takeaway
Ukraine's Sky Fortress acoustic network is ~9,500 (2024) to ~14,000 (NATO doc, Jan 2026) smartphone-class sensors at $300–1,000 each; Zvook sensors report azimuth and direction of movement over Ethernet/fiber/LTE/LoRa to a map UI. Interceptor crews (e.g. Wild Hornets Sting) still fly by camera/VR with AI terminal guidance; 2026 reporting says networked systems are only *beginning* to share tracks between interceptor crews, and crews' stated top need is "better radar", not more bandwidth.

### Inferences
- An acoustic node's product is a *bearing + class + timestamp* at a few Hz at most (a per-sensor azimuth), not a full position track; position comes from fusing several sensors centrally. That is a lower-dimensional feed than a camera detector's (x, y, v, class) and shows that national-scale sensor networks already run on cellular/LTE/LoRa backhaul at smartphone cost.
- Interceptor guidance today is operator-in-VR plus terminal AI; the track feed that cues the crew comes from radar/acoustic networks through human relays and (newly, 2026) networked handoff. The missing piece practitioners name is sensor quality (radar), not link capacity.

## KQ4. How common operating pictures ingest drone-derived tracks; formats; dedup; sensor-to-shooter timing

### Takeaway
Delta ingests drone video, radar, satellite and chatbot inputs and (per NYT via NV) cut the detect-to-strike chain from ~20 min to 3–5 min; its AI detector claims ~70 % of visible equipment at ~2.2 s per target. TAK/CoT moves tracks as XML (a few hundred bytes to ~2.3 kB) or protobuf (Meshtastic: median 87 B after protobuf+zstd); Link 16 uses 75-bit words in 7.8 ms slots; STANAG 4607 is tailorable GMTI. The many-sensors-one-target problem is handled by gridlocking + track-to-track correlation (nearest-neighbour/PDAF/MHT with Munkres assignment), and vendors (Saab TDFE, Systematic SitaWare) sell it as a product feature.

### Cited Findings

- The 3–5 min Delta kill chain is dominated by human steps (verification, allocation to a drone crew, flight time), not by transport of the detection: a 2.2 s detector plus sub-second transport is <2 % of the loop. - Deduplication in COPs is done centrally after sensor registration; any edge-side merge (e.g. 0.5 m / 0.5 m/s gating) must still carry enough covariance for the COP's own correlator, otherwise naive fusion of correlated tracks is "useless" (see KQ5, Sharma et al.).

## KQ5. Event-triggered estimation, send-on-delta and DIS dead reckoning: prior art and reported savings

### Takeaway
The "send only when the receiver's prediction would be wrong" idea has three well-cited lineages: send-on-delta Kalman filtering (Automatica 2007; Sensors 2017), variance/innovation-based triggering (Trimpe & D'Andrea CDC 2012 / TAC 2014; Trimpe & Campi 2016 compares SoD, measurement-based, variance-based and relevant sampling; a 2025 arXiv unifies innovation-based triggers incl. stochastic SoD and SoD-with-prediction), and distributed/consensus filters with KL-divergence triggers (Battistelli–Chisci 2018 Automatica; Dong et al. 2022). Reported savings range from 17 % (event-triggered UKF + CI, aircraft tracking) to 86 % of communication at 16 % accuracy loss (event-triggered diffusion KF on a testbed). DIS (IEEE 1278.1) dead reckoning uses default thresholds of 1 m / 3° with a 5 s heartbeat (secondary sources) and is the direct ancestor of the design.

### Cited Findings
- **Send-on-delta Kalman (networked monitoring).** Automatica 2007 (DOI 10.1016/j.automatica.2006.08.022): sensors transmit "only when their value changes by more than a set amount" and the filter treats silence as information; "reduces sensor data traffic with relatively small estimation performance degradation" (no percentage in abstract) — [BibSonomy record](https://bibsonomy.org/bibtex/1d01439c1a21fccabf8f394f63142874)
- Diaz-Cacho et al., Sensors 2017, "Basic Send-on-Delta Sampling for Signal Tracking-Error Reduction": the delta is chosen in real time for a desired mean transmission rate; vs periodic transmission it "improves deeply the tracking-error while maintaining the desired average throughput" — [PMC5335925](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5335925/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/5bc39b2caa304b8c9ccbbc6dbc4cba12)
- A 2009 MDPI Sensors survey notes send-on-delta "proposed for Kalman filters, where sensor data values are transmitted only when encountering a user-defined change" — [MDPI Sensors 9(4):3078](https://0-www-mdpi-com.brum.beds.ac.uk/1424-8220/9/4/3078) (older)
- **Variance-based triggering.** Trimpe & D'Andrea, CDC 2012: the sensor "runs a copy of the remote estimator and transmits a measurement if the associated measurement prediction variance exceeds a tolerable threshold"; variance follows a switching Riccati iteration converging to a periodic solution — [PDF](https://is.mpg.de/uploads/publication_attachment/attachment/320/trimpe-CDC12.pdf); multi-sensor extension IEEE TAC 59(12):3266–3281, 2014 — [MPI-IS record](https://ics.is.mpg.de/publications/tac14b_web); [PDF](https://al.is.mpg.de/uploads_file/attachment/attachment/322/trimpe-TAC14b.pdf)
- Trimpe & Campi, EBCCSP 2016, "On the Choice of the Event Trigger in Event-based Estimation" compares send-on-delta, measurement-based, variance-based triggering and relevant sampling for a scalar linear Gaussian process — [PDF](https://marco-campi.unibs.it/pdf-pszip/EBCCSP-2016.pdf) (conclusions not retrieved; PDF blocked)
- Trimpe, "Predictive and Self Triggering for Event-based State Estimation" (arXiv 1609.07534): instantaneous event decisions "do not necessarily translate into better usage of the shared communication resource"; proposes predictive/self triggers so the network can reserve slots ahead — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07534)
- A 2025 arXiv paper gives "a unified framework for innovation-based event triggers" covering stochastic send-on-delta (SSOD) and send-on-delta with prediction (SODP) — [arXiv 2503.13043](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.13043)
- **Distributed / consensus filters with triggers.** Battistelli, Chisci & Selvi, Automatica 2018, "A distributed Kalman filter with event-triggered communication and guaranteed stability": nodes transmit only when "particularly significant for estimation purposes"; stability needs network connectivity and collective observability — [UniFi record](https://flore.unifi.it/handle/2158/1131689); [PDF](https://flore.unifi.it/bitstream/2158/1131689/4/Automatica_data_driven.pdf); a distributed Bayes filter transmits "only when the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the current local posterior and the one predictable from the last transmission exceeds a preset threshold", with stability proven in the linear-Gaussian case — [UniFi record](https://flore.unifi.it/handle/2158/1161702); Dong, Battistelli, Chisci & Cai, IEEE SPL 2022 (KLD transmission tests, distributed target tracking) — [UniFi record](https://flore.unifi.it/handle/2158/1274629); related Polimi work — [Polimi record](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1310496)
- **Reported savings.** Alanwar et al., "Event-Triggered Diffusion Kalman Filters" (arXiv 1711.00493): on a physical testbed "saves 86 % of the communication overhead ... while causing deterioration of performance by 16 % only" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.00493); event-triggered UKF + fast covariance intersection for aircraft tracking: "communication times are reduced by 17 %" at accuracy close to plain UKF — [Electronics Optics & Control (Chinese)](https://researching.cn/articles/OJdf8a18cfaa88234d)
- **Track-to-track fusion under communication constraints.** Sharma, Tharmarasa & Kirubarajan (2024): "unknown correlations between the tracks render a standard Kalman filter (naive fusion) useless"; covariance intersection "provides near-optimal estimates but at the cost of a conservative covariance" — [arXiv 2412.06725](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06725v1); Fraunhofer/IEEE AESS lecture: the distributed Kalman filter "works at arbitrary communication rates since the calculation is completely distributed", unlike centralized fusion which needs every update — [IEEE AESS](https://ieee-aess.org/presentation/introduction-track-track-fusion-and-distributed-kalman-filter); Chen et al., "Distributed Fusion Estimation With Communication Bandwidth Constraints", IEEE TAC, cited in a 2015 survey (DOI 10.1155/2015/683701) — [survey record](https://www.peeref.com/works/405130) (older)
- **DIS / IEEE 1278.1 dead reckoning.** Standard thresholds described as "1 m in linear coordinates, 3 degrees in orientation, or 5 seconds", algorithms per Annex B of IEEE 1278.1 (secondary: a simulation patent) — [USPTO 8834163](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8834163); heartbeat "usually set to five seconds"; ESPDUs are sent when dead reckoning diverges "within some standard deviation", on appearance change, on removal, or on timeout — [USPTO 8024481](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8024481); open-dis: algorithms from IEEE 1278.1-1995 Annex B, "expected to receive a new update approx every 5 seconds or so" — [open-dis javadoc](https://open-dis.sourceforge.net/javadoc/open-dis/docs/edu/nps/moves/deadreckoning/DIS_DeadReckoning.html); NPS DIS data dictionary lists Entity State PDU "Size: 1280" (bits, i.e. 160 B base; conflicts with the 1,080-byte figure in patent 8024481) — [NPS data dictionary](https://faculty.nps.edu/brutzman/vrtp/mil/navy/nps/disEnumerations/JdbeHtmlFiles/pdu/29.htm); [Wikipedia DIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_Interactive_Simulation)
- Threshold mechanics: "when this value exceeds a predefined error threshold, known as the spatial error threshold, the parameters ... are updated"; "a smaller threshold results in more frequent update packets"; auto-adaptive multi-threshold DR "can achieve considerable reduction in update packets without sacrificing extrapolation accuracy" — [NUS lecture notes](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~cs4344/0607s1/deadreckoning.pdf); [Auto-adaptive DR (Academia.edu)](https://www.academia.edu/795560/An_auto_adaptive_dead_reckoning_algorithm_for_distributed_interactive_simulation); [Maynooth game-DR paper](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/1282/1/TWCGAMES_final_AK.pdf) (older)

### Inferences
- The closest single prior-art match to "edge runs the receiver's predictor and sends only on divergence" is DIS dead reckoning (1990s) plus Trimpe's variance-based trigger (2012/2014); the closest to "thresholds widen under a byte budget" is Diaz-Cacho 2017 (delta adapted to a target mean rate) and Trimpe 2016 self/predictive triggers (reserve airtime ahead).
- Published savings are strongly setup-dependent (17 %–86 %); the 86 % figure comes from a diffusion filter on a testbed, not from target tracking with manoeuvring vehicles, so it should be cited as an upper bound, not a prediction.
- No source found that uses DIS dead reckoning in live operations; its documented use is simulation/LVC training.

### Gaps
- Trimpe & Campi 2016 conclusions (which trigger wins at a given rate) not retrieved.
- No dedicated 2020–2026 survey of event-triggered state estimation located before the search budget ran out.
- No paper found that quantifies send-on-delta savings specifically for *vehicle/target tracking* with reported bytes/s.

## KQ6. Deconfliction and blue-force position sharing: Remote ID, ADS-B, BFT, Link 16, military UTM

### Takeaway
Civil standards give concrete floor numbers for "what a position share costs": Remote ID is 25-byte messages with Location at ≥1 Hz (data ≤1 s old); ADS-B is a 112-bit extended squitter with position and velocity each ~2 Hz (0.4–0.6 s random spacing); Link 16 words are 75 bits in 7.8 ms slots; TAK-based BFT reports every 3–15 s; US BFT-2 moved refresh from "minutes" to "seconds". Military UTM was not researched (budget).

### Cited Findings
- **Remote ID (ASTM F3411 / Open Drone ID).** "The LOCATION message is broadcast/published ... at least once per second"; other messages "once per 3 seconds" (some regions tightened BASIC ID and SYSTEM to 1 s); LOCATION/SYSTEM data "not older than 1 second"; legacy Bluetooth "imposes a strict size limitation ... in each radio burst", so data is split into message types; a message pack bundles messages on Wi-Fi NAN, Wi-Fi Beacon or BT5 extended advertising — [MAVLink Open Drone ID](https://mavlink.io/en/services/opendroneid.html); each F3411-19 message type (Basic ID, Location/Vector, Self ID, System, Operator ID) "limited to 25 Bytes" — [USPTO 11882223](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11882223); a BLE-legacy transmitter fits "only one 25-byte ODID message per advertisement" and rotates types — [dronecot PR #7](https://github.com/snstac/dronecot/pull/7); ESP32 hardware only transmits BLE legacy advertising; iOS only receives BT4 legacy — [Bluetooth Framework note](https://www.btframework.com/droneremoteid.htm); current edition F3411-22a — [ASTM store](https://store.astm.org/f3411-22.html)
- **ADS-B.** DF17 extended squitter is 112 bits; ICAO Table 2-79 gives airborne position (BDS 0,5) and airborne velocity (BDS 0,9) at 2 per second with 0.4–0.6 s random intervals; surface position 2/s moving, 1 per 5 s stationary; identification 1 per 5 s (high) or 10 s (low) — [ICAO NACC ADS-B Out overview](https://www2023.icao.int/NACC/Documents/Meetings/2021/ADSB/P01-OverviewADSBOut-ENG.pdf); "emitted twice per second at random intervals ... uniformly distributed over the range 0.4 to 0.6 seconds" — [USPTO 6459411](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6459411); [ADS-B for Dummies](https://www.sigidwiki.com/images/1/15/ADS-B_for_Dummies.pdf)
- **Blue Force Tracker.** BFT-1 "can take minutes to load new data and update position-location information, whereas with BFT 2, refresh time is reduced to a matter of seconds" (2011, satellite L-band; JCR → JBC-P) — [US Army, 2011](https://www.army.mil/article/61624/army_fields_next_generation_blue_force_tracking_system) (older); [US Army, 2010](https://www.army.mil/article/48138/army_to_upgrade_force_tracking_system) (older); TAK-based BFT: GPS read "typically every 3–15 seconds for tactical scenarios" and sent as CoT (vendor guide) — [sitx.io](https://sitx.io/resources/blue-force-tracking-guide)

- **Mesh PLI at scale.** Silvus: 559 nodes exchanging all-to-all PLI used <35 % airtime (see KQ2) — [Unmanned Systems Technology](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2022/10/large-scale-tactical-mesh-radio-network-demonstrated/)

### Inferences
- A drone-to-drone position share needs on the order of 25–30 B at 1–2 Hz (Remote ID / ADS-B class) per node, i.e. ~25–60 B/s; a delta-coded link can undercut this only for smooth trajectories, and must still meet a freshness bound (≤1 s for Remote ID) via keyframes.

## KQ7. Counter-arguments: where predictor-based delta sync fits poorly, trust/spoofing of track feeds, and what practitioners say they lack

### Takeaway
The literature's strongest objections are (1) consistency-based trust in shared perception is itself attackable (TrustFlip, 2026) and self-reported positions cannot reveal a lying node without independent ranging; (2) multi-hop relays add latency that practitioners say makes them unsuitable for fast-moving targets; (3) practitioners' stated shortfalls are radar quality, ground-control/AI targeting components, single-provider link dependence and platform fragmentation — not track-feed bandwidth per se.

### Cited Findings
- Consistency/trust defenses for cooperative perception "penalize vehicles whose observations conflict with the majority", and TrustFlip (May 2026) shows physical adversarial objects can make honest nodes look inconsistent without injecting any false data — [TrustFlip (arXiv 2605.22122 summary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.22122)
- Position-spoofing detection in UAV formations: methods "relying only on self-reported positions and pairwise distance measurements fail to identify malicious UAVs"; proposed localization-feasibility test via semidefinite relaxation — [arXiv 2312.03787](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.03787)
- False data injection into distributed UAV sensor values via MITM or counterfeit signals; "distributed systems without central control are susceptible" — [NDSS DISS 2020](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/diss2020-23011-paper.pdf) (older); an attacker can flood a swarm's sharing pipeline with minor obstacles so it cannot report major ones — [arXiv 1709.04137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.04137) (older)
- Survey of GNSS spoofing on UAV swarms: swarm-level weaknesses in cooperative configurations "remain understudied"; swarm control logic "can amplify an attack's impact into cascading failures" — [Univ. of Bedfordshire record](https://researchportal.beds.ac.uk/p/en/research-outputs/a-survey-ongnss-spoofing-attacks-onuav-swarms-challenges-current-); 2026 paper on secure UAV swarms models GPS-spoofing resistance as an attack–defense game and adds behavior-driven trust evaluation for insiders — [arXiv 2605.26876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26876)
- Multi-hop latency: relay chains are "less suitable for very precise manual targeting of fast moving targets" — [dev.ua](https://dev.ua/en/news/rosiiany-zbilshyly-dalnist-keruvannia-dronamy-1788522323)
- Shahed-type drones launched in groups "do not exchange information or co-ordinate their movements like a true swarm" — [Daily Alert / Forbes](https://dailyalert.org/archive/index.php?id=85009)
- What practitioners say they lack: "We just need better radar" (interceptor commander "Phoenix"); "ground control systems, radar and AI-powered targeting components" (Shmyhal) — [Defense News, 2026-03-05](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/03/05/novel-interceptor-drones-bend-air-defense-economics-in-ukraines-favor/); single-system dependence after the Starlink blackout (Brovdi) and cloud dependence as "a huge risk" (Dmitriev) — [NV](https://english.nv.ua/nation/starlink-outage-hits-ukrainian-frontlines-spacex-blames-software-failure-50532360.html); 200+ drone models and the push for one control standard — [Ukrainska Pravda, 2024-12-09](https://pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2024/12/9/7488398) (older); Replicator transparency gap — [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2025/09/03/dod-replicator-drone-tech-transition-fielding-questions-linger/)
- Swarm vendors constrain autonomy by design: Swarmer sets "boundaries on what the AI model can do" and keeps humans in the firing loop — [Mind.ua](https://mind.ua/en/publications/amp/20272770-lords-of-the-swarm); [To Vima/WSJ](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/ai-powered-drone-swarms-have-now-entered-the-battlefield)

### Inferences
- Predictor-based delta sync assumes the sender is honest and the motion model is adequate. Against a jinking interceptor target or a decoy-rich environment (Gerbera decoys outnumber Shaheds in Sting kill counts) the trigger fires constantly and the saving collapses toward keyframe-only rates; and a spoofed edge can silence itself (no deltas = "nothing changed") without any detectable anomaly unless keyframes carry authentication and the COP cross-checks against independent sensors.
- Because COPs already dedupe centrally with gridlocking and correlation, a link layer that reports a *merged* edge track without covariance may be worse for the COP than raw per-sensor tracks; conservative (CI-style) covariances should ride along.

### Gaps
- No practitioner statement specifically about track-feed bandwidth being the bottleneck was found; the absence is itself informative but could reflect the unfinished search.
- No source on how Ukrainian C2 authenticates drone-derived target reports (trust model for Delta inputs).
