## Key Question 1: North Korean GPS jamming (2010–2016, 2024–2026), drone incursions, and ROK/ICAO responses

### Takeaway
North Korean GNSS interference against the South is a recurring, officially attributed phenomenon: the 2016 wave touched >1,000 aircraft and ~700 ships, the May–June 2024 wave was counted by Seoul's science ministry at 201 aircraft and 731 ships in its first three days and later estimated at ~500 aircraft, ICAO found interference in the Incheon FIR "ongoing since 2 October 2024" and censured the DPRK by name in April and October 2025, and at least one ROK military UAV loss (a naval S-100 off Yeonpyeong, April 2024) has been attributed by a lawmaker to NK jamming. The December 2022 five-drone incursion (one into Seoul's P-73 no-fly zone) exposed that ROK radars could not reliably detect sub-3 m drones, and the 2024–2026 ROK-to-NK drone affairs (Pyongyang leaflets Oct 2024; civilian flights Sept 2025–Jan 2026) show both sides now treat small drones as a political-military fault line.

**2010–2016 campaigns (older information)**
- First NK jamming detected during the Ulchi Freedom Guardian exercise in 2010, then Key Resolve 2011 and US-ROK air drills 2012; an MP cited "2,143 jamming attacks on South Korea ... since 2010"; "Between 2010 and 2013, North Korea launched electronic attacks intermittently, causing disruptions for a total of 1,402 South Korean aircraft and ships" — [GlobalSecurity/Sputnik, June 2016](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/2016/dprk-160630-sputnik02.htm)
- 23–25 Aug 2010: signals from near Kaesong interfered with ROK military and civilian GPS receivers on land and at sea — [GlobalSecurity/VOA, Oct 2010](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/2010/dprk-101007-voa01.htm)
- Signals originated from "Kaesong and Haeju" and disrupted GPS-dependent mobile phone functions; in 2012 "four South Korean planes ... failed to land on the first pass" because of navigation issues (transport ministry figure) — [North Standard P&I club note](https://north-standard.com/insights-and-resources/resources/archive/news/north-korea-jamming-gps-signals)
- 31 Mar–early Apr 2016: campaign "lasted nearly a week and affected signal reception of more than 1,000 aircraft and 700 ships, originating from five locations along the border"; sources at Haeju and Mount Kumgang — [UPI, 31 Mar 2016](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2016/03/31/North-Korea-jamming-GPS-signals-in-South-can-affect-planes-cellphones/2551459443059/); "962 airplanes ... nearly 700 fishing ships" — [GlobalSecurity, 1 Apr 2016](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/2016/dprk-160401-pdo02.htm); NBC counted ~280 vessels, Fox >130 fishing boats (lower early counts) — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/north-korea-jams-gps-signals-fishing-boats-south-n548986); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/pentagon-concerned-about-north-korea-jamming-gps-signals-officials-say)

- 2016 response: ROK awarded a 15-billion-won (US$13 M) contract for eLoran technology as a land-based GPS backup — [Bangkok Post via PressReader, 9 May 2016](https://www.pressreader.com/thailand/bangkok-post/20160509/282789240650408)

### Inferences
- The peninsula's documented GNSS-denial profile is "episodic but intense": multi-day campaigns from fixed border sites (Kaesong, Haeju, Mt Kumgang) that hit hundreds of aircraft and ships, plus persistent lower-level interference over the Incheon FIR since Oct 2024. Any sensor-feed product pitched in Seoul should assume edge platforms lose GNSS for minutes to days and that position reports may be spoofed, not just absent.
- The Dec 2022 timeline (first radar track at 10:25, six minutes to classify as drone, five hours airborne, no kill) is the local benchmark for "detection and tracking fail on small targets"; the March 2026 TPS-880K coverage (seven radars, deliveries to Apr 2027) shows coverage is still being built out, and the July 2026 near-shootdown of a USMC drone shows friendly/unknown identification remains weak.
- The S-100 loss near Yeonpyeong and the expert comment on terrestrial PNT suggest ROK judges will respond to arguments about continuity of situational awareness when GNSS is gone.

## Key Question 2: ROK drone and counter-drone organisations, programs, requirements, and US-ROK exercises

### Takeaway
Since the 2022 incursion, Seoul built a joint Drone Operations Command (Pocheon, Sept 2023), then in June 2026 converted it into a policy "National Defense Drone Headquarters" and pushed operations back to the services, alongside a plan for 500,000 drone-trained personnel, ~60,000 training drones by 2029, >20,000 expendable drones, a fast-tracked K-LUCAS long-range attack drone before 2030, and a 2027 drone/counter-drone budget line of 806.1 billion won (+18.8%). Counter-drone fielding is real but thin: seven TPS-880K radars, ~5 Block-I "Cheongwang" 20 kW lasers (10 more planned), Hanwha integrated anti-drone/jammer sets at key sites, and a 17-billion-won rapid-acquisition hard-kill interceptor-drone project (May 2026). Published requirements on data links, bandwidth, GPS-denied navigation or swarm were not found in English sources; the nearest signals are the TICN-II aerial-relay program, a DAPA/ADD loitering munition with a LEO satellite data link, and the August 2026 Ulchi Freedom Shield scenarios on GPS disruption and drone attack.

## Key Question 3: Korean tactical data links and C2 formats

### Takeaway
ROK forces run a multi-link stack under the Joint Tactical Data Link System (JTDLS, 합동전술데이터링크체계): Link-K (Korean Link-16-like joint link via Hanwha's MIDS-class terminals), Link-16, Link-11, KVMF (Korean Variable Message Format, the Army's ground link built on US VMF/MIL-STD-2045-47001D with >130 messages), with Link-22 being added; transport is the Army's TICN (HCTR trunk radio, TMMR multiband radio) with a TICN-II follow-on (5G tactical base stations, aerial relay drones) under Hanwha Systems. Battalion-and-below C2 is B2CS feeding ATCIS. No evidence was found that ROK units use TAK/CoT; a sensor feed aimed at ROK Army judges should map to KVMF/B2CS-style messages, while CoT/TAK matters for the USFK side.

### Cited Findings
- JTDLS "shares tactical information among ground, naval and air forces in near real time" and is adding Link-22 to Link-K, Link-16 and KVMF — [DBpia paper abstract](https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/articleDetail?nodeId=NODE11317304); ROK forces use "Link-K, Link-16, Link-11, KVMF" and research focuses on multi-link data management — [KoreaScience 2020](http://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202018355987856.page)

- KVMF: ADD unveiled it after six years of R&D "based on VMF already used in the U.S. Army" — [Korea Times, "Korea develops indigenous tactical data link"](http://m.koreatimes.co.kr/pages/article.asp?newsIdx=120099); "standardized by designing over 130 KVMF messages and the MIL-STD-2045-47001D-based application protocol" — [KoreaScience](http://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202018355987856.page); vendor page for KVMF TDL products — [GDL Systems](https://www.gdlsys.co.kr/en/sub/sub03_01.php); defence software house listing TDL work — [Weunus](http://www.weunus.com/TDL.html)

### Inferences
- A new sensor feed for ROK Army consumers would most plausibly be exported as KVMF (K-series VMF) messages or as B2CS/ATCIS track inputs; for naval/air consumers, Link-K J-series-like tracks; for USFK and combined exercises, CoT. KVMF being VMF-derived (MIL-STD-2045-47001D headers, bit-packed fields) is itself a precedent for compact, low-bandwidth tactical messaging that Korean judges know.
- TICN's inability to sustain high-rate data on the move, and the TVWS paper's remark that TICN cannot support small-unit high-speed data, support a pitch for sub-kilobit-per-second feeds; TICN-II aerial relays would be the natural carrier.
- Any real deployment would need KCMVP-validated crypto (or at least a path to it) and Korean-language UI/documentation; neither is a hackathon blocker but both are evaluation questions.

### Gaps
- No public figures for TICN/HCTR/TMMR data rates in kbps/Mbps were found.
- The KVMF message catalogue (K-numbers, field layouts) is not public in the sources reached; the GDL page was blocked.
- No evidence either way on ROK Army use of TAK/CoT; USFK unit-level adoption was not confirmed.
- Whether KCMVP (vs a separate MND/DSA military crypto approval) is the governing requirement for tactical drone links was not established from official sources.

## Key Question 5: Peninsula specifics shaping link and navigation requirements

### Takeaway
Korean mountains break line-of-sight radio (a 2ID unit reported being limited to satellite-backed JBC-P because terrain "inhibited line-of-sight radio systems"), the Army's own TICN cannot carry high-rate data on the move, the DMZ is a 4 km-wide sensor belt now being automated as GOP manning drops to 6,000, and the West Sea/NLL islands (Baengnyeong, Yeonpyeong) are where GNSS jamming is most frequent and where the Navy is buying USVs. ROK statements on GPS-denied operations exist mainly as exercise scenarios (UFS 2026) and infrastructure (eLoran, KPS), not published drone requirements; cold-weather and typhoon effects on drones were not found in sources.

### Cited Findings
- 2ID sustainment unit in Korea: "the mountainous terrain inhibited line-of-sight radio systems," limiting coordination to Joint Battle Command-Platform; they compensated with liaison officers — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/282464/building_ready_sustainers_while_deployed_on_the_korean_peninsula); 2ID used an inflatable satellite dish for ground tactical air control during UFG because of the mountainous landscape (2009) — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/26605/soldiers_use_inflatable_satellite_dish_to_stay_connected_in_rugged_terrain_of_korean_peninsula)
- Post-armistice Signal Corps built mountaintop microwave relay sites "from the DMZ southward" in remote locations — [DTIC ADA562152](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA562152.pdf); Korean War operations complicated by running wire over rugged hilltops — [Defense Media Network](https://www.defensemedianetwork.com/stories/the-korean-wars-land-battle-legacy/2/)
- ROK Army mechanisation plans run up against the terrain (older analysis) — [East Asia Research Center, 2018](https://eastasiaresearch.org/2018/10/22/south-korea-wants-to-mechanize-all-its-infantry-the-problem-is-the-terrain/)
- TICN directional 1:1 high-speed links are for fixed stations; omni 1:N links are low speed; no high-speed data on the move — [JKAIS](https://kais99.org/jkais/journal/Vol25no07/Vol25no07p30.pdf); TICN-II adds aerial relay drones and 5G tactical base stations — [KED Global](https://www.kedglobal.com/aerospace-defense/newsView/ked202212150003)

### Inferences

## Key Question 6: Counter-arguments and ROK-specific obstacles for a foreign hackathon project

### Takeaway
Korea's acquisition law mandates domestic preference, foreign vendors usually need a Korean partner for language and access, offsets carry heavy liability terms, the European Chamber complained in Sept 2026 that DAPA "often opens projects only to Korean companies or applies unclear standards to foreign firms," crypto must pass the NIS-run KCMVP for government use, and defence-firm designation requires facility and security standards under Article 35 of the Defense Acquisition Program Act. None of these stop a hackathon demo, but they define what "credible route to fielding" means to Korean judges.

### Cited Findings
- Under the Defense Acquisition Program Act, DAPA "has an obligation to apply domestic preferences"; foreign articles only "in exceptional cases where domestic articles are not available"; low-security items can go to international bidding — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cc7bc544-b902-4649-96cb-2487498fb9bf)
- A 2021 "Korea Defense Capability" policy reportedly set an 80/20 local-to-foreign procurement quota (dated); "working with local partners is considered essential for foreign defense contractors in order to overcome language barriers"; offset terms with "unlimited liability" deter foreign firms — [trade.gov Country Commercial Guide](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/south-korea-defense-industry-equipment); [archived copy](https://www.privacyshield.gov/ps/article?id=Korea-Defense-Industry-Equipment)
- ECCK 2026 white paper (reported 30 Sep 2026): "The Defense Acquisition Program Administration often opens projects only to Korean companies or applies unclear standards to foreign firms"; urged opening AI and cyber defence projects to foreign bidders — [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/09/30/european-business-group-urges-korea-to-open-defense-market)
- Export-license and classification friction described at the 2021 CSIS–DAPA conference — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/executive-summary-2021-csis-dapa-conference); DAPA is both regulator and buyer and structures technology-transfer deals — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/korea-defense-market-open-heres-what-us-companies-need-know-they-walk)
- Market-entry advisers list certification procedures, long approval timelines, technology transfer and localisation requirements — [Asian Insiders, Jun 2025](https://asianinsiders.com/2025/06/24/entering-south-korea-defence-market/)
- Defence-firm designation requires facility and security standards (Article 35 DAPA Act); defence M&A needs MOTIE approval with DAPA review — [Seoul Economic Daily, May 2026](https://en.sedaily.com/politics/2026/05/11/what-procedures-are-required-for-defense-firm-ma-deals)
- KCMVP (NIS) validation for crypto modules protecting government/public-network data; new 3.0 rules tighten physical security; PQC additions — [NIS](https://eng.nis.go.kr/EAF/1_7_2_1.do); [hwsecurity.net](https://hwsecurity.net/blog/kcmvp-3-changes/)
- Domestic startups themselves report slow procurement and red tape (testing abroad) — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/korean-drone-startups-test-wares-in-the-great-white-north-to-avoid-red-tape-at-home/12738676); Carnegie notes Korea's transition from arms exporter to "trusted defense partner" is still in progress — [Carnegie, Feb 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/02/are-long-term-nato-south-korea-defense-ties-possible-transitioning-from-an-arms-exporter-to-a-trusted-defense-partner)

### Inferences
- A pitch to ROK judges should pre-empt the domestic-preference question by positioning the product as radio-neutral middleware that a Korean integrator (Hanwha Systems, LIG D&A, or a startup) could carry, and by showing an export path to KVMF/B2CS-style messages and a KCMVP-compatible crypto boundary rather than claiming direct DAPA sales.
- Judges are likely to ask for Korean-language documentation and a Korean partner; the May 2026 US–ROK drone alliance (common standards, US platform listing) is a plausible counter-narrative for a non-Korean team.

### Gaps
- No official text found on classification rules or security clearance for foreign nationals working on ROK defence software.
- Whether tactical drone links must use KCMVP-validated modules, or a separate MND military crypto approval, was not established.
- No Korean-language-requirement rule was found in sources; the point rests on trade.gov's "language barriers" observation.
