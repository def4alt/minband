## KQ1. Scale and effect of GNSS jamming/spoofing on drones (Ukraine, Baltic/Black Sea, Middle East, Korea)

### Takeaway
Along the Ukrainian front GNSS is effectively unavailable ("literally no GPS"), Russian EW systems are reported roughly every 10 km of front, and reported FPV drone success rates range from ~10 % to ~40 % with EW cited as a leading cause; in the Baltic region 123,000 flights were disrupted in Jan–Apr 2025 alone; the Black Sea, Eastern Mediterranean and Persian Gulf see persistent spoofing that places receivers at airports; North Korea's jamming campaigns (May–June 2024: ~500 aircraft; Nov 2024: 331 reception failures in 10 days) hit civil aviation and shipping, while ROK military says its own operations were unaffected. Numbers are mostly self-reported by combatants or regulators and vary widely between sources.

### Cited Findings

**Ukraine — scale**
- A RUSI report from late 2022 (as reported secondhand) concluded that 90 % of the thousands of drones Ukraine had amassed before Feb 2022 were shot down or crashed by summer 2022; average lifespan of a small quadcopter was reduced to three flights and of fixed-wing models to six — [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/ukraine-is-losing-10000-drones-a-month-to-russian-electronic-warfare-systems-that-send-fake-signals-and-screw-with-their-navigation-researchers-say); [Task & Purpose](https://taskandpurpose.com/news/ukraine-10000-drone-losses) (older: 2022–23 data).
- A later RUSI assessment (based on three unnamed Ukrainian officers interviewed in April–May 2023) estimated Russia was taking down around 10,000 Ukrainian drones per month; an outside expert said the figure was "likely" overestimated. The same report said Russia maintains a major EW system roughly every 6 miles (≈10 km) along the ≈750-mile (≈1,200 km) front — [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/ukraine-is-losing-10000-drones-a-month-to-russian-electronic-warfare-systems-that-send-fake-signals-and-screw-with-their-navigation-researchers-say); [Task & Purpose](https://taskandpurpose.com/news/ukraine-10000-drone-losses) (older: 2023).
- A Ukrainian officer told Kyiv Post that Ukraine had lost nearly 100 drones to Russian GPS spoofing attacks on navigation (date not in snippet; likely 2022–23) — [Kyiv Post archive (Military Times)](https://archive.kyivpost.com/ukraine-politics/military-times-us-forces-could-learn-from-intense-electronic-war-battle-in-ukraine.html).
- Ukrainian drone pilot Zhluktenko: "At the front line, there is literally no GPS"; without satellite positioning operators compare what the camera shows with satellite imagery and maps to figure out where the drone is. The same reporting cluster cites a Ukrainian group saying ~75 % of small tactical UAV losses at the front are caused by EW, and notes Eastern Ukraine is "very flat and has a largely uniform landscape", making standout features hard to find — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/ukrainian-drone-pilots-often-weird-093202645.html); [New Geopolitics](https://www.newgeopolitics.org/?p=5475) (exact outlet for the 75 % figure unclear from snippet).
- Ukrainian Ground Forces EW official, 17 Apr 2026: more than 50 % of enemy (Russian) aerial assets are jammed by Ukrainian EW (Ukrainian claim) — [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/04/17/8030590/).
- Kherson regional governor Prokudin: on average 90–95 % of (Russian) FPV drones do not reach the target and are neutralised by various means (regional, all means, not EW alone) — [NikVesti](https://nikvesti.com/en/news/incidents/299933-prokudin-65-percent-russian-drones-shot-down-kherson-region-week).
- Russian counter-drone commander via Izvestia: only "10 out of 100" Ukrainian drones reach the target, attributed to altitude differences and Russian EW (one-sided claim) — [EurAsian Times](https://www.eurasiantimes.com/90-ukrainian-fvp-drones-fall-prey-to-russian-ew-attacks-while-mighty-ad-systems-struggle-reports-say/amp/).
- Ukrainian drone commander Robert "Madiar" Brovdi: FPV effectiveness for both sides is 20–40 %; causes include electronics failing (no detonation) and a significant share suppressed by EW — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/amp/post/44059).
- One analysis estimates only about a third of Ukrainian UAV missions succeed (methodology unverified) — [Jawrona Substack](https://jawrona.substack.com/p/exclusive-ukraine-lost-75-of-its).

**Ukraine — Russian systems and counter-counter-measures**
- R-330Zh Zhitel: per its technical specifications it jams ground-based radio communications at up to 25 km and airborne communications at up to 50 km; it also disrupts Inmarsat and Iridium satcom, NAVSTAR (GPS) navigation and GSM 900/1800/1900 base stations, and can direction-find emitters — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/4044550-ukrainian-drone-forces-destroy-rare-russian-zhitel-ew-station.html); [Babel](https://babel.ua/en/news/121998-ukrainian-drones-attacked-the-rare-ew-station-zhitel-of-the-occupiers-in-the-luhansk-region); [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/10/06/ukraines-drones-incinerate-rare-russian-r%e2%80%91330-zhytel-radio%e2%80%91interference-complex/).
- Ukraine says only 23 Zhitel destructions have been visually confirmed since Feb 2022 (as of Oct 2025); estimated cost ≈$10 M per station; Russia used Zhitel to blind Ukrainian recon drones over Sievierodonetsk; one was reportedly destroyed 55 km behind the front (Sept 2026) — [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/10/06/8001560); [thepublic.info](https://thepublic.info/en/news/ukrayinski_viiskovi_urazili_rosiisku_stanciiu_reb_zitel_u_zaporizkii_oblasti); [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/09/05/ukraines-lasars-group-says-its-bomber-drones-destroyed-rare-russian-zhitel-jammer-55-km-behind-front/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-330Zh_Zhitel).
- Russian Shahed/Geran drones that hit Kyiv on 1 Jan 2025 carried a new Chinese-made 8-element circular CRPA (agricultural-market part); later a 16-element Chinese CRPA was found; the Russian 12-element "Kometa" CRPA was first found in early 2025 and "Kometa-M" with up to 16 elements appears in Iskander-K; CRPA lets munitions keep satellite lock under EW — [Militarnyi](https://militarnyi.com/en/news/new-chinese-crpa-antenna-found-on-russian-shahed/); [Liga.net](https://news.liga.net/ua/politics/news/ekspert-v-drone-shahed-vpervye-nashli-rossiyskuyu-antennu-eto-problema-dlya-reb-foto); [thepage.ua](https://thepage.ua/ua/news/obstril-kiyeva-1-sichnya-v-op-oprilyudnili-dani-analizu-shaheda).
- Ukraine struck the VNIIR-Progress plant in Cheboksary that makes Kometa arrays (reported strikes 9–10 June 2025 and overnight 5 July 2025; dates differ by outlet) — [Kyiv Independent](https://www.kyivindependent.com/ukraine-hits-russian-electronic-warfare-facility-making-shahed-iskander-components-general-staff-says-06-2025); [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/?p=410732); [Liga.net](https://news.liga.net/en/amp/politics/news/ukraine-struck-a-factory-that-produces-systems-for-iskander-and-shahed-missiles).
- Shahed-type drones take a GPS fix shortly before/after takeoff, then typically switch receivers off and fly on inertial navigation, optionally re-acquiring GPS just before impact; RUSI's Thomas Withington: "It's not always necessarily very accurate, but it's as accurate as it needs to be" (AFP explainer, March 2026, about the later Middle East war) — [Korea Times (AFP)](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/world/20260317/why-iranian-drones-are-hard-to-stop).

### Inferences
- On the Ukrainian front the baseline assumption must be "no trustworthy GNSS at the edge"; any system that needs the edge's absolute position must either carry its own alternative nav or explicitly carry a growing position uncertainty. A link-layer sync product is affected because entity coordinates it ships are only as good as the sensor's self-localization.
- Baltic/Black Sea/Korean jamming is mainly a civil-aviation and shipping safety problem at altitude (1,500–13,000 m in Estonia); its relevance to small drones is the same mechanism (receivers lose lock or are relocated to airport coordinates), and consumer-drone geofencing turns spoofing into forced landings.
- Loss and success percentages (10–40 % FPV success; 50 %+ "jammed"; 75 % of losses to EW) are self-reported, sector-specific and conflated across EW types (control-link, video-link, navigation); nothing in the record isolates GNSS denial from link jamming as the cause of loss.

## KQ2. Alternative navigation approaches fielded or in trials (vendors, programs, claimed accuracies)

### Takeaway
Vision-based absolute positioning against satellite/3D terrain maps is the dominant fielded answer (Maxar/Vantor Raptor <10 m RMSE claimed; Ukrainian OSCAR ≤20 m claimed; Quantum Systems, Shield AI, Tekever, Palantir/Red Cat), with on-board terminal guidance (Auterion Skynode S, tens of thousands of kits) covering the last mile when both GNSS and the link are jammed; magnetic-anomaly (AQNav, best <74 m), LEO signals-of-opportunity (meter-level claimed) and better inertial units are in trials; all accuracy numbers are vendor claims with no independent verification found.

### Cited Findings

**Vision / terrain-relative navigation**
- Maxar (now Vantor) launched Raptor on 3 Mar 2025: Raptor Guide gives on-board positioning with "demonstrated absolute accuracy of <10 m RMSE"; Raptor Sync georegisters drone video to terrain with <3 m absolute accuracy; Raptor Ace extracts target coordinates from video at <3 m; uses only the existing camera plus a 90-million-km² 3D terrain database; claimed to work at night and in low-relief terrain (vendor claims; test conditions not stated) — [Vantor press release](https://vantor.com/blog/maxar-launches-raptor-a-first-of-its-kind-software-that-unlocks-next-gen-gps-resilience-for-autonomous-systems/); [eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/maxar-launches-vision-based-software-for-gps-resilience/); [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/maxar-launches-raptor-a-first-of-its-kind-software-that-unlocks-next-gen-gps-resilience-for-autonomous-systems); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250325579897/en/Maxar-Launches-Raptor-a-First-of-its-Kind-Software-that-Unlocks-Next-Gen-GPS-Resilience-for-Autonomous-Systems).
- Twist Robotics (Ukraine) OSCAR module (Jan 2026): camera captures terrain, algorithm matches landmarks to a map, computes coordinates and feeds them to the autopilot "as a reliable GPS signal"; claimed accuracy up to 20 m with no cumulative error (vendor claim) — [Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/01/28/8018266).
- PG Robotics "Salamandra" module on the Lucky Strike-2.1 FPV: analyzes camera images of the ground to fix position; drone can continue its route, hold position and automatically return to launch after complete GPS loss (vendor claim) — [Razom UA Media](https://razomua.media/en/news/tekhnolohiyi/lucky-strike-21-ukrayinska-vidpovid-na-frontove-hlushinnya-gps).
- Perun (Ukraine, Apr 2026): AI optical navigation with claimed error "up to 5 %" (of what is unstated); can recognise GPS spoofing and auto-switch to optical nav (vendor claim) — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/02/28/ukraine-develops-fiber-optic-module-fitting-for-all-drones-integrates-optical-navigation-module/) (cluster; exact article uncertain).
- Vermeer (US): day/night cameras + compute + preloaded 3D terrain maps from satellite imagery; CEO says it is operational on 40–50 Ukrainian drones (2025) — [The Defense Post](https://gecici.thedefensepost.com/?p=123050).
- UKRSPECSYSTEMS Shark-M (July 2026): GNSS-denied launch using on-board sensors, then optical navigation matching live video to satellite imagery (vendor announcement) — [The Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/07/28/ukraine-shark-m-drone/amp/).
- Quantum Systems deployed the "Receptor AI" upgrade for Vector in Ukraine (July 2024): Jetson Orin-based, optical navigation day/night and in poor visibility, plus AI object recognition, with no weight or range change; Ukraine had bought >400 Vector systems, later +300 funded by Germany — [The Defense Post](https://www.thedefensepost.com/2024/07/15/german-drone-ukraine-upgrades); [Soldier Systems](https://soldiersystems.net/2024/07/15/quantum-systems-deploys-receptor-ai-in-ukraine-for-the-first-time/); [Drones Magazin](https://drones-magazin.de/?p=8425).
- Shield AI: first V-BATs sent to Ukraine lacked Hivemind and could not operate where GPS/comms were jammed; integration took ≈8 months; summer-2024 trials included a two-day test against seven jammers and a 60-mile mission; in one August mission a V-BAT flew 140 km across the front, located a Russian SAM battery and relayed targeting data to HIMARS while GPS and comms were jammed; company says 35+ missions and 200+ targets in 2025 (vendor figures; some sources are sponsored content) — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/12/21/shield-ai-ukraine-defense-tech-gary-steele); [TWZ sponsored](https://www.twz.com/sponsored-content/v-bat-drone-has-proven-its-ability-to-operate-in-heavily-contested-skies); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_AI_MQ-35_V-BAT).
- Tekever describes an on-board visual-based navigation system that compares visual cues (edge compute) with satellite data to update position and orientation in 3D independent of GNSS; no accuracy or AR3-specific deployment data found — [Tekever](https://www.tekever.com/?p=4204).
- Red Cat's Black Widow (US Army Short Range Reconnaissance program of record) demonstrated GPS-denied navigation with Palantir's VNav (edge sensor fusion of visual cues and inertial data); "first known commercial demonstration of visual navigation software on a drone already accepted into a U.S. Army program" (date not in snippet) — [GPS World](https://www.gpsworld.com/red-cat-proves-drone-software-for-gps-denied-navigation/).
- US Army AvMC tested imagery-to-map-database navigation on a helicopter (2023) — [The Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2023/07/20/us-army-visual-navigation/amp/) (older).
- DARPA Fast Lightweight Autonomy (Draper/MIT) vision-aided UAV needed no GPS or prior maps (older program) — [GPS World](https://www.gpsworld.com/draper-equips-uavs-with-vision-for-gps-denied-navigation/).

## KQ3. Cooperative/collaborative localization between drones: what is exchanged, how often, how many bytes, what accuracy

### Takeaway
Academic cooperative-localization work exchanges inter-vehicle ranges (UWB/RF Doppler/OFDM delays), relative bearings (vision) and compressed state/landmark summaries; published communication figures are per-landmark or per-frame totals (≈1.8–4 KB per landmark, 29 KB per LiDAR descriptor, 2–3 MB per km of trajectory) rather than bytes/s, and no fielded military swarm with public byte-level specs was found; Link 16 solves the same uncertainty-compression problem with a 4-bit track-quality field because full covariance "takes 21 real numbers".

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 systematic review classifies relative-localization sensors for GNSS-denied swarms into UWB ranging, passive vision and active optical markers; radio ranging is computationally cheap but observes angles poorly, vision is accurate but compute-heavy — [KhPI review](https://pm.khpi.edu.ua/article/view/350033).
- A 2020 survey covers UWB-based localization for multi-UAV/heterogeneous robot systems, including ad-hoc deployment and collaborative localization in GNSS-denied settings — [arXiv 2004.08174 summary](https://awesomepapers.io/robotics/papers/2004.08174).
- An overview of mutual localization in swarms notes that cooperative methods add complexity mainly because the swarm must keep communication links and protocols running between members — [TransNav](https://www.transnav.eu/files/Overview_of_Mutual_Localization_Techniques_Between_Unmanned_Aerial_Vehicles_in_Swarm,1507.pdf).
- 2025 "Relative Localization of UAV Swarms in GNSS-Denied Conditions": spectral clustering splits the swarm into sub-clusters merged via inter-cluster anchors; OTFS waveforms serve both communication and ranging; it studies the trade-off between communication and localization performance — [arXiv 2509.04412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04412) (full text blocked).
- 2026 OFDM-based swarm positioning (BUAA): UAVs transmit positioning waveforms in turn, estimate inter-UAV delays and rebuild relative coordinates via multidimensional scaling; the length of the transmitted delay data is a design parameter balancing quantization error against latency — [BUAA](https://research.buaa.edu.cn/en/publications/ofdm-based-uav-swarm-cooperative-positioning-design-and-optimizat/).
- TCDRL: two-stage framework clusters the swarm to cut communication and computation, then fuses UWB and on-board sensors with an EKF; simulations report robustness across communication rates — [TRID](https://trid.trb.org/View/2727827).
- Pairwise cooperative navigation with ranging + magnetic-anomaly measurements limits each UAV to one ranging/communication partner at a time for groups of roughly 2–20 UAVs — [arXiv 2005.09541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09541).
- NATO STO MP-SET-222 (2016) on signal-enhanced UAV swarms for GNSS-poor areas explicitly poses whether large bandwidth is needed and whether EHF or lower frequencies are preferable (discussion paper) — [NATO STO](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/STO-MP-SET-222/MP-SET-222-12P.pdf).
- Multi-robot SLAM bandwidth figures: SlideSLAM (decentralized metric-semantic SLAM) averages 3.96 KB per landmark indoors, 1.84 KB outdoors, 2.03 KB in forest, and "2–3 megabytes per kilometer trajectory with 1,000 landmarks" — [arXiv 2406.17249](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17249); DCL-SLAM sends a 29.12 kB global descriptor per LiDAR frame plus 99.92 kB/frame filtered cloud only for loop-closure verification (raw VLP-16 scan 1 MB, HDL-64 2 MB); distributed pose-graph optimization transmitted 3.46 MB vs 10.06 MB centralized — [arXiv 2210.11978](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.11978); edge-assisted ViT–NeRF multi-robot SLAM claims >90 % bandwidth reduction vs image-based pipelines — [arXiv 2603.11085](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11085); ONERA 2019 data-sharing strategy for decentralized collaborative VI-SLAM — [ONERA](https://w3.onera.fr/copernic/sites/default/files/2019_iros_data_sharing_strategy_decentralized_collaborative_visual_inertial_slam.pdf); a 2026 Mars-analogue three-robot ad-hoc network study measured real peer-to-peer throughput/latency — [arXiv 2601.21063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21063v1).
- Link 16 track quality (TQ) is a 4-bit value (0–15) that "maps to a range of position errors"; the TQ value corresponds to the area of a χ²-based uncertainty ellipse per MIL-STD-6016B; a more accurate characterization is the track error covariance, "but because error covariance data takes 21 real numbers, the bandwidth constraints of the data link network only allow transmission ... upon request"; Link 11 uses TQ 0–7 — [US Patent 8052054](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8052054); [2018 CBD paper](https://computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/cbd/2018/803400a089/17D45VsBTTN); [Docsity Link 16 prep](https://www.docsity.com/en/docs/link-16-exam-prep-2026-2027-or-197-questions-and-answers/17788076/) (last is a study aid).
- Drone telemetry baseline (MAVLink): GPS_RAW_INT carries raw GNSS with eph/epv as HDOP/VDOP (unitless, 65535 = unknown) and h_acc/v_acc in mm; GLOBAL_POSITION_INT is the fused estimate; default stream rates ≈4 Hz; streaming GLOBAL_POSITION_INT at 5 Hz plus SYS_STATUS and VFR_HUD gives ≈0.3 kB/s; rates are set with MAV_CMD_SET_MESSAGE_INTERVAL; PX4's MAV_X_RATE caps bytes/s per link and throttles streams; a known issue (mavlink #1063) is that eph/epv are misused as accuracy — [xmavlink GpsRawInt](https://xmavlink-util.hexdocs.pm/Common.Message.GpsRawInt.html); [ArduPilot discuss: lowering telemetry datarates](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/lowering-telemetry-datarates-in-ardupilot/82830); [MAVLink message rates](https://mavlink.io/ko/guide/message_rates.html); [PX4 modules](https://docs.px4.io/v1.14/en/modules/modules_communication.html); [PX4 discuss](https://discuss.px4.io/t/why-decrease-ekf2-gps-v-noise-and-ekf2-gps-p-noise-parameters-when-using-rtk-gps/19746).
- Swarm products: Auterion Nemyx (3 Sept 2025) coordinates drones from different manufacturers as an AuterionOS app; a demo ran eight FPVs and two fixed-wings through a coordinated, vision-guided attack with humans setting the target and Nemyx handling timing and target allocation; optimized for Skynode S — [Auterion](https://auterion.com/auterion-launches-nemyx-enabling-fully-coordinated-drone-swarms/); [The Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2025/09/05/auterion-nemyx-drone-swarm); [Resilience Media](https://resiliencemedia.co/auterion-demonstrates-a-multi-manufacturer-drone-strike-under-real-conditions/). Kyiv-based Swarmer claims its AI networking has enabled >82,000 operations; one operator supervises while drones handle navigation, targeting and adaptation (no data-link details published) — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/109476-swarming-drones-could-overwhelm-air-defenses-changing-future.html); [Ukraine's Arms Monitor](https://ukrainesarmsmonitor.substack.com/p/drone-warfare-in-ukraine-martian).
- A March 2026 market analysis says reports of true mesh-shared targeting among drones in Ukraine "remain anecdotal and unconfirmed" and that Ukraine has mostly used "lots of individually controlled cheap drones"; FPVs are still mostly flown one by one over Starlink-backed links by lone operators — [Robotics Press](https://www.robotics.press/news/drone-swarm-technology-market-trend-analysis/).

### Inferences
- Cooperative localization in the literature moves two kinds of data: (a) raw measurements (ranges, Doppler/delay, bearings) at sensor rate, and (b) compressed state summaries (poses, landmark descriptors, covariances). Only (b) is naturally a "small predictable state" problem; (a) needs tight timing (UWB two-way ranging) that a store-and-forward sync layer cannot provide.
- The Link 16 precedent (compress a 21-number covariance into 4 bits because the link cannot afford it) is direct evidence that military C2 already accepts coarse uncertainty encoding at low rate; a delta-coded state stream with an uncertainty field fits that tradition.
- No fielded system publishes bytes/s for inter-drone state sharing; the closest hard numbers are MAVLink telemetry (~0.3 kB/s for 5 Hz position + status) and SLAM landmark budgets (KB per landmark).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve any paper's full text (arXiv, alphaxiv blocked), so update rates (Hz), per-message byte counts and achieved relative accuracy (m or cm) per method are missing.
- No fielded or demonstrated military cooperative-localization system with public specs (Anduril Lattice, Nemyx, Swarmer, DARPA OFFSET) was found before the search budget ended.
- MAVLink message payload sizes (GLOBAL_POSITION_INT ≈28 B, GPS_RAW_INT ≈30 B+, v2 overhead ≈12 B) were only available from the search tool's own unverified recollection, not a fetched page; verify against common.xml.

## KQ4. How drone position error becomes target-location error, what accuracy different uses need, and how uncertainty is expressed in C2 formats

### Takeaway
TLE is three-dimensional and driven by the sensor's self-location, pointing/attitude error scaled by range (≈1 m per km per mrad), range measurement and terrain elevation; doctrine groups TLE into categories (CAT I/II used for precision munitions; a study aid gives CAT II as 7–15 m, unverified) and precision strike wants ≈10 m at 5 km; C2 formats carry uncertainty compactly (CoT point ce/le in metres with 9999999 = unknown; Link 16 4-bit TQ; MAVLink h_acc/v_acc), so a drone that cannot localize itself must either report a large ce or be ignored for fires.

### Cited Findings
- TLE is "the difference between the actual and the expected location ... 3-dimensional and affected by range to target, self-locating ability of the sensor, GPS accuracy, environmental conditions, etc." — [NDIA Cunningham 2007](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2007/psa_apr/CUNNINGHAM.pdf) (older).
- TLE50 is the radius of a circle centred on the true target coordinate containing 50 % of observations — [NDIA Weinzettle 2005](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2005/psts/weinzettle.pdf) (older).
- Total system error = root-sum-square of target location error, velocity-estimation error × time, and delivery error (cannon timeline equation) — [NDIA 2004](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2004/precision_strike/CANNONTimelineEquation.pdf) (older).
- A TLE "on the order of, or less than, 10 meters at 5 km range" is needed to reduce collateral damage and improve lethality — [NDIA Geswender 2009](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2009/gunmissile/7713geswender.pdf) (older).
- One milliradian (1σ) of angle error at tens of kilometres causes tens of metres of TLE; legacy GPS + pointing-angle + terrain methods were "only accurate to 100 m at best", inadequate for precision strike; an imagery-registration method reached ≈5 m CEP — [JHU APL Tech Digest, Criss](https://secwww.jhuapl.edu/techdigest/Content/techdigest/pdf/V19-N04/19-04-Criss.pdf) (older, late 1990s).
- Safran's STERNA joint-fires system advertises "Cat I / II (depending on payload)" TLE; a study-aid flashcard lists CAT II as 7–15 m (unofficial) — [Safran](https://www.safran-group.com/products-services/joint-fires-support-system-sterna); [Brainscape](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/identify-target-location-error-terms-15629483/packs/22012266).
- US Army ATP 3-60 (Targeting, May 2015 edition) is the doctrinal source for TLE categories but could not be opened — [ATP 3-60](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/army/atp3-60.pdf).
- Army field-artillery practice: "Every mil matters" (Fires Bulletin, 2016) on a battalion's fight against error — [Fires Bulletin](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20161020000235/http:/sill-www.army.mil/firesbulletin/archives/2016/sep-oct/Every%20mil%20matters-One%20battalion's%20fight%20against%20error.pdf) (older; content not extracted).
- Vendor: Maxar/Vantor Raptor Sync and Ace claim <3 m absolute accuracy for ground coordinates pulled from drone video — [eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/maxar-launches-vision-based-software-for-gps-resilience/).
- Operational example: a Shield AI V-BAT relayed targeting data for a Russian SAM battery to an M142 HIMARS while GPS and comms were jammed (Aug 2024) — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/12/21/shield-ai-ukraine-defense-tech-gary-steele).
- Cursor on Target: an XML event format for real-time SA/C2; the `point` element carries `lat`, `lon`, `hae`, `ce`, `le` — [FreeTAK docs](https://www.mintlify.com/FreeTAKTeam/FreeTakServer/concepts/cot-messages); NPS example CoT events set `ce` and `le` to 9999999 alongside `hae` — [NPS GitLab](https://gitlab.nps.edu/Savage/robodata/-/commit/f34d9865c93a96ba65565a6b7a8480137211ff07).
- General CE/LE semantics: CE is horizontal deviation at a stated confidence (CE90 = 90 % radius), LE is vertical; total error combines them — [UP42 accuracy docs](https://docs.up42.com/data/accuracy); [USPTO patent 12632973](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12632973).
- Link 16: 4-bit TQ maps to position-error ranges; full covariance only on request due to bandwidth — [US Patent 8052054](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8052054).
- MAVLink: h_acc/v_acc in mm; eph/epv DOP with 65535 unknown — [xmavlink GpsRawInt](https://xmavlink-util.hexdocs.pm/Common.Message.GpsRawInt.html).

### Inferences
- A drone whose own position is only known to the 10–20 m band of visual nav (or hundreds of metres on tactical INS after minutes) cannot produce CAT I/II coordinates by itself; it can still produce useful SA tracks and cue a second sensor. The honest product is "position + ce/le that grows with time since the last fix", which maps directly onto CoT's ce/le and Link 16's TQ.
- Because TLE scales with range × angular error, platform attitude error (not just position) matters; a compact state that carries attitude/pointing confidence is more useful to fires than position alone.

### Gaps
- Official TLE category thresholds (CAT I–VI in metres) could not be confirmed; ATP 3-60 and NDIA PDFs were blocked.
- Official CoT schema text defining ce/le units and the 9999999 sentinel was not retrievable (mitre.org blocked); the definitions above beyond "attributes exist" rest on the search tool's unverified recollection.
- MIL-STD-6016 TQ-to-metres table not found in open sources.

## KQ5. Loss-of-link and loss-of-GNSS behaviours; what minimal information lets the operator trust the drone again

### Takeaway
Open-source autopilots coast on inertial dead reckoning for only tens of seconds (ArduPilot Plane switches to dead reckoning after 20 s without GPS; Copter 4.3's dead-reckoning failsafe is described as realistically good for 10–30 s) and PX4's position-loss failsafe defaults to descend/terminate; fielded answers are on-board optical re-localization (OSCAR, Salamandra: continue route, hold, return to launch without GPS) and on-board terminal guidance (Skynode S, Hivemind) so the mission continues without the link; no source on operator re-acquisition protocols or minimal "trust" telemetry was found.

### Cited Findings
- ArduPilot Plane: the vehicle detects loss of GPS for more than 20 seconds and switches into Dead Reckoning mode until GPS is regained — [ArduPilot Plane failsafe docs](https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/apms-failsafe-function.html).
- ArduPilot Copter 4.3 added a dead-reckoning failsafe letting the aircraft coast on inertial data for a configurable window; a guide says ArduPilot documentation treats 10–30 s as a realistic ceiling for unaided flight (unverified against official docs) — [DroneXL guide](https://dronexl.co/guides/gps-denied-drone-flight/).
- ArduPilot forum: GPS failure is handled by the EKF failsafe; a priority chain (Smart RTL → RTL with compass → dead reckoning) is not a built-in fallback — [ArduPilot discuss](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/does-ardupilot-4-6-x-support-rtl-without-compass-graceful-degraded-rtl/131026); a user reading the code says a plane losing GPS and RC enters long-failsafe RTL using the EKF without GPS, with accumulating position error — [ArduPilot discuss](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/conyinue-mission-on-loss-of-rc-and-gnss-based-on-imu/34391); camera-based RTL dead reckoning was discussed as a workaround — [ArduPilot discuss](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/rtl-dead-reckoning-using-camera/29028).

### Inferences
- In the dual-failure case (link + GNSS), the autopilot's own horizon is tens of seconds of inertial coasting; survival beyond that requires an on-board absolute fix source. A ground-side predictor that keeps extrapolating the last state will diverge at exactly the INS drift rates in KQ2 (hundreds of metres in minutes for tactical MEMS), so the first message after a blackout must carry a fresh absolute fix with a confidence value rather than a delta.
- What an operator needs after reacquisition, by analogy with CoT/Link 16 and the vendor behaviours above, is: current position with ce/le, source of that fix (GNSS/optical/INS), time since last absolute fix, mode/failsafe state, and the last targets seen with their own ce — none of this is documented as a standard in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No human-factors or doctrinal source on operator re-acquisition after blackout or on "minimum telemetry to re-establish trust" was found (search budget exhausted before that query).
- Official ArduPilot dead-reckoning parameters (FS_DR_ENABLE/FS_DR_TIMEOUT) and PX4 current parameter names could not be verified (docs blocked).
- No quantitative RTL-without-GPS success rates for any fielded optical module.

## KQ6. Counter-arguments: where low-rate state sharing does not help navigation, and what experts say the real bottleneck is

### Takeaway
The record points to compute-on-board (terminal guidance, optical re-localization) and physical-layer choices (fiber-optic, no-RF loitering, CRPA antennas) as the fielded answers to jamming, not better telemetry; sharing small state cannot supply absolute fixes over featureless terrain, cannot replace tight-timing ranging, and does not touch the hit-rate drivers that commanders cite (electronics failures, pilot skill, EW of control links), while true inter-drone mesh sharing in Ukraine remains "anecdotal and unconfirmed".

### Cited Findings
- Fiber-optic FPVs "cannot be jammed because they trail a physical cable rather than transmitting over radio"; Ukraine is standardizing their purchase and they are one of the two dominant categories among 413 systems approved in H1 2026 — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/08/22/ukraine-is-buying-jam-proof-fiber-optic-drones-for-first-time-to-single-standard-so-any-maker-can-compete/); [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/07/13/ukraine-approves-more-than-two-new-drone-systems-a-day-this-year-almost-all-of-them-are-now-homemade/).
- Ukraine's top-ranked strike drone has "no radio signal, no way to jam it" and loiters 48 h — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/08/19/no-radio-signal-no-way-to-jam-it-ukraines-top-ranked-strike-drone-can-wait-48-hours-for-its-target/).
- Hit-rate drivers per a Ukrainian commander include electronics failing to detonate and EW suppression, not navigation alone; effectiveness 20–40 % on both sides — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/amp/post/44059).
- Visual navigation's weak point: eastern Ukraine is flat and uniform, making standout features hard to find — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/ukrainian-drone-pilots-often-weird-093202645.html); "each navigation approach has strengths and limitations depending on the environment" (vendor-hosted Bloomberg piece) — [ANELLO Photonics](https://www.anellophotonics.com/drone-navigation-firms-seek-new-ways-past-warzone-jamming).
- Russia's counter-counter-measure is hardware (8/12/16-element CRPA arrays) that keeps GNSS lock rather than any data-sharing scheme — [Militarnyi](https://militarnyi.com/en/news/new-chinese-crpa-antenna-found-on-russian-shahed/).
- RUSI's Withington on Shahed INS: "as accurate as it needs to be" for the target set — [Korea Times (AFP)](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/world/20260317/why-iranian-drones-are-hard-to-stop).
- True mesh-shared targeting among drones in Ukraine "remain[s] anecdotal and unconfirmed as of March 2026"; most use is "lots of individually controlled cheap drones" over Starlink links — [Robotics Press](https://www.robotics.press/news/drone-swarm-technology-market-trend-analysis/).
- Cooperative localization "add[s] system complexity, mainly because the swarm must keep communication links and protocols running between members" — [TransNav](https://www.transnav.eu/files/Overview_of_Mutual_Localization_Techniques_Between_Unmanned_Aerial_Vehicles_in_Swarm,1507.pdf).
- Reporting on Skynode/Shrike provides no model architecture, confidence thresholds or false-positive rates, and no independent verification of hit rates — [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/auterion-autonomous-targeting-50000-ukrainian-shrike-drones); [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2024/06/skynode-s-auterion-autonomy-kit-lets-attack-drones-fly-through-jamming/).
- Crewed aircraft "are still able to fly safely without GPS" by switching sources — [ERR / SVT](https://news.err.ee/1609792437/svt-gps-disruption-has-affected-123-000-flights-in-the-baltic-region).
- The DASA call treats jam-resistant low-bandwidth telemetry as a separate challenge from GNSS-denied autonomy — [IUK Business Connect](https://iuk-business-connect.org.uk/?p=127938).

### Inferences
- Where low-rate state sharing is irrelevant: (1) supplying the absolute fix itself (needs imagery/terrain/magnetic/SoOP sensing on board); (2) UWB/RF ranging that needs microsecond timing; (3) terminal guidance in the last seconds, which is a vision-and-compute problem on the airframe; (4) fiber-optic and no-RF platforms that have no radio link to economize.
- Where it plausibly helps: keeping the ground picture (entity positions/velocities with growing uncertainty) coherent through blackouts and 10–20 % loss; letting one platform with a good fix (V-BAT-class, or a Raptor/OSCAR-equipped drone) publish compact landmark/target states that cheaper platforms and operators can use for relative localization; carrying ce/le-style confidence so fires users can discount stale tracks.
- The experts' bottleneck, read across sources, is the RF link's existence at all (hence fiber and silence), on-board compute and sensors for autonomy, and verification of claimed performance; bandwidth efficiency of telemetry is a second-order concern in the cited material.

### Gaps
- No expert statement was found that directly evaluates low-rate state sharing as a navigation aid (CSIS Bondar, CNA Bendett and War on the Rocks queries were not executed before the search budget ran out).
- No quantitative evidence on how often cooperative fixes between drones are used operationally.
