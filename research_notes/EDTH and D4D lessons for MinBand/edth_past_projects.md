> **Naming caution.** Several events with similar names are easy to mix up: (a) **EDTH**, run by the European Defense Tech Hub (Benjamin Wolba and colleagues, with VC Inflection), which is the subject here; (b) **EUDIS Defence Hackathons**, run by the European Commission, a separate series; (c) the **London Defence Tech Hackathon** at Sandhurst, run by Future Forces (Richard Pass), also separate; (d) the Darkstar Defence Hack (Tallinn) and others. AP and Reuters coverage sometimes blends them.

## Q1. Which EDTH editions took place (cities, dates, organisers, partners), and what were their challenge tracks and problem statements?

### Takeaway
EDTH began with one Munich weekend in June 2024. By mid-2026 it had grown into a pan-European series: 35 hackathons, 400+ teams and about 40 companies founded, with about 50 events planned for 2026 alone. Each edition is a roughly 48-hour build weekend. Challenges come from sponsors (Helsing, Quantum Systems, STARK, ARX, Fincantieri, TYTAN and others) and from Ukrainian frontline units (3rd Assault Brigade, 3rd Army Corps). The problem statements shifted over time. In 2024 they were broad (C-UAS, IFF, demining, sensor fusion, secure comms). By 2026 they were sharp and EW-centred: Shahed and fibre-optic drones, "keeping systems working when everything is jammed", "comms that don't rely on infrastructure", and "target tracking across messy sensor setups".

### Cited Findings

**Origin and scale**
- EDTH founder Benjamin Wolba (physics background, no military experience) got the idea after seeing a defence hackathon in El Segundo in Feb 2024. He ran the first European version with physicist Jonatan Luther-Bergquist, a partner at VC firm Inflection, four months later in Munich, in June 2024 — [Tech.eu, 2026-06-29](https://tech.eu/2026/06/29/the-hackathon-that-become-europe-s-defence-startup-ecosystem/); see also [Wolba Substack launch post](https://benjaminwolba.substack.com/p/bridging-deep-tech-and-defense-launching) and [Inflection write-up](https://inflection.fund/writings/european-defense-tech-hackathon)
- By the June 2026 Tech.eu profile, EDTH had run 35 hackathons (Copenhagen, Paris, Kyiv, Berlin, London, Prague, Brussels, Tallinn and others) with 400+ teams, and about 40 teams had gone on to form companies — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/06/29/the-hackathon-that-become-europe-s-defence-startup-ecosystem/)
- EDTH planned "no less than 50" events or hackathons across Europe in 2026 — [Resilience Media, 2026-03-11](https://resiliencemedia.co/hackathon-ing-our-way-to-a-new-defence-ecosystem/)
- By mid-2025 EDTH reported 10 hackathons, 1,000+ builders, and an alumni network of 40–50 startups — [EDTH newsletter "10 Hackathons In"](https://pro.eurodefense.tech/p/10-hackathons-in-and-we-keep-going); [2024 review](https://eurodefense.tech/one-year-of-fostering-defense-innovation-in-europe-2024-in-review-and-whats-next/)
- EDTH has also announced an "Asia Tour: From Singapore to Seoul" for 2026 — [eurodefense.tech/asia-tour-2026](https://eurodefense.tech/asia-tour-2026/) (no details retrieved)
- Format: challenges revealed Friday; the first ~24 h for preparation and team-forming; then 24 h of building from Saturday 12:00 to Sunday 12:00; demo day, winners' pitches and award ceremony on Sunday — [EDTH FAQ](https://eurodefense.tech/faq/); Luma agendas e.g. [Prague 2026](https://luma.com/edth-2026-prague)
- At the first hackathon, prepared challenges were offered, but teams could also bring their own projects "so long as they were about defense and made their progress clear" — [Becoming Ready for Deployment (first-hackathon results)](https://eurodefense.tech/becoming-ready-for-deployment-key-results-from-the-first-european-defense-tech-hackathon/)

### Inferences
- The series runs roughly monthly, with several cities per month in late 2025 and 2026. The team's "upcoming defence hackathon" is very likely an EDTH edition or a close relative of one, built on the same 48 h, no-prize-money, jury-with-Ukrainian-input template.
- Problem statements have converged on the EW environment. By 2026, "jammed", "comms that don't rely on infrastructure", "mesh networking", "data fusion" and "tracking across messy sensor setups" appear explicitly (Brussels, Paris, Rome, Tilburg). MinBand fits these better than it would have fitted the broad 2024 tracks.
- Sponsor tracks reveal company priorities. STARK asked for mission-effective software for unmanned systems. Helsing asked for acoustic detection. Fincantieri and the USV challenge pointed to maritime work. The 3rd Army Corps and 3rd Assault Brigade set frontline challenges.

## Q2. Who won or placed (per edition), what did they build, and what happened to them afterwards?

### Takeaway
Most winners are cheap, physical sensing or effector prototypes aimed at a concrete Ukrainian threat: Shahed, FPV or fibre-optic drones, mines, helicopters. Examples include an SDR/TDoA emitter locator, a sub-$1,000 60 GHz FPV radar, event-camera Shahed tracking, a 3D-printed air-defence rocket and an acoustic LoRa mesh. A smaller group of software, data and C2 projects also places (OSINT pipeline, wargaming, swarm software, mesh comms). Follow-on outcomes are real but mostly early-stage: startups founded, Brave1 or Ukraine testing, NATO DIANA 2026 places, small grants, and pitches to the Bundeswehr CIHBw.

### Cited Findings

**2024**

Other Munich 2024 spin-outs named by EDTH: **NORDA Dynamics** (autonomous UAV piloting and targeting; "Best Defense Growth Startup" from United Angels Network; tested in Ukraine and Kursk), **PLENO** (modular autonomous FPV guidance), **Unbound Autonomy** ("AI pin for tactical communications"), **Mimotype Technologies** — [2024 review](https://eurodefense.tech/one-year-of-fostering-defense-innovation-in-europe-2024-in-review-and-whats-next/); [results post](https://eurodefense.tech/becoming-ready-for-deployment-key-results-from-the-first-european-defense-tech-hackathon/)

### Inferences
- **Winning archetype:** a cheap, physical, field-demonstrable sensor or effector against a named Ukrainian threat (Shahed, FPV, fibre-optic drone, mine, helicopter), with a price point or manufacturability story (under $1,000 radar, 3D-printed rocket, commodity microphones).
- **Distributed sensing and mesh networks are a recurring winning motif:**
  - SDR/TDoA (Munich 2024, 1st)
  - LoRa acoustic mesh (Munich May 2025, 2nd)
  - seismic mesh (Amsterdam 2025, 2nd)
  - SandFox mesh comms (Paris 2025, 1st)
  - RFØMN acoustic mesh (Berlin 2025, 1st)
  - Sauron distributed cameras (Berlin 2025, field test)

  Judges reward systems that work as a network of cheap nodes.
- **Software, data and C2 projects can win** (Genova's OSINT pipeline, Odile Vision, Omnivision, SandFox), but they have usually paired a clear operator-facing output (dashboard, alerts, resilient link) with a live demo.
- **Follow-on value is mostly non-cash:** access to Brave1 testing, military unit cooperation (3rd Assault Brigade), CIHBw pitches, field tests (Project Q/Tegel), DIANA, EUDIS accelerators and small regional grants. Of the outcomes found, Neurobus and NORDA have the most operational traction. The outcome claims are largely self-reported by EDTH.

## Q3. Were there past EDTH projects on low-bandwidth ISR, sending detections or metadata instead of video, EW-resilient comms, mesh relays, or operator situational-awareness maps? How did they do?

### Takeaway
I found **no EDTH project that does what MinBand does**: shipping tracked-entity state deltas instead of video over a single-digit-kbps link, with a shared dead-reckoning twin. Close neighbours exist and **they placed well**:
- Mesh comms (SandFox, Paris 2025, 1st)
- Mesh sensor networks that send detections, not raw data (RFØMN, Berlin 2025, 1st; LoRa acoustic mesh, Munich May 2025, 2nd; OmniSensor, Amsterdam 2025, 2nd)
- Jamming-tolerant swarm coordination (The Swarmers, two awards)
- Prediction-driven edge tracking without comms (Ghostlock, Rome 2026, 1st)
- Drone-footage-to-intel and scene intelligence (Mozok, Tallinn 2026, 3rd; Aereus, now NATO DIANA)

The niche looks like whitespace that sits directly on top of what judges already reward.

### Inferences
- Mesh, comms and distributed-detection entries won 1st place at least three times (Munich 2024, Paris 2025, Berlin 2025). Judges and soldiers clearly value link resilience and networks of cheap sensors. MinBand is complementary rather than competing: it is the payload-efficiency layer that would ride on a SandFox- or RFØMN-style mesh, or on LoRa as in the Munich 2025 acoustic team.
- Ghostlock's winning pitch was quantified efficiency through prediction (89% compute cut). MinBand's pitch is the same idea for the link: "≈60–800 B/s vs ≈1.5 Mbps", or about three to four orders of magnitude. That framing has been rewarded before.
- MinBand risks being seen as "yet another detector". Edge detection is crowded (Neurobus, Excubitor, Eagle Eye, Sauron, Vilnius 1st). The differentiator to lead with is the **link and the twin**, not YOLO.
- No visible past project integrated TAK, Delta or Kropyva. A CoT/TAK export of the twin, if feasible, would give operators a familiar map and would be novel within EDTH as far as I could find. This is inference from absence of evidence.

## Q4. What were the published judging criteria, and what do judges, organisers and winners say made projects win?

### Takeaway
EDTH deliberately gives **no prize money**. The jury honours "teams that made the most progress and have the best chances to continue toward deployment". The published Amsterdam 2025 rubric has five questions: real problem, effectiveness, originality, mass-manufacturability, and progress and drive shown during the weekend. Juries mix military experts, defence-company staff and investors, and EDTH says it always includes Ukrainian representation. In Ukraine-hosted editions, brigade officers personally pick or endorse teams. The rewards are visibility, endorsement, pitches to militaries and field testing.

### Inferences
How MinBand might score against the five published criteria. These are my judgements, not sourced.
1. **Real problem:** strong if framed with the 2026 challenge language ("keeping systems working when everything is jammed", "comms that don't rely on infrastructure") and a concrete scenario, such as a recon drone over a jammed sector where a video link dies.

## Q5. What do sponsor challenge statements tell us about current demand?

### Takeaway
Demand has moved from "detect drones" toward fighting in a contested EM environment. Across 2025–26, the repeated asks are:
- countering Shahed and fibre-optic drones (non-RF detection: radar, acoustic, event cameras)
- GNSS-denied navigation and localisation
- jamming-resilient, infrastructure-free communications and mesh networking
- multi-sensor fusion and tracking across heterogeneous sensors
- autonomy that reduces operator load
- maritime/USV and swarm orchestration

These line up closely with MinBand's thesis.

### Inferences
- MinBand covers four 2026 demand phrases at once: "keeping systems working when everything is jammed", "comms that don't rely on infrastructure", "tracking targets across messy sensor setups" (the 3D Kalman tracker and twin), and "autonomy that reduces operator load" (the operator watches a clean entity map, not raw video). The pitch should quote these phrases back to the jury.

That is a natural framing, though the team should check it against what Ukrainian judges consider realistic (data-link margins and minimum link rates under EW).

Choosing a track or challenge that names comms, jamming or fusion is likely better than a generic C-UAS track.
