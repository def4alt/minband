## Q1. What is "D4D"? Organisers, cities, dates, sponsors, tracks and problem statements

### Takeaway
"D4D" is, with high confidence, **Deploy for Defense**. It is a Seoul-based APAC defence-tech builder community run by David Choi's EGCED Tech (익시드테크). It held its first 24-hour hackathon in Seoul on 3–5 Jul 2026 and is co-hosting the **Korean Defense Tech Hackathon (D4D x EDTH)** in Seoul on **9–11 Oct 2026**. That October event is almost certainly the "upcoming defence hackathon" the MinBand team is entering, given the user's "EDTH and D4D" pairing. No other defence-hackathon "D4D" was found.

## Q3. Across comparable defence hackathons, which winning projects are closest to MinBand, and what did they do that MinBand does not?

### Takeaway
No hackathon winner found does what MinBand does: deterministic dual-ended dead reckoning of entity states streamed at tens to hundreds of bytes per second. The neighbours fall into four groups:
- **Drone-imagery-to-3D/geo situational awareness:** AEREUS, EUDIS 2025.
- **GPS-denied on-drone CV localisation and guidance:** Overwatch (NatSec 2024); Sensorama Lab/KEF Robotics (DIU Kraków 2024).
- **Edge C2 and early warning:** SwarmD2C2 and Project Kamikaze (NatSec 2026); NATO Innovation Challenge 2026 entries "working without continuous comms".
- **EW and counter-drone:** the Brave1/D3 Jammer Hackathon.

Winners overwhelmingly cluster in **counter-UAS/interception with physical hardware**. Neighbours typically had a physical platform, a kinetic or EW effect, GPS-denied geolocation, or direct operator/COP integration. MinBand currently lacks those.

### Cited Findings

**Closest analogues to MinBand (sorted by relevance)**
1. **AEREUS (Germany), EUDIS Hackathon Spring 2025, top-3 EU-wide.** Turns drone imagery into **real-time 3D scene reconstructions and geospatial intelligence** for tactical teams, to improve situational awareness and mission decisions. Spring 2025 ran 9–11 May 2025 across 8 EU states with the theme "Rapid Defence Solutions for the Ukrainian Battlefield and Beyond"; the EU-wide pitch was 9 Jul 2025. — [EU Reporter, 16 Jul 2025](https://www.eureporter.co/defence/2025/07/16/bringing-bold-ideas-to-the-frontline-eudis-hackathon-spring-edition-winners-announced/); [European Commission DG DEFIS](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/bringing-bold-ideas-frontline-eudis-hackathon-spring-edition-winners-announced-2025-07-15_en)
2. **Overwatch, 1st Silicon Valley National Security Hackathon, 2nd place (May 2024, SF).** "Deployed computer vision models on drones for object localization without GPS." The event had 500+ participants, judges from CIA, DIU and Stanford, and was backed by Shield Capital. Top three won cash plus Starlink terminal access. 1st place was **AI Laser Sentinel**, an AI-powered laser to shoot down drones. — [Shield Capital](https://shieldcap.com/announcement/first-silicon-valley-national-security-hackathon-provides-new-solutions-to-critical-government-challenges-attracting-more-than-500-participants); [Devpost listing, 4–5 May 2024 (Stanford Defense Tech Club, Gordian Knot Center, Cerebral Valley, Shack15)](https://natsechack.devpost.com/)
3. **Sensorama Lab / KEF Robotics (Ukraine/Norway), DIU hackathon at the NATO–Ukraine Defence Innovators Forum, Kraków, 4–6 Jun 2024, winner.**
   - Theme: automated last-mile visual guidance without human intervention.
   - 13 teams (4 Ukrainian, 9 NATO) judged on "open-standard criteria"; US$100k pool.
   - DOU reports US$50k for 1st place and US$15k for 3rd place (AI Autonomix).
   - KEF's product, Tailwind, is GPS-denied visual navigation. KEF later won a US$1.25M USAF Direct-to-Phase-II SBIR.
   - Sources: [DIU](https://www.diu.mil/latest/unmanned-aerial-system-automated-visual-guidance-hackathon-held-at-nato); [DOU](https://dou.ua/lenta/news/automated-visual-guidance-hackathon-results/); [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/entrepreneurship/pittsburghs-kef-robotics-secures-air-force-funding-despite-delays/)

   - 2nd place: **CANOPY**, cross-domain attribution and orbital protection.

### Inferences (MinBand vs. neighbours: what they did that MinBand does not)
- **Physical platform and live effect:** most winners (Team Bone, Harlequin, Trusk, AI Laser Sentinel, MAELSTROM, Jammer Hackathon EW systems) demoed hardware or an effect on a threat. MinBand is a data-layer product. To score on "military applicability", it likely needs to show itself as the cueing and track layer *for* an effector, such as C-UAS interceptor cueing or an artillery or ISR COP.
- **Geo-registration without GPS:** Overwatch and KEF solve "where is the object in world coordinates without GPS". MinBand's positions come from the iPhone's local tracking frame. A judge will ask how MinBand gets MGRS or lat/lon in a GPS-jammed area.
- **3D scene vs. entity state:** AEREUS streams reconstructions (heavy). MinBand's differentiator is sending only entity deltas. The pitch should frame it as "AEREUS-class situational awareness at 1/1000th the bandwidth", and the demo should show a byte counter next to an H.264 baseline.
- **Comms-degraded design:** NATO IC 2026 explicitly rewarded continuing to operate without continuous operator comms. MinBand's dead-reckoned twin, which keeps predicting through outages and repairs by resending state, maps directly onto this language. Show a live link cut (jam or unplug) and the twin coasting, then re-converging.
- **COP/TAK interoperability:** none of the found winners advertised TAK. Commercial C-UAS vendors treat a TAK plugin as table stakes. Emitting MinBand entities as Cursor-on-Target to ATAK/WinTAK would be a cheap, visible interoperability win.
- **Multi-node fusion:** Singapore's "Lord of the Rings" integrated-capability prize and D4D's C2/COP track premise both reward fusing many sensors. MinBand currently fuses one edge device to one laptop. Two or more phones feeding one twin, with ID deconfliction, would match judges' language.
- **EW signature:** the Jammer Hackathon shows the adversary's playbook is to detect, spoof and suppress *video and control links*. A low-duty-cycle, tiny-payload link is plausibly harder to detect and DF than continuous video. This is an inference that needs RF-level evidence (duty cycle and airtime numbers) before it is pitched as fact.

## Q4. What judging criteria and winner patterns recur?

### Takeaway
The recurring rubric across events is **problem fit + military/operator applicability + working technical implementation + scalability/business**. Military applicability is usually the single largest weight; D4D Oct 2026 gives it 30%. Recurring winner traits:
- a live working demo, often hardware;
- counter-UAS or interception themes;
- small full-stack teams with prior assets (corporate or startup teams win);
- operator or Ukrainian-frontline validation;
- a credible post-event pathway (accelerator, test range, term sheet).

### Cited Findings
- **D4D Oct 2026 rubric:** stage 1 is problem fit 25%, military applicability 30%, technical implementation 25%, creativity 20%. The final is impact/scalability 35%, global expansion 25%, technical difficulty/differentiation 20%, founder/team drive 20%. ### Inferences
- For D4D Oct, about 55% of stage-1 points (military applicability plus problem fit) are about *whose problem* you solve. Naming a concrete ROK operator scenario matters as much as the bandwidth numbers. Possible scenarios: an ROK Air Force CCA or ISR feed over a jammed link; counter-drone cueing near airbases; maritime/coastal watch.
- "Global expansion" (25% of the final) and the Oregon pathway suggest a dual-use, export-friendly story would help, for example disaster response or border/coastal monitoring with the same low-bandwidth link.
- Teams that integrate with sponsor tools score better. Using CLIKA (edge-AI compression) and NUCODE tooling in the Tactical Edge track is likely to be noticed by those partner judges.
- Since hardware is "actively encouraged", flying or carrying the edge device for real is likely worth the risk. Examples: phone on a drone or pole, and a real radio-constrained link such as LoRa or a throttled Wi-Fi/BLE hop, rather than only a synthetic eval.

## Q5. Judge or organiser commentary on common mistakes and what they want to see more of

### Takeaway
Explicit defence-hackathon judge commentary is sparse. The main themes are:
- keep momentum after the weekend: realistic testing, end-user follow-through, published 6-month outcomes;
- work from the provided operator problem statements instead of inventing use-cases;
- reuse sponsor and open-source tools;
- don't over-scope;
- organisers increasingly say they want ventures and pathways, not one-off prototypes.

### Cited Findings
- **Bryce C. Barros** (mentor and judge at the Taiwan Defense Tech Hackathon, 2–4 Oct 2026), in the *Taipei Times* (9 Oct 2026), argued:
  - the government, industry and stakeholders should build a **repeatable pathway** so momentum doesn't fade after the event;
  - prototypes "would need testing under realistic conditions" before deployment;
  - there should be closer ties with end users, citing Ukraine's experience;
  - hackathons alone won't deter grey-zone coercion.
  - Teams worked on coastal-security gaps, such as protecting undersea cables, for police, coast guard and navy.
  - A search summary also attributes to the piece a suggestion that organisers publish **six-month results** documenting end-user collaboration, realistic testing, follow-on funding and adoption. A second search could not confirm this wording.
  - Source: [Taipei Times, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2026/10/09/2003865641)
- **EDTH (organiser) on the Taiwan event:** "Not weekend prototypes, and not picking winners". EDTH said it would keep working with the strongest teams afterwards. — [EDTH Asia Tour via search](https://eurodefense.tech/asia-tour-2026/)
- **D4D organiser framing:** the event is "not a contest of pitching ideas or business plans" but of solving and implementing real defence problems on site. D4D wants to build an APAC Brave1-like platform. — [Asiae](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026071010261919430); [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/tech-science/20260712/d4d-hackathon-lays-foundation-for-defense-ai-ecosystem-in-apac)
- **Mentor write-up from "Critical Ops"**, a DC defence-tech hackathon run by Johns Hopkins, Georgetown and GWU students:
  - teams leaning on sponsor tools or open source "moved significantly faster";
  - the organisers' problem statements came from real defence and intelligence challenges, and "the teams that succeeded did not try to invent a new use case";
  - the guide is subtitled as being about scoping, finding real users and "avoiding the trap of building too much".
  - Source: [Luke Chen, Substack](https://lukechen.substack.com/p/how-to-win-a-defense-tech-hackathon)
- **AP coverage of the London 2025 event:** an organiser and co-founder pointed to post-hackathon outcomes (Ukraine testing, acquisition, term sheets) as the measure of success. — [AP/Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/cybersecurity/news/22944257/hackathon-teams-race-to-solve-defense-tech-challenges-as-europe-boosts-military-capabilities)
- **General (non-defence) judge guidance**, lower relevance:
  - over-scoping means nothing works end-to-end;
  - flashy dashboards without real users;
  - pitching a product that wasn't demoed;
  - show it working within about 90 seconds, then explain;
  - name the hardest engineering decision while the demo is on screen.
  - Source: [JetBrains, "Notes from the judging table", Jun 2026](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)

### Inferences
Applied to MinBand at D4D x EDTH:
- **Lead with a live, end-to-end demo inside 90 seconds:** phone detects and tracks; the laptop twin moves; a live byte counter shows about 100 B/s against about 1.5 Mbps video; the link is cut and the twin coasts; the link returns and state re-syncs.
- **Use the provided operator problem verbatim:** pick the Tactical Edge or UAS/C-UAS problem statement and state which ROK operator problem it addresses. Do not pitch a generic "bandwidth" platform.
- **Show a post-event pathway:** for example, "validate on the Oregon UAS test range via the D4D basecamp, then integrate with a C-UAS or ISR partner". This matches what organisers say they want to see.
- **Don't over-build:** one edge device, one twin, one clean jam/outage scenario, and optionally CoT output to ATAK. Skip extra features.

- Interviews with EDTH, EUDIS or Brave1 judges about mistakes were not found in this pass. The EDTH researcher may have more.
