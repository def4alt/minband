# Prove the link live, then field it

MinBand's best route to a podium at D4D x EDTH in Seoul (build on 10 October, demo day 11 October 2026) is to pitch itself as **the link layer that keeps a commander's picture alive when video dies**, not as one more detector. The team should prove that live: a real phone, a measured video baseline, the link cut on stage, and tracks landing on a TAK map. Across the EDTH editions whose results could be found and D4D's July debut, this research found **no project that streams tracked-entity state instead of video to a shared dead-reckoned twin**. Its nearest neighbours win often. Distributed-sensing and mesh entries took first place at Munich 2024, Paris November 2025 and Berlin November 2025, and Rome 2026 went to Ghostlock on a single quantified prediction claim (89% less detection compute). Podium projects nearly always pair a working, usually physical demo against a named threat with an output an operator recognises and a credible next step. EDTH pays no prize money and treats the pathway as the reward: Brave1 testing, Bundeswehr pitches, NATO DIANA, or D4D's Oregon test range. Seoul's rubric puts this in numbers. At track level, **military applicability (30%) and problem fit (25%) outweigh technical implementation (25%)**, and the final weights **impact and scalability at 35% and global expansion at 25%**, so a bytes-per-second chart without a Korean operator scenario and a fielding path will stall at the track. For real battlefield use, the main gaps are not compression. **Positions live in a printed-marker frame that no C2 system can ingest, nothing is encrypted, the ground station transmits almost as often as the drone, and the wire format does not fit the roughly 10–80 B/s telemetry links drones already carry.** Page fetches were blocked during research, so nearly every external source was read through search-engine summaries: check winner lists, rubric weights and quotes before anyone repeats them on stage.

## Mesh and prediction entries win, but nobody has shipped MinBand's idea

EDTH has grown from one Munich weekend in June 2024 to **35 hackathons, 400+ teams and about 40 companies founded by June 2026** ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/06/29/the-hackathon-that-become-europe-s-defence-startup-ecosystem/)). It deliberately offers no prize money. Juries honour "the most progress during the hackathon" and "the best chances to continue toward deployment" ([EDTH FAQ](https://eurodefense.tech/faq/)), and organisers say every panel includes Ukrainian representation because operational feedback matters ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/06/29/the-hackathon-that-become-europe-s-defence-startup-ecosystem/)). The only published rubric asks five questions: is it a real problem, how effective is it, how original is it, **how mass-manufacturable is it**, and how much progress did the team show during the weekend ([EDTH Amsterdam 2025](https://eurodefense.tech/european-defense-tech-hackathon-2025-amsterdam/)).

The typical winner under that rubric is a cheap, physical sensor or effector that can be shown working in the field against a named Ukrainian threat. D4D's July edition followed the same pattern. Team Bone, from BONE AI and its hardware subsidiary D-Makers, won with an AI interceptor drone flown in a GPS-denied, obstacle-rich indoor space; the win was credited to doing everything from soldering to airframe machining in 24 hours ([Wowtale](https://wowtale.net/2026/07/13/261362/)). The second pattern matters more for MinBand: **networks of cheap nodes and resilient links keep winning first place**. Ghostlock, Rome's July 2026 winner, is the closest model for MinBand's pitch. ## Fielding depends more on geolocation, crypto and radio silence than on bytes

On bytes alone, MinBand already beats DARPA's objective. What separates it from real use is everything around the bytes. Links in the field range from multi-Mbps encrypted mesh, such as Shark-M's Silvus link claimed at 180 km ([Ukrspecsystems](https://ukrspecsystems.com/drones/shark-m-uas)), to jamming so heavy that Ukraine has delivered **352,000 fibre-optic FPVs since July 2025** ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/articles/2026/01/25/8017810/)). The market also cuts against MinBand's instinct. A Ukrainian defence-minister adviser wants domestic mesh modems "with enough bandwidth for stable HD video" ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/04/29/ukraine-needs-to-build-its-own-high-speed-mesh-tech-as-china-is-russias-supplier-and-western-modems-are-too-expensive/)), and Ukraine's national architecture streams video to central AI. MinBand should therefore be sold as the layer under video rather than a replacement for it: tracks always, image chips when the budget allows, video on request. That layer keeps the picture alive at the edge of coverage and lets one thin link serve many drones.

### Anchor every track to the earth, with honest error bars

The printed-marker origin is the largest single gap, because no C2 system can use it (`ios/MinBand/Origin.swift`; positions are metres with Y up in `core/src/wire.rs`). Fielded systems combine platform pose with gimbal angles and a range source, and the range source dominates the error. Without a rangefinder, errors at oblique angles run to hundreds of metres. In one study a terrain model cut a 600 m error to about 180 m, and a 50 m target-height error alone produced 130–150 m on the ground ([Sensors 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8914804/)). A laser rangefinder stays under 8 m at 10 km, and GNSS-free visual localisation reached a 19.5 m mean error in a 2026 study ([NaviLoc](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/10/2/97)).

The fix starts by taking the camera's WGS84 pose from the platform's navigation solution, keeping the local frame only as a compression tile whose origin is sent once. The Kalman covariance the tracker already computes (`ios/MinBand/Tracker.swift`) should then travel in `EntityState` and from there into CoT `ce`/`le` ([FreeTAKServer docs](https://www.mintlify.com/FreeTAKTeam/FreeTakServer/concepts/cot-messages)). Every fix should be tagged with how its position was obtained, and every output labelled as **awareness grade (tens of metres) or fires grade (under 10 m)**, never blurring the two. Several room-scale constants also have to grow with that error: the budget controller clamps thresholds to 0.05–2.0 m (`core/src/edge.rs`), fusion merges tracks within 0.5 m and 0.5 m/s of each other (`server/src/fusion.ts`), and class priors cap a person at 3 m/s and clamp entities to the marker plane (`core/src/classes.rs`). At drone ranges these become covariance-gated association, vehicle motion priors and ground height from a terrain model.

### Encrypt and go quiet before anything else ships

Security is a declared non-goal today (`docs/DESIGN.md`), and that cannot survive contact with real users. Every recon datalink in this market advertises AES. Unencrypted DJI DroneID broadcasts revealed pilot locations early in the war ([C4ISRNET](https://c4isrnet.com/battlefield-tech/2022/10/17/how-ukraine-learned-to-cloak-its-drones-from-russian-surveillance)), and Russia's Rubicon unit specialises in radio reconnaissance to find Ukrainian drone operators ([RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/russia-drone-rubicon-secret-ukraine-war/33532804.html)).

Reading the code for this report turned up three emission problems. The first is that the ground station talks as much as the drone. The edge sends nothing but `Hello` until it hears an `Ack` (`core/src/edge.rs`), and the server then acks every delivered datagram once 100 ms have passed or a gap appears (`server/src/world.ts`, `ACK_INTERVAL_MS = 100`). So **the ground station transmits roughly as often as the drone, up to ten times a second, and a receive-only ground station gets no data at all**. The ground transmitter is usually what gets an operator located. The second is a periodic fingerprint: `Hello` repeats the device ID in clear every 5 s, and keyframes go out on a fixed 2 s period. The third is that `Pose`, sent twice a second, becomes a beacon broadcasting the platform's own location once positions are geodetic.

The fixes start with a pre-provisioned, receive-only session start, so the edge transmits without waiting for an `Ack`. The eval harness already pre-acks the edge and runs with no acks (`tools/eval/README.md`), so the resilience cost can be measured. The budget controller should become an emission-policy controller, with modes such as silent, burst-on-event, periodic-minimum and normal, and a maximum duty cycle alongside the byte budget. Keyframe timing should be jittered and updates batched into fewer bursts, because fewer, shorter transmissions give intercept receivers fewer chances and less dwell time for direction-finding; that is why low-duty-cycle methods appear among the techniques for links with low probability of intercept ([K-State UAS text](https://kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub/unmannedaircraftsystems/chapter/chapter-13-data-links-functions-attributes-and-latency/)). Encryption has to be compact. Standard AES-GCM framing adds 28 B per packet, about **47% overhead at 60 B/s** with one packet a second. An implicit nonce with a 4-byte counter and a 64-bit tag cuts this to roughly 10–12 B, or 17–20%; Ascon-AEAD128 allows truncated tags ([IETF draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ochkas-cose-ascon/)), and the counter doubles as the replay protection that authenticated encryption alone does not give ([IACR ePrint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1882.pdf)). Keys should be per sortie and loaded by cable before flight, the model PX4 uses for MAVLink signing ([PX4](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/mavlink/message_signing.html)), with IDs and pose kept inside the ciphertext and libsodium or the reference implementations used instead of custom crypto.

### Fit the radios drones already carry

The current wire format assumes Wi-Fi-class IP. An `Update` is about 31 B of `postcard`-encoded f32 position and velocity, datagrams run up to 1,200 B, and the metrics add a 28 B UDP/IP header (`proto/PROTOCOL.md`). Real low-rate links are much tighter. ExpressLRS telemetry at 500 Hz gives roughly 78 bps at a 1:128 telemetry ratio and 625 bps at 1:16 ([ExpressLRS](https://www.expresslrs.org/info/telem-bandwidth/)), and its MAVLink mode forces a 1:2 ratio at the cost of control update rate ([ExpressLRS MAVLink](https://www.expresslrs.org/software/mavlink/)). Meshtastic squeezes CoT to a **median 87 B per message within a 237 B MTU** over LoRa ([Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/apple/developer/tak-protocol/)). Auterion's Skynode S splits its links into high-bandwidth IP links for video and low-bandwidth serial MAVLink links that carry no video ([Auterion docs](https://docs.auterion.com/hardware-integration/skynode-s/connectivity)), and that serial path is exactly where an entity stream would add ISR value today. The engineering work aims at a **10–50 B/s tier**: a bit-packed fixed-point codec at 8–16 B per entity, framing sized to the radio's MTU, priority by information value (new entity first, then class change, then prediction error, then routine refresh), cluster reports once the entity count exceeds a cap, and a MAVLink 2 transport with signing.

If MinBand reaches 10–20 B per entity update, a testable claim follows: 4–8× denser than Meshtastic's compressed CoT on the same LoRa link. This tier suits loitering recon and relay drones. It does not suit strike FPVs in their terminal phase, where telemetry bandwidth comes out of the control link.

### Detect what soldiers name, and let them check it

The COCO subset in `core/src/classes.rs` (person, backpack, cup, chair, laptop and similar) is the wrong taxonomy, and it is also the "fixed-category" detection DARPA excludes. Ukraine's Avengers system targets military hardware and claims about 70% detection at 2.2 s per object ([UNITED24](https://united24media.com/defense-tech/what-can-ukraines-battlefield-ai-do-detect-russian-hardware-in-22-seconds-for-starters-22951)). Decoys with thermal and radar signatures are common ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/03/04/forbes-ukraines-decoy-tanks-trick-russian-forces-next-active-dummies-will-spy-on-them/)).

Four changes follow. The first is a military taxonomy configurable per mission, with attributes (moving, hot, possible decoy) and affiliation always set to "unknown". The second is a thermal model path, trained on the public battlefield IR dataset now ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/russia-ukraine-battlefield-infrared-dataset)) and on Brave1 or Avengers Labs data later ([The Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/08/12/ukraine-ai-drone-target-detection/amp/)). The third is a protocol state that moves an entity from unconfirmed to operator-confirmed or rejected. The fourth is image chips on demand, building on the thumbnail capability bit that `Hello` already reserves; chips should load progressively, because a 2 KB chip takes about half a minute at 60 B/s.

Evaluation has to change as well. `tools/eval` measures how well the twin stays in sync with the edge's own tracks; it does not measure perception. DARPA scores data reduction "with preserved mission utility", so the metric that will persuade buyers is task success: an operator using MinBand correctly reports count, class and location within a set time, compared against an operator watching video.

### Leave the iPhone without rewriting the core

ARKit's visual-inertial tracking, LiDAR depth and the Neural Engine have no drop-in drone equivalent, so the port re-architects pose and depth rather than recompiling YOLO. ARKit gives way to the drone's own visual-inertial odometry and navigation; LiDAR to a laser rangefinder, a terrain-model ray-cast or triangulation from multiple views; CoreML to TensorRT, Qualcomm's toolchain or Hailo's compiler. Power decides the hardware. A Hailo-8 draws about 2.5 W typical ([Mouser](https://www.mouser.com/new/hailo/hailo-hailo-8-ai-processor/)) and ModalAI's VOXL 2 weighs 16 g ([PX4](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/flight_controller/modalai_voxl_2.html)). A Jetson Orin Nano draws 7–15 W ([NVIDIA datasheet](https://static6.arrow.com/aropdfconversion/b4f120a8c52d5dd5e59875b129c4f94b77c7c3b6/jetson-orin-nano-datasheet-web.pdf)), above DARPA's 2–5 W budget. The deterministic Rust core is MinBand's most portable asset and should stay free of iOS dependencies. An AuterionOS app is the fast route to many airframes; the precedent is the Nemyx swarm app, for which Auterion claims more than 50,000 Skynode S units in service ([Auterion](https://auterion.com/product/nemyx/)).

Fusing tracks across drones needs three more things. Track IDs must be globally unique, built from platform, sortie epoch and local ID, instead of today's per-device `u32`. Platforms need a shared timebase: `server/src/clock.ts` only estimates each device's offset to the laptop's clock, yet at 15 m/s 200 ms of clock error is 3 m, and a fusion benchmark confirms that clock error degrades track association ([Drones 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/8/5/167/review_report)). Relays need store-and-forward with de-duplication.

## Conclusion

The hackathon plan and the fielding plan are the same plan at different depths. The things that win track points on Sunday are the things a Ukrainian unit will ask for in six months: a measured run on a real narrowband radio, a CoT feed into TAK, and positions with real coordinates and error bars. Nothing on the weekend list is a detour. The deeper lesson is that MinBand was designed for a cooperative room: two-way Wi-Fi, a printed origin, a ground station that acks constantly, and no adversary listening. The battlefield reverses each of those assumptions. The receiver must be able to stay silent, every position must sit on the earth with honest uncertainty, and the bearer is a telemetry radio rather than IP. MinBand's shared deterministic predictor and state-based repair already suit that world unusually well, because they need neither retransmission queues nor acknowledgements to stay coherent.

The main strategic risk is not a rival product but the framing. Buyers pay to harden video links, primes build their own entity models, and judges reward hardware. MinBand wins by being the narrow, OEM-neutral layer those systems lack: the feed that rides the mesh, fills the telemetry link, and lands in TAK, Delta or Lattice. It should be measured against video on the soldier's task, not on bytes alone.
